## Why This Matters For The Agent

The reference source for TMS click masking. It supplies the evidence behind `design/auditory-masking.md`: that noise built from your own click masks at lower and safer levels than generic noise, that sham under-estimates how much masking real TMS needs, and that the EEG rather than self-report is the reliable check.

## What They Did

Released TAAC, an open-source MATLAB GUI that records the stimulator- and coil-specific TMS click and generates a masking noise from it, customised per participant in both time and frequency.

Validation: `n = 20` healthy adults (age 24-60, 10 female), Nexstim NBS focal figure-of-eight biphasic coil (50/70 mm) at 70% MSO. Real TMS with the coil 2 cm posterior to vertex, AP current; sham with the coil at vertex tilted 90° touching the scalp. Jittered inter-pulse interval 2000-2300 ms. Volume raised until 10 consecutive pulses went undetected; condition and noise order randomised.

SPL measured properly: Brüel & Kjær 4128 head-and-torso simulator with the same JVC HA-FX8 in-ear earphones used on participants, Larson Davis 2900 analyser, A-weighted running SPL, in a low-reverberation lab (T30 < 0.3 s, LAeq < 37 dB).

Four noises compared: white noise (WN), noise adapted to the click as in Massimini et al. 2005 (AN), and two customised noises (CN1, CN2) generated by TAAC's broadband shuffling.

## Main Results

Median minimum SPL required to mask the click:

| | WN | AN | CN1 | CN2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sham TMS | 81.6 dB | 85.4 dB | **78.3** | **79.0** |
| Real TMS | 87.1 dB | 89.0 dB | **84.1** | **82.9** |

Customised noise required lower SPL in **100% of subjects**: median reduction 4.1 dB (sham) / 5.6 dB (real) versus WN, and 7.5 dB / 7.8 dB versus AN. In intensity terms, reductions of **61%, 82%, 72%, and 83%**.

ANOVA: noise type `F(3,57) = 73.67, p < 0.001`; condition `F(1,19) = 22.18, p < 0.001`; interaction `F(3,57) = 2.10, p = 0.11` (n.s.). All pairwise comparisons significant except CN1 vs CN2.

Exposure limits at the measured real-TMS levels — WN: NIOSH 5 h / OSHA 12 h; AN: 3 h / 9 h; CN1: 10 h / 13 h; CN2: 13 h / 21 h. Maximum SPLs exceeded equivalent SPLs by only ~1.2 dB IQR, around the just-noticeable difference, so the impulsive contribution is considered negligible.

**Real TMS needed more masking than sham** in every noise type — attributed to bone conduction, since during real stimulation the coil vibrates perpendicular to the scalp with a larger contact area.

Electrophysiological demonstration (`n = 1`, sham TMS, 120 pulses at 70% MSO): with noise off, a clear N100 at 96 ms and P200 at 196 ms; with CN1 on, both abolished, and the participant reported hearing no clicks.

## Arguments Worth Reusing

**Masking beats offline AEP removal.** ICA-based removal of the AEP assumes that cortical activation by TMS and by its click are independent, and that the EEG response is a linear superposition of a genuine TEP and an AEP with no interaction between them. Masking avoids any potential interplay rather than assuming it away.

**Absence of the N100/P200 complex is the most reliable criterion** for verifying masking efficacy — more trustworthy than asking the participant.

**Loudness alone is not the answer.** Simply increasing the volume of a generic noise has not always worked in previous TMS-EEG studies, and risks delivering unsafe levels. Customising the noise achieves masking at lower, safer SPL.

## Limitations

- Only broadband shuffling was explored; most of the parameter space is untested, including spatial shuffling.
- The electrophysiological proof is a single participant, in the sham condition.
- Psychophysical validation used one stimulator and coil at one intensity.
- Requires MATLAB with the Signal Processing and Communications System toolboxes.
- The authors propose but did not implement closed-loop tailoring driven by the presence or absence of the AEP.

## Agent Rules Extracted

- Recommend masking noise built from the user's own stimulator and coil recording, not generic white noise.
- Always state that masking must be titrated under real TMS, never sham — quote the ~6 dB gap.
- Give the EEG N100/P200 criterion as the verification method, with self-report as secondary.
- Raise hearing-safety exposure hours for long or repeated sessions.

## Claims To Avoid

Do not claim masking abolishes auditory input in every setup — the abolition demonstration is one participant under sham. Do not present spatial shuffling as validated. Do not use these SPL values as targets for a different stimulator, coil, or earphone combination; they are setup-specific.
