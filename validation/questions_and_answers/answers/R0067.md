# Online checks during a TMS-EEG session: go/no-go criteria

## Assumptions
- Single-pulse TMS-EEG (TEPs) over a cortical target (motor or non-motor), using a TMS-compatible amplifier (sampling ≥ 5 kHz, DC or very low high-pass, wide dynamic range) and 60–64+ channels.
- Neuronavigation is available, and you can show a running average of TMS-locked epochs online (an rt-TEP-style display, or the amplifier's averaging view with a baseline window of about −100 to −5 ms).
- The numbers below are practical heuristics, not universal standards. Calibrate them to your own system and record your lab's thresholds before data collection starts, ideally in a preregistration or SOP.

The basic rule: **do not "collect now, clean later."** Most TMS-EEG problems (decay artifacts, muscle artifacts, sensory contamination, weak cortical activation) can be fixed at the setup stage, and preprocessing cannot fix them afterwards. Decide using a short test block of about 20–30 pulses, and only then start the full recording.

---

## 1. Before the first pulse (resting EEG, no stimulation)

| Check | GO | NO-GO / fix first |
|---|---|---|
| Electrode impedance | All channels < 5 kΩ (ideally lower), especially those under and near the coil | Any channel near the coil > 5 kΩ, or many channels > 10 kΩ → re-prep |
| Raw signal quality | Clean, stationary EEG; visible alpha with eyes closed; no flat or railing channels | Flat, saturated or highly noisy channels; large slow drifts |
| Line noise | Small and similar across channels | Line noise much larger on some channels than others (bad contact or ground/reference) |
| Leads and cables | Leads run orthogonal to the coil handle where possible, are bundled, and do not form loops under the coil | Loops under the coil, or cables that will move with the coil or head |
| Participant state | Comfortable, relaxed jaw and neck, head supported, eyes open and fixating if that is the protocol | Tense neck or jaw muscles, drowsy, uncomfortable |
| Coil/target | Hotspot or target and orientation registered; neuronavigation registration error acceptable (for example ≤ 3–5 mm at fiducials) | Poor registration; unstable head tracker |
| Noise masking | Masking noise (for example a noise built from the coil-click spectrum) played via insert earphones at a level the participant tolerates; foam layer under the coil | No masking, or masking at a level that is uncomfortable |

---

## 2. Test block (about 20–30 pulses at the planned intensity): the main go/no-go decision

Look at **single trials** and the **running average** at the channels under and around the coil, plus the full butterfly plot and topography.

### 2a. TMS pulse artifact and amplifier recovery
- **GO:** The amplifier does not saturate, or recovers quickly. The signal returns to a plausible EEG range within about 5–10 ms after the pulse on most channels.
- **NO-GO:** Channels stay railed or saturated for tens of ms, or the recharge artifact falls inside the analysis window. Fix: check the amplifier range and filter settings, move the recharge delay out of the window, and check electrode contact.

### 2b. Decay artifacts (electrode/skin polarisation)
- **GO:** After the pulse, the signal on electrodes near the coil returns to baseline quickly, with no large slow exponential drift.
- **NO-GO:** A slow decay of large amplitude (tens to hundreds of µV) that lasts tens to hundreds of ms on one or more electrodes near the coil. Almost always this comes from poor contact or high impedance, or from an electrode pressed or moved by the coil. Fix: re-prep those electrodes and reduce coil pressure on them, then re-test.

### 2c. Cranial muscle artifact
- **GO:** No large, sharp, biphasic deflection in roughly the first 5–20 ms, or only a small one that clearly does not hide the early TEP.
- **NO-GO:** A large muscle artifact (often ≥ 50–100 µV, sometimes in the mV range) over temporal or frontal sites that hides the 0–30 ms window, especially for lateral or frontal targets. Fix, in this order: rotate the coil (often 15–45° changes the artifact a lot), move the target slightly (a few mm, staying within the planned target tolerance), or reduce intensity if the protocol allows it. If the target cannot be changed, document this and decide in advance whether early components will be analysed.

### 2d. Evidence of cortical activation (early TEP)
- **GO:** A clear early response (within about 50 ms) under or near the stimulated site, with a local and lateralised topography (not centred on the vertex). In the 20-trial average it should be well above the pre-stimulus baseline noise. A widely used heuristic from the real-time TEP literature is **an early peak-to-peak response on the order of ≥ 10 µV** at channels near the target. Use your own lab's calibrated threshold if you have one.
- **NO-GO:** The early response is flat, or not distinguishable from baseline noise, or is only artifact. Fix, in this order: check coil-scalp contact and coil tilt (the coil should be tangential to the scalp), adjust orientation, raise intensity within the limits of the protocol and safety, and re-check the target. Do **not** start the main block hoping that averaging over more trials will fix it.
- For motor-cortex targets, also check that MEPs (if recorded) are stable and fit the planned intensity relative to RMT. Large MEPs or twitches add reafferent somatosensory responses.

### 2e. Sensory (auditory and somatosensory) contamination
- **GO:** Ask the participant after the test block. They should report that the click is not audible, or only faintly audible, over the masking noise. Scalp sensation should be mild. In the average, no dominant N100–P200 complex at central/vertex sites that is larger than the early local response.
- **NO-GO:** The participant clearly hears the click, or the sensation is strong or unpleasant, **and/or** the average is dominated by a symmetric vertex N100/P200 (about 100–200 ms) rather than a local early response. This pattern suggests a sensory-evoked potential rather than a direct cortical response. Fix: raise the masking level (within comfort and safety limits), adjust the masking spectrum, add or increase foam under the coil, and check earphone fit. Then re-test.

### 2f. Ocular and startle responses
- **GO:** Few TMS-locked blinks.
- **NO-GO:** Blinks or eye movements locked to the pulse in most trials. These appear as frontal, polar deflections at roughly 50–300 ms. Fix: give fixation instructions, improve masking (blinks often reflect startle or audibility), and pause until habituation happens.

**Decision rule after the test block:** start the main recording only if 2a–2f all pass. If any item fails, fix it and run a new test block. Keep the test-block data, labelled, but do not merge it into the main dataset.

## 3. During the main recording (continuous monitoring)

Check after every block, or every 50–100 pulses, and watch continuously if possible.

| Item | Continue (GO) | Pause and fix (NO-GO) |
|---|---|---|
| Coil position (neuronavigation) | Within the predefined tolerance, for example ≤ 2–3 mm displacement and ≤ 5° angle/tilt from the target | Drift beyond tolerance → pause, reposition, and log the trials affected |
| Running TEP | Early response shape and topography stable compared with the test block | Early response shrinks or changes shape (coil moved, contact lost) or vertex N100/P200 grows (masking failing) |
| New decay or noise channels | None | New decay or noisy channels near the coil → re-prep during a break |
| Muscle or startle | Stable at the test-block level | Growing artifact or frequent blinks |
| Participant | Alert, comfortable, no adverse effects | Drowsiness (alpha/theta changes, slow eye movements), discomfort, headache, or any adverse event → pause; stop if safety is a concern |
| Trial yield | On track for the target number of clean trials (plan extra, for example ≥ 20–30% more than the number of usable trials you need) | Projected clean-trial count below target → extend the block or add a block |
| Stimulator | Output, pulse waveform, and ISI as planned; coil temperature within limits | Coil overheating, stimulator errors, missing triggers |
| Triggers/markers | Every pulse marked, with the correct condition code | Missing or duplicate markers → stop and fix before continuing |

---

## 4. Hard stop criteria (end the session or block regardless of data quality)
- Any safety concern: seizure signs, syncope, strong discomfort, the participant asks to stop.
- A failure of equipment that affects stimulation or recording integrity (for example a damaged coil or lost amplifier synchronisation).
- An unfixable artifact pattern that makes the primary outcome uninterpretable, decided **using criteria set before the session**, not after looking at the effect of interest.

---

## 5. Documentation for every session
- Final impedances; coil location, orientation and intensity, both before and after any adjustment.
- Test-block outcome for each criterion (pass/fail, with the fix applied).
- The participant's ratings of click audibility and scalp sensation (for example 0–10) after the test block and at the end.
- Any pauses, repositioning, or excluded blocks, with their timestamps.
- Keep screenshots or saved averages of the test-block TEP and of the final running average.

## Quick decision summary
1. Clean baseline EEG with low impedances → otherwise **no-go**.
2. Test block: no saturation or decay, a manageable muscle artifact, a clear local early TEP, low sensory contamination, few blinks → **go**.
3. Any failure → fix (re-prep, rotate or move the coil, adjust intensity, improve masking) → repeat the test block.
4. During recording: keep coil position, TEP stability, participant state, and trial yield within predefined limits; otherwise pause and fix.
