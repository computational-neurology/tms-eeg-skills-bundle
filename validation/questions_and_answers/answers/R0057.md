# One or two rounds of ICA in TMS-EEG preprocessing?

## Short answer

Both are defensible. What matters more is that each ICA round has a **clearly stated artifact target**, a defined place in the pipeline, and its own QC. Two rounds is not automatically better than one.

- **Two rounds** is the classical TESA-style design (Rogasch et al., 2017). ARTIST (Wu et al., 2018), TMSEEG (Atluri et al., 2016) and several recent studies use variants of it. Choose it if you are reproducing one of these pipelines, or if your data have large TMS-locked artifacts and you are not using a projection-based method for them.
- **One main round** is the usual choice when you clean TMS-evoked muscle with SOUND/SSP-SIR or with pulse-window interpolation. That single round targets ocular and non-stimulus-locked artifacts. A later residual round is optional, not required.

**Assumptions:** ~60-channel EEG, a standard TEP/GMFA analysis, enough trials for a stable decomposition (roughly 100 or more), and the TMS pulse already removed and interpolated before any ICA. If your montage, trial count or target is very different, the recommendation may change (see the decision guide).

---

## What each round is meant to remove (classical two-pass logic)

| | **ICA round 1** | **ICA round 2** |
|---|---|---|
| **When** | After pulse removal/interpolation (e.g. −2 to 12 ms, cubic), downsampling and bad-trial rejection, **before** band-pass/notch filtering | **After** band-pass/notch filtering |
| **Target** | Large, TMS-locked, non-neural artifacts: **TMS-evoked scalp/cranial muscle activity** (biphasic, peaks ~4–10 ms, can exceed 1 mV, decays over tens of ms) and **electrode/amplifier decay** (smooth exponential drift after the pulse). ARTIST and TMSEEG mainly aim this round at decay. | Smaller, stereotyped artifacts that are easier to separate once the large ones are gone: **blinks and eye movements**, **persistent/tonic muscle activity** (not stimulus-locked), cardiac activity, and residual electrode noise or bad-channel components |
| **Why here** | These artifacts are several orders of magnitude larger than the TEP and dominate the variance. Filtering across them causes ringing and smearing, so they are removed before any filter is applied. | Once the large transients are gone, the decomposition is no longer dominated by them, so ocular and background components separate more cleanly. |
| **Typical size** | Few components (the large, high-variance ones) | Usually more components, each judged by topography, time course and spectrum |

A typical TESA order:

channel rejection → epoching → baseline → pulse removal/interpolation → downsample (~1 kHz) → bad-epoch rejection → **ICA 1** → band-pass/notch → **ICA 2** → interpolate bad channels → average reference → final baseline.

Published pipelines differ in where they place baseline correction, downsampling and re-referencing. Before writing methods text, check the order in the exact protocol you cite.

---

## The main caveat: round 1 carries the most risk

Round 1 targets artifacts that are **time-locked to the pulse, just like the TEP itself**. That weakens ICA's independence assumption in both directions.

- **Overcleaning.** Components that carry TMS-evoked muscle or decay can also contain real early cortical activity (N15, P30, N45). Systematic comparisons show that pipelines using ICA tend to produce smaller TEP amplitudes at all peaks, early and late, not only near the artifact.
- **Undercleaning.** Muscle artifacts are often only partly separated. After a two-pass FastICA pipeline, TMS-evoked muscle was still visible in the 5–15 ms window in about 62% of participants after prefrontal stimulation, 35% after premotor and 0% after parietal stimulation (Biabani et al., 2024).
- **Method dependence.** Changing only the decay-suppression method (FastICA, SOUND or model-based detrending) changes early TEP amplitude **and topography**, even when late windows agree (Rogasch et al., 2022).

For this reason, many current workflows **do not use ordinary ICA rejection for TMS-evoked muscle or for clearly stimulus-locked components**. They handle these with:
- a documented interpolation window (e.g. −2 to 10–12 ms), analysing only later windows such as 20–60 ms, or
- SSP-SIR / projection-based muscle suppression, with the number of components checked.

This leaves a single main ICA round, focused on ocular and background artifacts.

---

## The single-round alternative (SOUND/SSP-SIR-style)

1. Remove/interpolate the pulse, run baseline/diagnostic steps, and reject bad trials and channels. Keep a reference copy of the full channel layout.
2. **ICA (main round):** blinks, eye movements, and non-stimulus-locked/background muscle only. Do not reject components with clear TMS-locked activity here.
3. SOUND for channel-level noise. This needs valid electrode positions.
4. SSP-SIR for the remaining early TMS-evoked muscle artifact. Inspect its components before choosing how many to project out.
5. *Optional* residual ICA, only for stereotyped non-stimulus-locked artifacts that are still visible, with before/after TEP QC.
6. Late low-pass/notch filtering, re-referencing, baseline, then TEP/GMFA.

---

## Decision guide

| Situation | Suggested approach |
|---|---|
| Midline/parietal target, little visible muscle artifact | One round for ocular/background artifacts. A first round for muscle is usually unnecessary. |
| Lateral/frontal target (e.g. DLPFC, premotor) with large muscle artifact | Either the TESA two-pass design with strict sensitivity checks, or (often safer for early windows) interpolation/SSP-SIR for the muscle plus one ICA round for ocular artifacts |
| Reproducing a named published protocol | Follow that protocol's number and placement of ICA rounds, and report any deviation |
| Few trials, low channel count, or unstable components across runs | One cautious round with appropriate PCA compression, or skip ICA for that artifact class and use trial rejection or projection instead |
| Need to compare early TEP topography or source estimates | Avoid aggressive round-1 rejection and add the muscle vs. no-muscle subgroup check (below) |

---

## What neither round should remove by default

**Auditory and somatosensory responses** (largely the N100/P180–P200 complex) are not ordinary ICA artifacts. They overlap with genuine cortical TEP activity. Reduce them at acquisition (noise masking, foam spacer) and handle what remains with sham/sensory control conditions, contrasts or justified subtraction, not with component rejection.

---

## Practical settings for either approach

- **Before any ICA:** remove/interpolate the pulse window. Mark bad channels and **exclude them from the decomposition**, and repair them after ICA. Do not interpolate channels before ICA, because that creates dependent channels and rank problems.
- **PCA compression:** with ~60 channels, ~25–30 retained components is common. Keep the number of components below the number of trials.
- **ICA-training data:** if you high-pass or detrend only to stabilise ICA, do it on a separate training copy. Apply the resulting rejections to the analysis data, so that early filters do not shape the final TEP.
- **Component decisions:** never reject on a label alone, even an automated one. Judge each component by topography, time course and spectrum together:
  - TMS-evoked muscle: focal frontal/temporal/jaw topography, high-frequency power, brief biphasic time course.
  - Decay: smooth exponential time course with consistent polarity across trials.
  - Blinks/saccades: frontal or bilateral-frontal topography with a typical slow time course.
- **Concatenating conditions** for a single decomposition is valid only if they share the same non-neural noise structure.

## QC checklist

- Before/after butterfly plots and GMFA for 0–100 ms after each ICA round, plus topographies at the TEP peaks (compare topographies, not just amplitudes).
- **Sensitivity check:** change the number of rejected components by ±2 and confirm the TEP is stable.
- Compare the cleaned data with minimally preprocessed data in windows that should be artifact-free.
- For frontal/lateral targets: split participants by whether muscle artifact is visible in the *uncleaned* 5–15 ms window, then compare their cleaned TEPs and spatial correlations. If the subgroups diverge, the cleaning is not doing what it claims.
- Check whether cleaning affected active and sham/control conditions differently.

## What to report

For each ICA round, report:
- its stated target and position in the pipeline
- the algorithm and PCA rank
- the number of components removed and the variance they explained
- example topographies, time courses and spectra of rejected components
- the rejection criteria
- the retained data rank, retained trials and channels

The number of rejected components alone is not a quality metric. The evidence that rejected components were non-neural is what readers need.

## Bottom line

Use **two rounds** if you follow the TESA/ARTIST/TMSEEG logic:
- **Round 1** (before filtering): the large TMS-locked muscle and decay artifacts.
- **Round 2** (after filtering): ocular, persistent muscle, cardiac and residual noise components.

Use **one round** (ocular/background only) if TMS-evoked muscle is handled by interpolation or SSP-SIR, which is often the safer choice when early TEP latencies or topographies matter.

In either case, keep the target of each round explicit, leave sensory-evoked components to the experimental design, and show with sensitivity checks that your TEPs do not depend on the component decisions. Even after careful ICA, TEP amplitudes reflect both cortical and non-cortical contributions and should not be read as a pure measure of cortical excitability.
