# Stimulation intensity for left-DLPFC TMS-EEG: is 110% RMT enough?

## Short answer

**It's a reasonable starting point, but not on its own as the intensity rule.** 110% RMT is fine as a safety reference and starting calibration. It is not a good final dosing rule for TMS-EEG over DLPFC. What works for an rTMS protocol does not carry over directly here, because rTMS and TMS-EEG optimise for different things:

- **RMT measures corticospinal excitability in M1.** It is related only indirectly to how strongly DLPFC is engaged. Differences between people in coil-to-cortex distance, gyral folding and coil orientation mean that a fixed %RMT gives different cortical E-fields at DLPFC in different participants. RMT-normalised intensity does not guarantee equivalent cortical activation at a non-motor target.
- **In TMS-EEG the outcome is the EEG response, and DLPFC is the most artifact-prone of the commonly studied sites.** In the largest sensory-confound dataset (Biabani et al., 2024, n = 135; prefrontal vs premotor vs parietal), prefrontal stimulation had:
  - residual muscle artifact surviving ICA in about 62% of individuals;
  - the loudest perceived click, even though it used the lowest intensity;
  - the highest twitch and discomfort ratings;
  - the least effective white-noise masking;
  - the largest N100/P200, which partly tracks those sensory ratings.

  A fixed 110% RMT may therefore be too high for some participants: a large early muscle-artifact peak and sensory-dominated late TEPs. For others it may be too low: no visible early local response. You won't know which unless you look online.
- **A felt twitch is not a usable ceiling.** At DLPFC the early EEG muscle-artifact peak can be large even when the participant reports no twitch. This peak is a bipolar, MEP-like deflection after the pulse artifact (at more than about 4 ms), focal over frontal, temporal and jaw channels. A participant who is "comfortable" and not twitching can still give you an early window you can't interpret.

**Recommendation:** keep 110% RMT as the planned starting intensity. Then add an individual online-adjustment step with a pre-specified search range, ceiling and stopping rule, and lock the final value before the main blocks. Report the result as a decision chain: RMT, starting intensity, online check, final locked intensity.

## Suggested intensity procedure (pre-registerable)

1. **Determine RMT.** Use left M1, a first dorsal interosseous (FDI) hotspot, and the same coil and pulse waveform as the main experiment. Use a standard criterion, e.g. the lowest intensity giving ≥50 µV peak-to-peak MEPs in 5 of 10 trials (or an adaptive threshold-hunting procedure). Report it in %MSO.
2. **Set the starting intensity** at 110% RMT, rounded to the nearest whole %MSO. Check it against your safety maximum.
3. **Run an online TEP check at the DLPFC target.** Use the final coil position and orientation, masking noise and foam in place, and 10–30 trials. Inspect single trials and the short average for:
   - the early local response near the coil in about 8–50 ms. Roughly 8–10 µV peak-to-peak is a practical acquisition target, not a universal threshold;
   - the early muscle-artifact peak;
   - pulse and decay artifacts;
   - whether late N100/P200 dominates while the early response stays weak, which points to sensory contamination.
4. **Adjust in a fixed order:**
   - First, coil orientation and position. Small rotations or shifts of a few mm often shrink the muscle peak and improve the early response without raising intensity. DLPFC orientation should be individual, not a fixed 45°.
   - Second, intensity, in small steps within a pre-specified range, e.g. 90–120% RMT. Raise intensity only while the early muscle-artifact peak stays small enough for the planned early window to remain interpretable. Lower it if that peak, discomfort or masking failure takes over.
5. **Retune auditory masking** every time intensity or coil placement changes.
6. **Lock** the intensity before the main blocks and do not change it afterwards. Log the starting value, every change, the reason for it, and the final value.

If your study needs exactly the same %RMT for everyone (e.g. to match the rTMS protocol or for a between-group comparison), keep 110% RMT fixed. In that case, still run the online check and make it a go/no-go step rather than an adjustment step. Pre-specify what happens when the data are unusable: repositioning or rotating the coil, a backup DLPFC coordinate, or excluding the participant. State in the paper that fixed %RMT does not equalise cortical dose at DLPFC.

**Assumptions** (the question doesn't specify these): single-pulse TMS-EEG at rest; a figure-of-eight coil with biphasic pulses; neuronavigated targeting from individual MRI or a fixed coordinate; an endpoint that includes early and/or late TEP components. If you compare groups or conditions, also collect separate 0–10 ratings of click loudness, scalp twitch, discomfort and pain after each block. At this site, N100/P200 differences are expected from sensation alone.

## Methods text

**Version A: recommended (RMT start with online adjustment)**

> Resting motor threshold (RMT) was determined over the left primary motor cortex hotspot of the right first dorsal interosseous muscle as the lowest stimulator output eliciting motor-evoked potentials ≥50 µV peak-to-peak in at least 5 of 10 consecutive trials, using the same coil and pulse waveform as in the main experiment. For left DLPFC stimulation, intensity was initially set to 110% of RMT and treated as a starting reference rather than a guarantee of equivalent cortical engagement. Before the main recording, a short online TEP check (10–30 pulses, with masking noise applied) was used to inspect the early local response and artifact profile. Coil orientation and position were adjusted first, and intensity was then adjusted within a pre-specified range of [90–120]% RMT, so that a clear early local response was present while the early craniofacial muscle-artifact peak remained small enough to keep the [e.g., 15–50] ms window interpretable. The final intensity was then fixed for the remainder of the session. Stimulation intensity is reported per participant both as %MSO and as %RMT (group mean ± SD: [x ± y] %MSO; [x ± y] %RMT), together with the number of participants whose final intensity deviated from 110% RMT.

**Version B: if you keep a fixed 110% RMT for every participant**

> Resting motor threshold (RMT) was determined over the left primary motor cortex hotspot of the right first dorsal interosseous muscle as the lowest stimulator output eliciting motor-evoked potentials ≥50 µV peak-to-peak in at least 5 of 10 consecutive trials, using the same coil and pulse waveform as in the main experiment. Left DLPFC was stimulated at 110% of each participant's RMT (mean ± SD: [x ± y] %MSO). Because RMT indexes corticospinal rather than prefrontal excitability, this intensity was not assumed to produce equivalent cortical engagement across participants. Before the main recording, a short online TEP check (10–30 pulses) was used to confirm an interpretable early response without a dominant craniofacial muscle-artifact peak; when this criterion was not met, coil position and orientation were adjusted at constant intensity. [n] participants were [repositioned/excluded] on this basis.

For either version, also report the inter-trial interval, the total number of pulses delivered, and intensity as both %MSO and %RMT, each as an explicit number with central tendency and dispersion. These items are often under-reported.
