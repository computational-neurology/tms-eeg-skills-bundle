# Choosing Stimulation Intensity for DLPFC TMS-EEG: A Brief Guide

## Assumptions
- Single-pulse (or paired-pulse) TMS-EEG over DLPFC in healthy adults, figure-of-eight coil, neuronavigation available, with the early local TEP and/or later components as your endpoints.
- You can view TEPs online during setup, either with a real-time TEP display or with quick 10-30 trial averages.

## The key principle
For DLPFC, % resting motor threshold (RMT) is **a safety reference and a starting point. It is not your dose rule.** RMT measures corticospinal excitability in M1. It does not tell you how strongly DLPFC is engaged, and it says nothing about the artifact burden at a frontal site. The two can diverge a lot. Treat intensity as one variable in a coupled setup problem, together with target, coil orientation/position and masking, and set it from what the EEG shows online.

## Step-by-step

**1. Get your reference values**
- Measure RMT over M1 with the same coil and current direction. Use it for safety limits and as the starting estimate.
- If you have an individual MRI, model the E-field (for example SimNIBS, or an E-field-navigated system) at the DLPFC target. Set coil orientation from individual anatomy, with induced current roughly perpendicular to the target gyral crown or sulcal wall, rather than a fixed 45 degrees. Note the estimated cortical E-field at your starting intensity. Correcting for coil-to-cortex distance alone does **not** equalise cortical dose.

**2. Pick a starting intensity**
- Start in the commonly used range around 90-120% RMT, or at a modelled target E-field. Stay well inside your safety maximum and participant tolerance.

**3. Titrate online on the TEP, not the MEP**
- Collect short blocks of 10-30 trials and inspect single trials before trusting the average.
- **What you want:** a clear early local response near the coil. A practical acquisition target is about 8-10 µV peak-to-peak in the roughly 8-50 ms window over channels near the target. This is a rule of thumb, not a universal threshold.
- **Before raising intensity:** try small coil rotations and position shifts first. These often improve the early response with no extra intensity and less muscle artifact.
- **Raise intensity in small steps** only while the early response improves and artifacts stay manageable.

**4. Respect the ceiling, which is usually muscle artifact**
- At DLPFC the limiting factor is usually the **early muscle-artifact peak**, not safety. It is a bipolar, MEP-like deflection beginning after about 4 ms, strongest over frontal, temporal and jaw/facial channels. It grows with intensity and is often the only thing visible in the online average.
- This peak is **not** the participant's felt twitch. "No twitch, comfortable" does not mean the early window is clean.
- Stop increasing intensity once this peak threatens to make your planned early analysis window uninterpretable. Expect residual muscle artifact in a large share of prefrontal participants (in one large study, about two-thirds after ICA). Plan to reduce it at acquisition rather than counting on cleaning to remove it later.

**5. Watch for sensory contamination**
- If raising intensity mainly enlarges the late N100/P200 and not the early local response, suspect auditory or somatosensory input rather than more cortical engagement.
- Prefrontal sites can produce the loudest perceived click even at lower intensity. **Retune auditory masking (and check foam/spacer) every time intensity changes.** Collect click-loudness, scalp-sensation and discomfort ratings at the final intensity.

**6. Lock and document**
- Before the main blocks, define go/no-go: an interpretable early response, manageable muscle/decay artifact, effective masking, and acceptable tolerance. Then lock the intensity and do not keep tuning it during the main blocks.
- Report the decision chain:
  - RMT and starting %RMT and/or E-field estimate
  - online TEP response at each step
  - the artifact/discomfort ceiling you hit
  - the final value as %MSO, %RMT and, if modelled, cortical E-field (V/m)

## Design cautions
- **Per-subject tuned or fixed?** Online-tuned intensity gives better per-subject signal, but intensity then varies across participants. If you compare groups, sessions or sites, choose one approach in advance, record intensity as a covariate, and keep it fixed across conditions within a participant.
- **Comparing sites or groups:** % RMT (even distance-corrected) does not mean matched cortical stimulation. Either model the E-field per individual and normalise by it, or state unmatched dose as a limitation.
- **Sham/control:** match its sensory profile (click loudness, scalp sensation) to the real condition at the locked intensity.
