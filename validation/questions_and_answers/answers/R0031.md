# Intensity for left-DLPFC TMS-EEG: is a fixed 110% RMT appropriate?

## Short answer

**Not as the only rule.** 110% RMT is a reasonable *starting point* and safety reference, but it is not enough on its own to set dose for a TMS-EEG study over DLPFC. I would not write a methods sentence saying intensity was simply "110% RMT for every participant" without the extra steps below.

Matching your rTMS protocol makes sense for the treatment arm. For the TEP readout, though, the aim is different. You need a dose that gives an interpretable early cortical response and keeps artifacts manageable. A fixed %RMT does not guarantee that at a non-motor site.

## Why a fixed %RMT is not sufficient at DLPFC

1. **RMT measures something else.** RMT reflects corticospinal excitability of the hand area of M1. It is shaped by scalp-to-cortex distance, gyral folding and coil orientation *at M1*. At DLPFC the same %RMT can produce quite different cortical E-fields from one person to the next, so "110% RMT" does not mean equal cortical dose across participants.
2. **Distance correction does not fix this.** Even after the usual coil-to-cortex distance adjustment, modelled cortical E-fields still differed significantly between prefrontal, premotor and parietal sites (Biabani et al., 2024). Do not describe %RMT, with or without distance correction, as equalised stimulation.
3. **DLPFC is the most artifact-prone of the common non-motor sites.** In a large three-site comparison (n = 135), prefrontal stimulation gave:
   - residual muscle artifact after ICA in about 62% of participants;
   - the loudest perceived click, even though it used the lowest intensity;
   - the highest twitch, discomfort and pain ratings;
   - the least effective white-noise masking;
   - the largest N100/P200.

   At a fixed 110% RMT, some participants will have early windows dominated by cranial muscle artifact. Others may show a weak early local response with a large sensory-driven N100/P200.
4. **The ceiling at DLPFC is set by artifacts, not by twitches.** The relevant limit is the early bipolar, MEP-like muscle peak in the EEG, about 4 ms or later after the pulse, over frontal, temporal and jaw channels. It grows with intensity and can be large even when the participant feels no twitch. "The participant was comfortable" does not mean the early window is clean.

## Recommended intensity procedure

Keep 110% RMT as the anchor, then add an online check with a stopping rule:

1. **Measure RMT** with the same coil and current direction you plan to use, and record it in %MSO. You can also run an individual E-field model (MRI-based head model, e.g. SimNIBS) to describe the cortical dose at the DLPFC target.
2. **Start at 110% RMT** at the neuronavigated left-DLPFC target. Set coil orientation individually from anatomy or the E-field. The 45° convention is an M1 rule.
3. **Check a short online average** (about 10–30 trials), inspecting single trials too. Look at:
   - the early local TEP (about 8–50 ms) near the coil. A practical working target is roughly 8–10 µV peak-to-peak, not a hard threshold;
   - the size of the early muscle peak;
   - pulse, decay and recharge artifacts;
   - whether a late N100/P200 is dominating.
4. **Adjust in this order:** small coil rotations or position shifts first, which often shrink the muscle peak without changing dose. Change intensity second, within safety limits. Retune auditory masking every time intensity changes.
5. **Go/no-go:** go when the early window is interpretable for your endpoint and artifacts are manageable. If the early response stays weak while N100/P200 grows with intensity, suspect sensory contamination rather than stronger cortical activation. If anterior or lateral DLPFC stays heavily contaminated, consider a pre-specified backup target that is more medial or posterior.
6. **Lock and log** the final intensity, in %MSO and %RMT, before the main blocks. Keep it fixed for the rest of the session.

**If you must keep 110% RMT fixed for every participant** (for example, to mirror the rTMS dose exactly), say so plainly and treat online QC as a go/no-go check only. Adjust orientation and position, but not intensity. Also:
- report each participant's %MSO and early-window artifact status;
- collect click-loudness, scalp-sensation and discomfort ratings;
- pre-register a check comparing participants with and without residual muscle artifact for any early-TEP analysis;
- state in the limitations that cortical dose was not matched.

## Methods sentences

**Option A: recommended (110% RMT start, adjusted online)**

> Resting motor threshold (RMT) was determined over the left primary motor cortex hand area as the lowest intensity eliciting MEPs ≥50 µV in at least 5 of 10 consecutive trials in the relaxed first dorsal interosseous, using the same coil and current direction as for DLPFC stimulation. For the left DLPFC, stimulation started at 110% RMT. Because RMT indexes corticospinal excitability and does not guarantee equivalent cortical activation at non-motor targets, it served as a starting and safety reference. Before the main recording, intensity, together with coil orientation and position, was adjusted under neuronavigation using online TEP averages of 10–30 trials. The aim was a visible early local response (8–50 ms) at electrodes near the coil, with an early craniofacial muscle artifact small enough that the planned early analysis window remained interpretable, within safety and tolerability limits. Auditory masking was readjusted after every intensity change. The final intensity was then kept fixed for the session and is reported per participant as %MSO and %RMT (mean ± SD, range).

**Option B: fixed 110% RMT kept on purpose**

> Stimulation intensity over the left DLPFC was fixed at 110% of each participant's RMT (determined as above with the same coil and current direction), to match the dose used in the rTMS protocol. Because RMT does not equate cortical dose at non-motor targets, online TEP averages of 10–30 trials were inspected before the main recording. Coil orientation and position, but not intensity, were adjusted to minimise early craniofacial muscle artifact. Per-participant intensity (%MSO and %RMT, mean ± SD, range), the presence of residual early muscle artifact, and sensory ratings (perceived click loudness, scalp sensation and discomfort) are reported. Between-participant differences in cortical dose are acknowledged as a limitation.

## Assumptions and what to fill in

- I assumed single-pulse TMS-EEG with a figure-of-eight coil, neuronavigation, and RMT taken from the FDI hand area. Replace the RMT definition if you use a different muscle, MEP criterion or estimation method (e.g. an adaptive threshold-hunting algorithm).
- 110% RMT for single pulses at about 1 Hz or slower is within common safety practice. Confirm separately against current safety guidelines for any repetitive component.
- Whichever option you pick, report:
  - intensity as both %MSO and %RMT, with mean, SD and range;
  - stimulator, coil model and current direction;
  - how the target and orientation were defined;
  - inter-trial interval and total pulse count;
  - the online QC criteria;
  - the masking and sensory-rating procedure.
