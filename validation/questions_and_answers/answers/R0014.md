# ICA for F3 TMS-EEG with large early decay and saturating channels

## Short answer

I'll give you concrete ICA settings below, but please don't record all 30 participants as they are. ICA can reduce a moderate, stable decay artifact. It can't recover saturated channels, and it handles a 20 ms high-amplitude decay poorly. That decay is often non-stationary and partly non-linear, and it is time-locked to the same pulse as your early TEPs. If the data go into ICA in this state, you are likely to:

- lose information permanently wherever the amplifier clipped. Once a channel is at the rail, its signal is gone, and no decomposition can bring it back.
- break ICA's assumptions. ICA assumes linear, stationary mixing of independent sources. Clipping is non-linear. A decay whose size and shape change from trial to trial (with electrode movement, small coil shifts or drying gel) gets spread across many components.
- remove brain signal along with the artifact. TMS-locked artifacts and early TEPs share timing and topography, so they are not truly independent. Rejecting the artifact components will also take out real early TEP activity (roughly 0–40 ms), which is the window F3 studies usually care about.
- end up with results that depend on the pipeline. Different cleaning pipelines produce different early TEPs from the same data when artifacts are this large. A reviewer can fairly ask whether your effects come from the cleaning.

Assumptions: a 60–64 channel TMS-compatible amplifier, single-pulse TMS at F3 at roughly 90–120% RMT, and about 100–200 trials per condition.

## 1. Spend one to two pilot sessions fixing acquisition (highest return)

Tuning the hardware usually takes about 30–60 minutes per session at first, then becomes routine. That is much less than the cost of 30 compromised datasets.

| Problem | What to check or change |
|---|---|
| Saturation | Use the amplifier's widest input range or a DC/high-range mode. Sample at ≥5 kHz, ideally 10–20 kHz, so the pulse and its recovery are well resolved. Confirm the hardware anti-aliasing and low-pass settings are suitable for TMS. |
| Decay (electrode polarization or movement) | Get impedances below 5 kΩ, especially under and around the coil, and recheck them during the session. Use enough gel without bridging. Make sure the electrode pins are firmly seated and the cap is snug. |
| Lead and wire pickup | Route leads away from the coil and perpendicular to the coil handle or current direction. Bundle the cables and stop them from moving. Avoid loops under the coil. |
| Coil pressure | Use a thin spacer between coil and cap, or reduce pressure, so the coil isn't pushing on the electrodes. Use a coil holder or robot and neuronavigation to keep the coil stable. |
| Muscle (F3 is near frontalis and temporalis) | Small coil rotations (about ±15–45°) and small target shifts can greatly reduce cranial muscle activation. Use the lowest intensity that gives a clear TEP. |
| Monitoring | Watch the averaged response online (rt-TEP style) after about 20–40 trials. Aim for an early artifact of no more than tens of µV that is gone by about 10 ms, no saturated channels, and a visible TEP in the single participant. Adjust before collecting the main blocks. |

Also use noise masking and a foam layer to control auditory and somatosensory evoked potentials. ICA can't separate these well either.

If a channel still saturates after all this, write down which channel it is and why. Decide in advance to drop and interpolate it rather than "fixing" it with ICA.

## 2. ICA settings for the residual artifacts

These settings follow the common two-round FastICA approach (TESA-style). They work best once the artifacts have been reduced at acquisition.

### Before ICA

1. Epoch around each pulse, for example −1000 to +1000 ms. Use long epochs so ICA has enough data.
2. Cut out the pulse, about −2 to +10 ms. If the residual decay is still large after the hardware fixes, extend this, but report any window over about 15 ms as a limitation for early components. Fill the gap with constant or zeroed values for ICA, and interpolate it (cubic) only after ICA. Don't filter across the gap.
3. Remove clipped or bad channels and trials before ICA. Drop any channel that saturates in more than a small share of trials, and reject trials where any retained channel clips. Interpolate those channels only after ICA.
4. Downsample to about 1000 Hz after the pulse is removed, using an anti-aliased resampler.
5. Rank: reduce dimensionality with PCA to the true data rank. Subtract one rank for average reference and one for each interpolated or removed channel. Otherwise ICA produces unstable duplicate components.
6. Data amount: aim for at least about 20–30 × (number of channels)² samples. With 60 channels, about 150 trials of 2 s at 1 kHz just reaches this. Reduce rank further if you have fewer trials.

### Round 1: TMS-evoked decay and muscle artifacts (before temporal filtering)

- Algorithm: FastICA, symmetric approach, tanh nonlinearity. Fix the random seed and check stability, for example by rerunning or using an ICASSO-style check on a few participants.
- Input: unfiltered or demeaned epochs, so large, fast artifacts aren't smeared by filters.
- Remove only components with a clear, artifact-only signature:
  - Muscle or decay components with very large amplitude in about 11–30 ms and a focal topography under the coil. Example: TESA-style thresholds, where mean absolute z-scored time course in the early window exceeds about 8.
  - Components whose time course is a monotonic exponential decay at the stimulated electrodes.
- Be conservative. A common target is removing only enough components to bring early artifact power down to near the post-artifact level. Don't remove everything with large early variance.

### Then

- Interpolate the pulse gap (cubic, using the surrounding few ms).
- Band-pass about 1–80 or 1–100 Hz and notch at 50/60 Hz. Use zero-phase filters applied to the interpolated data. A 0.1–1 Hz high-pass is a trade-off: about 1 Hz helps ICA, but late components may need a lower cutoff. One option is to fit round 2 on a 1 Hz high-passed copy and apply the weights to the 0.1 Hz data.
- Re-reject bad trials.

### Round 2: everything else

- Use FastICA again with the same settings.
- Remove blink and horizontal eye movement, persistent muscle (high-frequency, spectral slope), electrode noise or single-channel components, and ECG. Use visual inspection supported by ICLabel or TESA classification.
- Don't remove components just because they are time-locked to TMS around 100–200 ms. That is where auditory and somatosensory potentials overlap with real TEPs. Handle those through the experimental design (masking, sham or control conditions) rather than ICA.

### Afterwards

- Interpolate the removed channels (spherical spline) and re-reference to average.
- Optional alternatives or complements for the early window: SOUND (channel noise) followed by SSP-SIR (muscle). Running both pipelines on a subset is a good robustness check.

## 3. Reporting and safeguards

- Pre-register the exact pipeline and component-rejection criteria. Report the number of components removed per participant and per round.
- Show TEPs and the global mean field power (GMFP) before and after each ICA round. Also show the variance removed by each round.
- Give a sensitivity analysis: compare main effects with and without round-1 cleaning, or with an alternative pipeline. If effects in the first 30–40 ms appear only after aggressive ICA, treat them as unreliable.
- If you still have to record the remaining participants before the hardware is tuned, record them at least with the widest amplifier range, high sampling rate, good impedances and careful lead routing. Plan to report the early window (<25–30 ms) as uninterpretable, and analyse only later components.

## Bottom line

Fix saturation and the decay at acquisition first; it costs about one pilot session. Use two-round FastICA (symmetric, tanh, rank-matched PCA, pulse removed and not filtered across, conservative component rejection) only for what is left. Recording everyone now and relying on ICA afterwards will likely lose the early TEPs you are probably most interested in, and the decay will still contaminate later components.
