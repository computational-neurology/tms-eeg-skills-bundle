# Is PCIst = 0.25 "below the 0.31 threshold"?

**Short answer: No, not as stated.** The 0.31 cutoff can't be applied to a PCIst value. It is the empirical cutoff (PCI\*) for the original Lempel–Ziv-based PCI (PCI_LZ / PCImax). PCIst is a different index on a different scale. Comparing 0.25 against 0.31 is therefore a category error, and you cannot conclude from it that the patient is "below threshold".

## Why the 0.31 threshold doesn't carry over

1. **Where the 0.31 comes from.** PCI\* = 0.31 was derived by Casarotto et al. (2016, *Annals of Neurology*). They used a benchmark population of conditions where consciousness was known: awake subjects and dreaming-REM subjects versus NREM-sleep subjects and subjects under midazolam, propofol or xenon anesthesia. The metric was the original PCI (Casali et al., 2013). That PCI binarizes source-reconstructed TMS-evoked activity against a bootstrap statistical threshold, compresses the resulting spatiotemporal matrix with Lempel–Ziv complexity, and normalizes by source entropy. The result is roughly bounded between 0 and 1. The cutoff only applies to that pipeline, including source modeling, the statistical binarization, and the normalization.

2. **PCIst is computed differently.** PCI based on state transitions (Comolatti et al., 2019, *Brain Stimulation*) works in sensor space. It reduces the post-stimulus response with SVD/PCA, keeps the components with enough signal-to-noise ratio, counts the response-related change in the number of state transitions for each component, and sums those counts across components. Its value depends on the number of components kept and on parameters such as the analysis windows, the SNR threshold, and the state-transition threshold grid. It is **not normalized to a 0–1 range**. In the validation datasets, typical PCIst values for conscious conditions are well above 1, in units of summed state-transition differences. PCIst correlates strongly with PCI_LZ across conditions, but a strong correlation does not mean the numbers are the same. You cannot read a PCIst value against a PCI_LZ cutoff.

3. **A PCIst of 0.25 is itself a warning sign.** If 0.25 really is the raw output of a standard PCIst implementation, that value is unusually low, even for unconscious conditions. Possible explanations:
   - The response was weak or failed to reach significance. Very few components passed the SNR criterion, so the "complexity" is driven by noise. Check that the TEP is real and large enough before stimulation stops, for example with real-time readout of the early response amplitude at the target.
   - Some rescaling or normalization was applied that is not part of the published method.
   - Parameters differ from the published defaults, for example the baseline/response windows, SNR (`min_snr`), `max_var`, or the k-value grid.
   - Some other index was actually computed, or the values are in an unusual unit.

   Check this before interpreting the number at all.

## What you can and can't conclude

- **Can't conclude:** "The patient is below the consciousness threshold." No validated mapping exists that sends PCIst = 0.25 onto the PCI\* = 0.31 scale.
- **Can conclude, cautiously:** if the pipeline is correct, you can compare the value against PCIst distributions from known-conscious and known-unconscious recordings processed identically, ideally in your own lab with the same montage and settings.

## Recommended steps

1. **Decide which index you need for a clinical-style threshold.**
   - To use PCI\* = 0.31, compute **PCI_LZ** (original Casali pipeline, source-space, bootstrap binarization) on the same data and compare that value to 0.31.
   - To stay with PCIst, use a **PCIst-specific cutoff** derived from a benchmark population. Either use a published PCIst benchmark that matches your acquisition and parameter settings exactly, or derive one yourself: the maximum PCIst observed in unresponsive non-dreaming conditions sets the cutoff, as Casarotto did for PCI\*. I'm not quoting a specific PCIst cutoff number here because it depends on implementation parameters. Take it from a matched benchmark rather than carrying over 0.31.
2. **Check the montage assumption.** You have 60 channels, which matches the high-density montages used in the original benchmark. That's helpful, but it does not justify comparing indices across the two scales. Very sparse montages do change PCI values, so montage matching still matters when you use a published cutoff.
3. **Make sure the response is valid.** The usual safeguards apply:
   - adequate early TEP amplitude at the target, at least a few µV in the first ~50 ms
   - no residual muscle, decay or ocular artifacts
   - effective auditory masking and minimized somatosensory input, so you aren't measuring sensory-evoked potentials
   - enough artifact-free trials (often ≥100–150)
   - consistent coil placement, ideally with neuronavigation

   A low value from a poor TEP is uninterpretable, not "low complexity".
4. **Use the maximum across targets.** Clinical use takes the maximum PCI over several cortical targets (PCImax). One low value from a single site doesn't establish low capacity for consciousness.
5. **Consider the sedative.** Under sedation, low complexity is expected with propofol, midazolam and xenon. It is *not* reliably expected with ketamine, which preserves high PCI with dream-like experiences. Depth of sedation, drug and timing all matter. In a sedated patient a low value mainly reflects the pharmacological state, not underlying brain capacity. If you are trying to assess capacity for consciousness rather than confirm sedation, repeat the measurement off sedation.

## Assumptions

- I assume "PCIst" means the state-transition-based index of Comolatti et al. (2019), computed with its standard implementation.
- I assume the 0.31 you quote is the Casarotto et al. (2016) PCI\* for PCI_LZ.
- If you actually computed PCI_LZ and only labeled it PCIst, then 0.25 < 0.31 would be the right comparison. Even then, it would be one data point, valid only if the TEP is high-quality, the stimulation site is appropriate, and the sedative is not ketamine. Ideally it would be the maximum across several sites.
