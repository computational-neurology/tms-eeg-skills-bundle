# Conclusion sentence for the post-20 Hz rTMS N45 change

## Short answer

I don't recommend a sentence that says "cortical excitability increased". A 30% larger N45 after 20 Hz rTMS doesn't support that claim on its own, for two reasons:

1. **TEP amplitude is not a pure measure of cortical excitability.** A TEP peak is a mix of several things: local cortical activity, spread to connected areas, how strongly the coil engaged the target, sensory-evoked input, residual artifact and preprocessing choices. A change in one peak shows that the response changed. It does not show that excitability changed.
2. **The N45 points the other way.** Pharmacological TMS-EEG work links the N45 mainly to GABA-A receptor-mediated inhibition: drugs that enhance GABA-A signalling make the N45 larger. A larger N45 could therefore mean *more* inhibition, which is the opposite of what the requested sentence says. Many systems shape the N45, so neither reading is certain. That is exactly why neither should be stated as a firm conclusion.

## Recommended conclusion sentence (default)

> "Following 20 Hz rTMS, the amplitude of the N45 component of the TMS-evoked potential over M1 increased by approximately 30% relative to baseline, indicating that high-frequency rTMS modulated early cortical responses to single-pulse TMS; given the proposed contribution of GABA-A-mediated inhibition to the N45, this change may reflect altered cortical inhibitory–excitatory balance rather than a simple increase in excitability."

## Alternatives, depending on what your design includes

- **If you had a sham or control rTMS session, with an active-vs-sham interaction on N45 (e.g., session × time):**
  > "20 Hz rTMS, but not sham rTMS, increased N45 amplitude over M1 (≈30% from baseline; [statistic, p, effect size, CI]), suggesting a protocol-specific modulation of early, putatively GABA-A-related cortical responses."

- **If you also recorded MEPs and they increased:**
  > "The increase in N45 amplitude was accompanied by an increase in MEP amplitude, consistent with a change in corticospinal excitability; the N45 change itself is best interpreted as a modulation of early cortical reactivity."

  Only use excitability language for the MEP result.

- **If there was no control condition (pre/post active session only):**
  > "N45 amplitude over M1 was ≈30% larger after 20 Hz rTMS than at baseline; in the absence of a sham condition, this change cannot be attributed specifically to rTMS and may partly reflect time, session, or state effects."

## Checks to run before choosing a sentence

**Statistics and reliability**
- Is the 30% a group mean with a test, a confidence interval and an effect size, or one participant's change? Report how many participants showed the effect.
- Is 30% bigger than the normal test-retest variability of the N45 in your setup? If you have no reliability data, say so.
- Were the N45 window, ROI channels and reference fixed before you looked at the data? A window or ROI chosen after inspection inflates the effect.

**Same stimulation before and after**
- Were the coil position, orientation and intensity identical (neuronavigation logs)? Did you keep the same absolute intensity, or re-threshold after rTMS? A small shift in coil position can change early TEP amplitude by this much.
- How many trials survived rejection in each block? Was the signal-to-noise ratio comparable?

**Sensory and artifact confounds**
- The N45 is mostly cortical, since sensory spatial overlap with the TEP mainly starts around 60 ms. However, it can still carry some sensory contribution, and realistic shams have produced a measurable N45. Were masking and ear protection identical pre and post? Did you collect separate post-rTMS ratings for click loudness, scalp twitch, discomfort and pain? 20 Hz rTMS can change sensation or arousal.
- Check for residual scalp-muscle or decay artifact in the N45 window, pre and post separately. "Early" does not mean "clean".

**Preprocessing**
- Were both sessions processed with exactly the same pipeline (same filters, ICA criteria, SOUND/SSP-SIR settings)? Did you remove a similar number of components with similar variance?
- Show topographies and rejected-component time courses for both sessions. ICA and strong regularisation change TEP amplitudes, and they may not do so equally across sessions.

**Specificity**
- Did other peaks (N15, P30, P60, N100) change as well? A change across all peaks suggests a global gain, SNR or sensory effect rather than a specific N45 mechanism.
- Is the effect local (ROI or LMFP) or global (GMFA)? GMFA shows overall scalp dispersion, not target-specific excitability.

## Assumptions

- The N45 was measured over ipsilateral M1 channels from single-pulse TMS-EEG, before and after one session of 20 Hz rTMS to M1, using the same pipeline throughout.
- The 30% is a group-level average change. There is no information on sham control, MEPs, sensory ratings or statistical testing, so the default sentence above is deliberately cautious. If you can confirm a sham-controlled, statistically supported effect with matched sensory ratings, use the first alternative sentence.
