# Auditory masking for TMS-EEG: setup and verification

## Assumptions

You didn't give details of your setup, so this answer assumes:

- a single-pulse TEP study with a figure-of-eight coil and a fixed target and intensity for each block;
- participants who can report what they hear (there is a separate note below for those who cannot);
- in-ear earphones are available;
- you can see an online average of the EEG during setup.

If your target is prefrontal or lateral frontal, read the "Site dependence" section closely. That is where masking most often fails.

---

## 1. Why mask at all (and not just clean the data afterwards)

Every TMS pulse makes a loud click. That click produces an auditory evoked potential, mainly a frontocentral **N100/P200**, which overlaps the late TEP. You could try to remove it offline with ICA, but that assumes the cortical response to TMS and the response to its click are independent and simply add up with no interaction. Masking avoids that assumption altogether. Preprocessing can't rescue a dataset where the late window is dominated by auditory input.

---

## 2. Build the masker from your own click

Plain white noise is a weak masker for your particular click. The click's sound varies between stimulators and between coils, and on some devices it changes with intensity.

**Recording the click** (follows the validated TAAC procedure, Russo et al. 2022):

- Use an omnidirectional microphone with no filtering or distortion anywhere in the chain.
- Place it about **25 cm to the side of the coil**.
- Record at **96 kHz, 24-bit**.
- Fire a few pulses in a quiet room with little echo.
- Cut the recording from the sharp onset of the click to the point where the signal returns to baseline.
- **Safety:** keep the microphone and every metal object outside the magnetic field. Metal inside the field can be thrown violently.
- Record a new click whenever you change the stimulator or coil, and check whether your device's click changes with intensity.

**Designing the noise:**

- **Click-based component:** your recorded click repeated at pseudo-random intervals (a fixed latency plus a uniform random jitter).
- **White-noise component:** generated white noise, which can be resampled to shift its balance between high and low frequencies.
- Mix the two at an explicit **white-to-click ratio**, and set the noise volume separately from the system volume.
- Turn on **broadband shuffling** first. It mixes the click with eleven resampled copies of itself (about ×0.3 to ×2.0), which spreads energy across the spectrum around the real click. This setting produced the validated noises, and it also reduces how many parameters you need to tune for each participant.
- Intensity shuffling (random attenuation of each click) is optional. **Spatial shuffling (inter-aural delay) has not been validated**, so don't rely on it.
- **Save the parameter set** so the same noise can be reproduced in every session.

**Tools:**

- **TAAC** (MATLAB, published and validated) is the default choice. It records the click, builds the noise, lets you tune it live while the participant listens, and can export a .wav file.
- **CAMpy-TMS** is a Python alternative for scripted or MATLAB-free workflows. It is still in progress, it is not calibrated, and it has not been validated against TAAC. Its "click-shaped" noise matches the click's spectrum but not its sharp onset, so the two approaches aren't interchangeable until someone tests them against each other. Its digital volume is not calibrated in dB SPL, so start at low hardware volume. Avoid its rhythmic click-train mode in single-pulse studies: a periodic train in the gamma range drives an auditory steady-state response at frontocentral sites, the same place you measure N100/P200.

**Why customised noise is worth the effort** (Russo et al. 2022, n = 20; one stimulator, coil and earphone setup):

| Minimum SPL needed to mask | White noise | Adapted noise | Customised noise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sham TMS | 81.6 dB | 85.4 dB | 78.3–79.0 dB |
| Real TMS | 87.1 dB | 89.0 dB | 82.9–84.1 dB |

Customised noise masked the click at a lower level in **every participant**: about 4–6 dB below white noise, which is roughly 61–83% less sound intensity. These SPL values apply only to that setup. **Don't use them as targets** for your own stimulator, coil and earphones.

## 3. Titration: do it under real TMS

**Real TMS needs about 6 dB more masking than sham** with the same noise (for example 84.1 vs 78.3 dB). The likely reason is bone conduction: during real stimulation the coil vibrates against the scalp. If you titrate on a sham or tilted coil, the real condition will be under-masked.

Procedure:

1. Fit **in-ear earphones**. They also block some room noise.
2. Put the coil at the **real target, orientation and intensity** you will use in the main blocks.
3. Fire pulses at a **jittered inter-pulse interval** (2000–2300 ms was used in the validation study).
4. Start quietly and raise the volume step by step until the participant **cannot detect a click on 10 consecutive pulses**.
5. If you are comparing noise types or conditions, randomise their order.
6. Put a **thin foam layer** under the coil to reduce vibration and bone conduction. This is a supporting measure, not a substitute for masking.
7. **Re-tune whenever intensity, coil, coil orientation, foam or target changes.** Tune for each site, not once per participant.

**Hearing safety.** Set a maximum volume in advance and don't exceed it. If you can, measure the actual SPL at the ear, for example with a head-and-torso simulator or ear simulator using the same earphones. Plan exposure time for long or repeated sessions. In the validation setup, the permitted exposure at real-TMS masking levels was about 5 h (NIOSH) for white noise and 10–13 h for customised noise. If you didn't measure SPL, say so in your methods.

---

## 4. How to check that it worked

Use several checks. Each one has gaps on its own.

### a. EEG criterion (the strongest check)
The most reliable sign that masking worked is that the **N100/P200 complex is absent** from the EEG. In the validation demonstration, with the noise off there was a clear N100 at about 96 ms and a P200 at about 196 ms. With customised noise on, both disappeared. That demonstration was in one participant, under sham.

Practical steps:

- **Click-only check:** run a short block with the coil tilted or as a sham, masking on at the level you titrated under real TMS. The frontocentral online average (around Cz/FCz, 80–250 ms) should show no N100/P200. If you can spare a few minutes, run a matched **noise-off** block to confirm the AEP is visible without masking. That proves the check is sensitive. Because the masker was set under real TMS, it should be more than enough for sham. If an AEP still appears in this check, the noise is inadequate.
- **Real-TMS check:** in a 10–30 trial online average, look at the early local TEP near the target compared with the late frontocentral N100/P200. A **weak early response with a large, broad N100/P200** points to sensory contamination (auditory, somatosensory, or both). Raise the masking level within your safety cap, add or adjust foam, then re-check. Under real TMS, a remaining N100/P200 can't be attributed only to the click, because scalp sensation and genuine cortical activity also contribute. Read it as a warning sign, not as a clean measure of masking.
- Inspect single trials before trusting averages, and don't over-interpret a noisy 10–30 trial preview.

### b. Perceptual report (secondary)
- Ask after titration and after every block whether the participant heard any clicks.
- When you compare sites, conditions or groups, collect **four separate 0–10 ratings after each block**: click loudness, scalp muscle twitch, discomfort and pain. Perceived click loudness is the rating most strongly linked to larger late TEPs. Pain ratings are not a useful proxy for contamination.
- A between-site or between-group comparison of N100/P200 is not interpretable without these ratings. Differences in click loudness alone can produce N100/P200 differences.

### c. For participants who can't report
For people with disorders of consciousness, infants and non-compliant patients, the **EEG criterion is the only check available**. Build the click-only and noise-on/off blocks into the protocol from the start.

---

## 5. Site dependence

In the largest sensory-confound dataset available (n = 135, three sites), white-noise masking failed to stop click perception in nearly all participants. It was **least successful at prefrontal cortex**, which produced the loudest perceived click despite the lowest stimulation intensity. For frontal or lateral targets:

- Expect more auditory and muscle burden, and use customised noise rather than white noise.
- Verify masking at that specific site, not at an easier one.
- Higher stimulation intensity does not tell you how loud the click will be.

---

## 6. Limits to keep in mind

- Masking reduces auditory input, but it hasn't been shown to remove it in every setup.
- Masking the click perceptually does not mean the neural confounds are equal. Even with masking active, N45, N100 and P180 differed between active TMS and a realistic sham that participants couldn't tell apart.
- Masking does nothing about **somatosensory** input (scalp sensation, vibration). For interpretation-sensitive designs, add a somatosensory control as well, such as a realistic sham, or site-matched electrical scalp stimulation with the coil tilted 90° so the click is preserved.

---

## 7. Session checklist

**Before the study**
- [ ] Click recorded from *your* stimulator and coil (96 kHz/24-bit, mic about 25 cm to the side, no metal in the field)
- [ ] Masker built (click-based plus white noise, broadband shuffling); parameter set saved
- [ ] Earphone chain tested; maximum volume cap defined; SPL measured if possible
- [ ] Click-only and noise-on/off check blocks written into the protocol

**On the day, for each participant and site**
- [ ] Coil at the real target, orientation and intensity; foam in place
- [ ] Titrate under **real TMS**: jittered pulses, raise volume until 10 consecutive clicks are undetected, stay under the cap
- [ ] Click-only check: no N100/P200 at frontocentral sites with masking on
- [ ] Real-TMS online average: early local TEP visible, not dominated by late N100/P200
- [ ] Go/no-go: proceed only if the click is inaudible, the AEP is absent in the click-only check, and the late window isn't dominating
- [ ] Re-titrate after any change to intensity, coil, orientation, foam or target
- [ ] Sound, twitch, discomfort and pain ratings after each block

**What to report**
- Where the noise came from (which stimulator and coil was recorded), the noise type and parameters, and the earphone model
- The titration criterion and the condition used for titration (real TMS)
- The verification method (EEG N100/P200 check plus self-report) and the result
- What triggered re-tuning, and the final volume or SPL. State plainly if SPL was not measured
- Sensory ratings for each block and condition
