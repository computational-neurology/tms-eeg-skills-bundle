# TMS-EEG Preprocessing Outline: 64-ch, 5 kHz, M1, BrainVision

## Assumptions (please confirm)
- The data are **continuous** BrainVision recordings (`.vhdr`/`.vmrk`/`.eeg`), and TMS pulses are marked by **hardware triggers** in the marker file. The exact marker label is a placeholder, e.g. `Stimulus/S  1`.
- **Single-pulse** M1 stimulation. The endpoint is TEPs plus GMFA/LMFP at roughly 15–300 ms, not immediate (<10 ms) responses.
- The amplifier is TMS-compatible, with a known online reference. There is a sham or sensory-control condition, or at least sensory ratings.
- Python/MNE is the default environment. The same order carries over to EEGLAB/TESA.
- All windows, filter bands and thresholds below are **placeholders**. Set them from your own data, not from these numbers.

## Ordered steps (one-line rationale each)

1. **Load the raw data at the native 5 kHz and save an untouched copy.** Later artifact-window and timing decisions need sample-level resolution and provenance, and you can't recover them once the data are resampled.
2. **Check the metadata.** Print the sampling rate, channel types (mark EOG/EMG as non-EEG), montage/electrode positions, online reference, annotations and condition labels. Wrong channel types, a missing montage or mislabelled conditions break everything downstream without any visible error.
3. **Extract and verify the TMS events.** Use the hardware markers. Count pulses per condition, check inter-pulse intervals and overlay pulse-locked traces. Duplicate, missing or delayed triggers smear TEPs and make the early windows impossible to interpret.
4. **Inspect the pulse, decay/recharge and muscle profile at native rate** (butterfly plot of the first ~50 ms). The pulse artifact window depends strongly on sampling rate and intensity, so it has to be set from your data rather than assumed.
5. **Remove the pulse window and interpolate across it** (e.g. a placeholder of about −2 to +5…10 ms, the shortest defensible window). Do this before any filtering, resampling or ICA. Filtering or decomposing across an unhandled pulse spreads ringing into the TEP window, and that ringing can't be undone later.
6. **Epoch around the pulses** (placeholder e.g. −1000 to +1000 ms, long enough to keep filter edges away from the analysis window). Epoching aligns trials to the pulse and keeps unrelated inter-trial segments out of the cleaning.
7. **Apply a provisional baseline correction** using a pre-pulse window free of preceding pulses and artifact (placeholder e.g. −500 to −10 ms). This removes DC offsets so the bad-trial/channel screening and ICA are not dominated by drifts. For short epochs, prefer this over an early 1 Hz high-pass.
8. **Downsample (optional), e.g. to a placeholder of 1000 Hz.** Do it only after steps 3–5 are verified, using a resampler with a proper anti-alias filter. This cuts computation for ICA. Doing it after pulse removal stops aliasing and anti-alias ringing from smearing the pulse into early latencies. Skip it, or keep a 5 kHz branch, if you will look at very early responses.
9. **Mark bad channels and remove gross bad trials** (flat, bridged, saturated or noisy channels; trials with large movement or other non-stereotyped artifacts). Mark bad channels but don't interpolate them yet. Noisy channels and unique artifacts destabilise ICA and inflate the averages.
10. **Run ICA for ocular, blink and non-stimulus-locked (background) muscle artifacts**, with bad channels excluded and rank handled. If ICA needs a high-passed training copy, keep it separate from the analysis data. This removes stereotyped non-TMS artifacts. Do **not** reject TMS-stimulus-locked components or auditory/somatosensory components by default, because they overlap with genuine TEP signal.
11. **Handle residual TMS-evoked muscle or decay if present.** Use projection (SSP-SIR), a justified slightly longer interpolation, or trial rejection, not ordinary ICA rejection. Muscle artifact is usually modest at M1 but still has to be checked. These methods target stimulus-locked artifact without stripping overlapping cortical signal.
12. **Optional SOUND** for channel-specific noise, if you use the SOUND/SSP-SIR branch, placed before SSP-SIR. It suppresses residual channel-level noise and can also reconstruct bad channels in a data-driven way.
13. **Filter late and gently.** For example, low-pass at about 45–100 Hz and a notch/bandstop at 50/60 Hz if needed. Only add a mild high-pass (≈0.1–1 Hz) if it is justified on long epochs. Filtering only after the sharp transients are gone avoids ringing that can look like N15/P30.
14. **Interpolate the bad channels marked in step 9** (spherical spline or SOUND). This restores the full montage after ICA, so interpolated channels don't create dependencies that bias the decomposition.
15. **Do a final round of trial rejection** (amplitude/variance or visual), and report retained trials per condition. ICA and filtering can reveal residual artifacts, and unequal trial loss across conditions biases comparisons.
16. **Re-reference to the common average across all EEG channels** (or a justified Laplacian/CSD). The reference defines the spatial features, so it must come after channel repair and before any ROI selection.
17. **Apply the final baseline correction** with a short, clean pre-pulse window (placeholder e.g. −110 to −10 ms) and trim the epochs to the analysis window. This sets a common pre-pulse zero for TEP amplitudes after all the cleaning shifts.
18. **Average the TEPs per condition and compute GMFA and LMFP.** Select the LMFP ROI (e.g. channels around C3/C1/CP3 for left M1) only after the full-channel reference is applied. Picking the ROI first and then applying the average reference within it would artificially shrink the local response.
19. **Produce QC outputs and a processing log.** These include pre- and post-interpolation traces, the bad-channel map, ICA component reports and exclusions, spectra before and after filtering/resampling, butterfly and topography plots, GMFA/LMFP, and trial counts. Without these, the cleaning can't be audited and reviewers can't separate artifact from physiology.

## M1-specific notes
- **MEP reafference.** Suprathreshold M1 pulses produce MEPs, and the resulting somatosensory reafference adds to TEPs from roughly 40–60 ms onward. Log MEP amplitudes if EMG was recorded, and consider them as trial-level covariates.
- **Sensory confounds.** The click and scalp sensation contribute strongly to the N100/P200. Interpret late peaks against sham or sensory-control data and sensory ratings (sound, twitch, discomfort, pain rated separately). Don't use ICA to "clean" them away.
- **Early components still need checking.** N15/P30 are closer to direct cortical activity but are not automatically artifact-free. Check that the interpolation window and filter edges do not overlap your earliest analysis window.

## Caveats
- Step order varies across published pipelines (TESA, ARTIST, TMSEEG, SOUND/SSP-SIR), especially for downsampling, baseline and filtering. If you are reproducing a named protocol, follow its order and say so.
- ICA-based cleaning tends to shrink all TEP peaks. When comparing amplitudes against ICA-free pipelines or normative data, keep this in mind or run a sensitivity check.
- Apply exactly the same pipeline, windows and resampling to active and control conditions.
- TEP, GMFA and LMFP are scalp-level responses shaped by cortical, sensory, muscle and preprocessing factors. They are not pure measures of M1 excitability.
