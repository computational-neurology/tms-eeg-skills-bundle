# Online QC and Go/No-Go Criteria for a TMS-EEG Session

## Assumptions

You didn't say which target, protocol or endpoint you're using, so I assumed the following:

- **Protocol:** single-pulse TMS-EEG with a TEP endpoint (early local TEP and/or later components).
- **Monitoring:** online display of single trials and short running averages (rt-TEP-style).
- **Target:** unspecified. Where criteria differ, notes for frontal/DLPFC, M1, parietal and i-TEP (very early, <~10 ms) studies are given separately.
- **Numbers:** the numeric values below are practical acquisition heuristics, not universal thresholds. Write your own values into the protocol before the session.

The main principle is that **online QC decides whether the data will be usable.** Preprocessing can't rescue data dominated by pulse, decay, recharge, muscle or sensory artifacts. If a check fails, fix the setup. Don't record anyway and plan to clean it offline.

---

## 1. When to check

| Phase | What you do | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Setup gate** (before the first main block) | Deliver setup pulses. Inspect raw single trials. Build a **10-30 trial online average**. Tune coil, intensity, cables and masking. | Go / Adjust / No-go for the main acquisition |
| **B. Ongoing monitoring** (during each block) | Watch raw EEG, artifact profile, coil tracking, rejection rate and participant state. | Continue / Pause and fix |
| **C. Re-gate** (after any change to intensity, coil position or orientation, target, foam/spacer, or a long break) | Repeat the relevant Phase-A checks. | Go / Adjust |

---

## 2. Setup gate: check by check

### 2.1 Signal integrity and synchronization

| Check | GO | ADJUST / NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| Electrode contact under and around the coil | Stable, low impedance (e.g., the common <5 kΩ convention, or your system's spec). No gel bridges. Cap fits firmly under the coil. | Drifting, noisy, bridged or loose channels near the target. Fix them before stimulating. A channel that gets worse during the session is a pause trigger. |
| Lead/cable routing | Wires near the coil run roughly **perpendicular** to the coil's main axis. No loops, no slack, no cable tension. Routing is documented. | Loops or wires running parallel near the coil. Re-route, then re-check the decay artifact. |
| Trigger timing and event labels | Every pulse has a marker. Latency is constant and condition labels are correct. | Missing or jittered triggers, or wrong labels. **Hard no-go** until fixed. |
| Amplifier saturation / recovery | Channels recover after every pulse. No channels stay clipped. | Persistent saturation or slow recovery. Check amplifier settings, sampling and synchronization. |

### 2.2 Early artifacts (inspect single trials, not only the average)

| Check | GO | ADJUST / NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| **TMS-pulse artifact** | Short and consistent. In a well-prepared high-sampling setup the visible pulse artifact can be under ~1-2 ms, but how long it looks depends on the amplifier, filters and sampling rate. | Pulse artifact extends well beyond the first few ms. Check amplifier recovery, decay, lead configuration, contact and synchronization. |
| **Decay / back-to-baseline** | Signal returns to baseline quickly and similarly across channels. In good high-sampling setups this can be ~2-3 ms, but it depends on the setup. The decay window ends before your analysis window starts. | Slow, exponential-like drifts in specific channels or along specific cable paths, or decay that lengthens with intensity. Fix contact or gel, re-route leads, make small coil adjustments, or lower intensity. **No-go if decay runs into your analysis window**, because it can mimic an early TEP. |
| **Recharge/discharge artifact** | Absent from the analysis window, or at a known, fixed latency outside it. | It falls inside the analysis window. Change the stimulator recharge timing or the analysis window before collecting data. |
| **Early muscle-artifact peak** (frontal, temporal, lateral and facially close targets) | Any focal bipolar, MEP-like deflection after the pulse artifact (later than ~4 ms, over frontal/temporal/jaw/facial channels) is small relative to the early local response. The early window stays interpretable. | That peak dominates the early window, or is the **only** time-locked activity visible. Rotate or reposition the coil slightly and/or lower intensity. If it still dominates the endpoint window, **no-go** for an early-TEP endpoint: change the target, use a backup target, or change the endpoint. |
| Craniofacial/scalp EMG in single trials | Occasional bursts, with the expected rejection rate within budget. | Bursts in most trials. Readjust, and remind the participant to relax the jaw and face. |

**Important:** whether the participant feels or shows a twitch tells you about comfort, not about the EEG. At non-motor sites the early muscle-artifact peak in the EEG can be large even when no twitch is felt. "The participant is comfortable" does **not** mean the early window is clean. Judge from the EEG itself.

### 2.3 Is there a usable cortical response?

| Check | GO | ADJUST / NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| **Early local TEP near the target** (10-30 trial average) | A clear early local response in channels near the coil. As a working target, about **8-10 µV peak-to-peak in the ~8-50 ms window**. Its topography is plausible: not a focal dipole over muscle-rich channels, and not confined to specific leads. | Response is flat or barely visible. Adjust coil orientation (aim for current roughly perpendicular to the target gyrus/sulcus wall), position, then intensity, staying within the artifact and comfort ceiling. |
| **Early vs late pattern** | Early local response and later components both present, in a sensible ratio. | **Red flag:** weak early local response with a large N100/P200. Suspect sensory contamination, a missed target, or too little effective stimulation. If raising intensity grows only N100/P200 and not the early response, stop raising it. |
| Consistency | Response shape is similar across successive mini-averages. | Unstable from one mini-average to the next. Check coil tracking, contact and participant state. Don't over-tune to a single noisy preview. |

For **M1**, RMT is useful for safety and as a starting intensity. It does **not** tell you the TEP is good, so the early-TEP check still applies. For **non-motor targets**, a fixed % of RMT isn't enough on its own. Set the final intensity from the online TEP, then the artifact and discomfort ceiling, then safety limits, and record that decision chain.

### 2.4 Sensory confounds (masking and controls)

| Check | GO | ADJUST / NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| **Masking titration** | Titrated under **real TMS** (not sham or a tilted coil) at the final intensity and coil position. With jittered inter-pulse intervals, the participant detects **no click across 10 consecutive pulses**. | Titrated on sham, or before the intensity or coil position changed. Re-titrate. Real TMS typically needs about 6 dB more masking than sham. |
| **EEG check that masking works** | **No clear N100/P200 auditory evoked potential** in the online average with masking on. This is the reliable criterion. Self-report is the weaker check. | A clear N100/P200 pattern (around 100 and 200 ms, central) persists. Improve the masker (one built from your recorded click works better than plain white noise), use foam/spacer, and re-check. If you can't suppress it and your endpoint is a late component, **no-go** for that endpoint. |
| Somatosensory / scalp sensation | Foam or spacer in place if planned. Sensory ratings collected (loudness, scalp sensation, twitch, discomfort). | Strong scalp sensation or pain. Readjust. Collect ratings anyway, because they're needed to interpret N100/P200. |
| Sham/control condition | Its sensory and artifact profile is close enough to active for the planned comparison. | Clearly different artifact or sensory profile. Revisit the control before collecting data. |
| Hearing safety | Total noise exposure at the masking level fits within exposure limits for the session length. | Over the limit. Use a more efficient custom masker, or shorten or split the session. |

### 2.5 Targeting and participant

| Check | GO | ADJUST / NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| Neuronavigation | Registration is acceptable. Coil is on target with stable orientation and tilt. Target and coil evidence saved. | Poor registration or unstable tracking. Re-register before data collection. |
| Tolerability | Participant tolerates the stimulation and the masking volume. | Unacceptable discomfort. Lower intensity or adjust the coil, then re-check the TEP. If you can't find a setup that is both tolerable and interpretable, **no-go**. |
| Safety | No adverse signs. Screening valid. | Any safety concern is an immediate **stop**. |

### 2.6 Feasibility

| Check | GO | NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| Expected usable trials | Rejection rate seen online, projected over the planned blocks, still leaves enough trials per condition for the endpoint. | Projected usable trials fall below your minimum. Fix the cause, or add trials if time, tolerability and noise exposure allow. |

The online preview uses 10-30 trials and is only a QC tool. It doesn't show final reliability, and the final trial count must be planned separately.

## 3. Overall setup-gate decision

**GO to main acquisition only if all of the following hold:**

1. Triggers and labels are correct, and channels near the coil are stable.
2. Pulse, decay and recharge artifacts are short, consistent and outside the analysis window.
3. The early muscle-artifact peak doesn't dominate the window your endpoint uses.
4. An early local TEP is visible with plausible topography, if early TEPs are a QC variable or an endpoint.
5. Masking was titrated under real TMS, and the online average shows no clear N100/P200 auditory evoked potential.
6. Control/sham conditions are acceptable, and sensory ratings are being collected.
7. The participant tolerates the setup, and noise exposure is within limits.
8. Projected usable trials meet the endpoint requirement.

**ADJUST (then re-check) when any item fails but looks fixable.** Work through these in order:

1. Fix wires, gel and contact. Re-route leads.
2. Make small coil rotations or repositioning, then intensity changes, checking the artifact ceiling and comfort each time.
3. Re-titrate masking and check the auditory evoked potential again.
4. Move to the pre-specified backup target.

**NO-GO (don't start, or stop) when any of these apply:**
- A trigger or synchronization fault can't be fixed.
- Decay or muscle artifact still dominates the endpoint window after reasonable adjustment.
- There's no interpretable early response at any tolerable intensity, for an early-TEP endpoint.
- The auditory evoked potential can't be suppressed, for a late-component endpoint.
- The participant can't tolerate the setup.
- There's any safety concern.

Set a time or iteration limit for tuning in advance, so the session doesn't drift into overfitting noisy previews.

## 4. During recording: pause triggers

Pause and re-check if you see any of the following:

- **A sudden change** in pulse, decay or muscle artifact, or new decay in a channel. This usually means contact or a lead has shifted.
- **Coil drift** beyond your tolerance for position or angle, or a change in tilt.
- **Rejection rate rising** so that usable trials per condition will fall short.
- **Channel drift or rising impedance** near the coil.
- **Trigger problems:** missing or irregular markers.
- **Participant changes:** drowsiness, jaw tension, discomfort, or reports of hearing the click again.
- **Any change** to intensity, coil, target or foam. Re-titrate masking and re-check the auditory evoked potential before resuming.

Also check that **artifact profiles are similar across conditions** (active vs control, different intensities or orientations). Artifact differences between conditions can produce false condition effects.

## 5. Target- and endpoint-specific tightening

- **DLPFC / lateral frontal / temporal targets:** expect craniofacial muscle artifact and loud perceived clicks. Residual muscle artifact after offline cleaning is common at prefrontal sites, and white-noise masking works worst there. Watch the ~5-20 ms window closely. Choose coil orientation for the individual rather than defaulting to 45°. Keep a backup target ready. Don't design an endpoint that depends on samples you expect to interpolate.
- **M1:** the RMT threshold and TEP quality are separate gates. MEP-related reafferent activity and i-TEP muscle contamination need attention.
- **Parietal:** usually lower muscle burden, but online inspection is still required.
- **i-TEP / very early components:** use stricter criteria. You need high sampling, documented pulse-artifact and back-to-baseline timing, documented lead configuration, and checks that the early peak doesn't simply reverse with current direction. A higher sampling rate shortens the visible pulse artifact but doesn't remove decay. Decay lengthens with intensity. Don't accept an early peak as neural without artifact and control evidence.
- **Comparisons between sites or groups:** collect sensory ratings in every session. N100/P200 differences are expected from differences in click loudness and scalp sensation alone. Masking must be tuned and checked per site.

---

## 6. What to log at each gate

- Target coordinates, coil model, orientation, current direction and handle angle.
- Intensity decision chain: RMT or E-field starting point, then online TEP response, then artifact/comfort ceiling, then final value.
- Masker type and source click, titration condition (real TMS), volume or SPL if measured, and the result of the auditory evoked potential check.
- Screenshots of single trials and the 10-30 trial average: early window, topography and N100/P200 window.
- Pulse and decay durations, problem channels, cable routing, and the impedance/contact policy.
- Rejection rate per block, every deviation and adjustment, sensory ratings, and tolerability.

---

## Caveats

- An online early TEP is a **QC and optimization signal**, not proof of pure cortical activation. A visible early deflection can still be decay, residual pulse artifact or muscle artifact.
- Real-time monitoring helps choose target, coil and intensity. It doesn't replace sham or sensory controls or offline QC.
- Passing masking perceptually doesn't guarantee that neural sensory confounds match between active and sham conditions.
