# Choosing stimulation intensity for DLPFC TMS-EEG: a brief guide

**Assumptions:** single-pulse (or mostly single-pulse) TMS-EEG over DLPFC to record TMS-evoked potentials (TEPs), with a figure-of-eight coil, biphasic pulses, MRI-guided neuronavigation, and healthy adults. If you plan rTMS or paired-pulse protocols, or are working with a clinical population, the logic below still holds, but you will need extra steps.

---

## 1. Core principle

The intensity at DLPFC should produce a **clear, reproducible, early local cortical response** while keeping **muscle artifacts and sensory (auditory/somatosensory) confounds** as small as possible. The motor threshold only gives you a starting point. It is not the goal itself.

## 2. Why %RMT alone is not enough

- The motor threshold (RMT) measures corticospinal excitability at M1. It predicts DLPFC excitability poorly.
- Scalp-to-cortex distance, gyral geometry and skull thickness are different at DLPFC and M1, so the same %RMT can produce quite different electric fields at the two sites.
- Even so, %RMT is still the most common way to report intensity. It is useful for comparing with the literature and for safety framing, so measure and report it anyway.

## 3. Recommended workflow

1. **Measure RMT** over the FDI hotspot with EMG, using a standard method (e.g., relative-frequency or adaptive threshold hunting). Record both %MSO and the stimulator's dI/dt readout if it has one.
2. **Pick a starting intensity.** For DLPFC single-pulse TEPs, the typical range is **~100–120% RMT**. Below ~90% RMT, many participants give weak or unreliable TEPs.
3. **Optional, recommended: E-field-informed adjustment.** Using individual MRI and E-field modelling (e.g., SimNIBS), scale the DLPFC intensity so that the modelled field at the target roughly matches the field at the M1 hotspot at RMT, or a fixed V/m target. A simpler alternative is a distance-adjusted correction (about 2.7–3% MSO per mm of extra scalp-to-cortex distance; Stokes et al., 2005).
4. **Titrate on the EEG (most important step).** Use real-time TEP monitoring with about 20–40 trials per check:
   - Look for an early (≤ ~50 ms) local response under the coil. A commonly used benchmark is roughly **≥ 6–10 µV peak-to-peak** in the average at the electrodes nearest the target (Casarotto et al., 2022).
   - If the response is too weak, first adjust **position and orientation** (rotate the coil, move slightly medially or away from the temporalis/frontalis muscles). Only then raise the intensity in small steps (~2–5% MSO).
   - If large muscle artifacts appear (biphasic, high-amplitude deflections in the first ~5–20 ms that swamp the signal), change orientation or move more medially before you try lowering intensity. Lateral and anterior DLPFC sites are the worst for this (Mutanen et al., 2013).
5. **Check the sensory confounds** at your chosen intensity:
   - Use noise masking (ideally a TMS-click-derived, time-varying mask) plus a thin foam spacer. Confirm the participant cannot hear the click.
   - Higher intensity means a stronger scalp sensation. Ask for perceived intensity/discomfort ratings, and consider a sensory/sham control condition.
   - A large vertex N100/P200 with no clear early local response suggests the EEG is dominated by sensory evoked potentials rather than a direct cortical response (Conde et al., 2019).
6. **Fix the intensity per participant** before the main recording, and keep it constant across sessions and conditions unless dose is your experimental factor.

## 4. Practical considerations

- **Stimulator ceiling:** with 110–120% RMT plus distance corrections, high-RMT participants may need more than ~80–90% MSO. That can mean coil heating and the device running out of output, so plan for it (cooled coil, inter-trial interval).
- **Trial count vs. intensity:** a slightly lower intensity with good artifact control and more trials (e.g., 100–150+ per condition) usually beats a high-intensity, artifact-heavy recording.
- **Intensity as a factor:** if you are interested in dose–response, use 2–3 intensities (e.g., 80/100/120% RMT, or E-field-matched levels), randomize them, and match sensory stimulation across levels as closely as you can.
- **Safety:** single-pulse DLPFC TMS at these intensities is well within current safety guidelines (Rossi et al., 2021). Still screen participants and monitor discomfort.

## 5. What to report

- %MSO, %RMT, the RMT method, and E-field estimates (V/m) if modelled
- Coil type, pulse waveform, current direction and orientation (angle relative to the midline)
- Target definition (coordinates or method) and how the coil position was tracked
- Any online TEP criterion used and the adjustments made (position, orientation, intensity)
- Masking procedure and sensory/perception ratings

## 6. Quick summary

**Start at about 100–120% RMT. Adjust for anatomy (E-field or distance). Then finalize position, orientation and intensity based on a clean early local TEP in real-time monitoring, while minimizing muscle and sensory confounds.**
