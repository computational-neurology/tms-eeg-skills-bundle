# Online QC During a TMS-EEG Session: What to Check and Go/No-Go Criteria

## Assumptions

- Single-pulse TMS-EEG with a TEP endpoint (local early TEP, GMFA/LMFP, or late components). Target, EEG system and population were not specified, so the criteria below are general. Notes for specific targets and for very early (i-TEP) endpoints are at the end.
- You can see an online view: raw single trials plus a running or short-block average (an rt-TEP-style display or your amplifier's online averaging).
- Any numbers below are **practical acquisition targets, not universal thresholds**. Write your lab's values into the protocol **before** the session so the decision is not made after looking at the data.

## Core principle

Treat target, coil orientation, intensity, masking, electrodes/leads and artifact profile as **one coupled setup problem**. Solve it in a short setup phase before the main blocks:

1. Deliver a short setup block, typically **10-30 trials**.
2. Inspect **single trials first**, then the average and its topography.
3. Adjust, repeat, and only start the main acquisition when the pre-defined go criteria are met.

The 10-30 trial average is a **QC signal, not an inference basis**. Use it to tune the setup, but do not overfit to a noisy preview, and do not treat it as evidence about your effect. Never start or continue a recording on the assumption that offline preprocessing will rescue pulse, decay, muscle or sensory artifacts that dominate the analysis window.

---

## Stage 0 - Before the first pulse (hard prerequisites)

All of these must be in place. If any one fails, it is a no-go until fixed.

| Check | Go condition |
|---|---|
| Electrode contact | Impedance/contact within your lab's limit (commonly < 5 kΩ for passive electrodes) across all channels, especially under and around the coil. No gel bridges, no loose or unstable electrodes. |
| Lead/cable routing | No loops or slack near the coil, no cable tension. Wires near the coil run roughly **perpendicular** to the coil's main axis/handle where feasible. Routing documented. |
| Triggers/synchronization | TMS trigger arrives in the EEG with correct, stable timing and correct event labels (checked on a few test pulses). |
| Neuronavigation | Head registration accepted; target, coil orientation, current direction and handle angle verified and saved. |
| Masking hardware | In-ear earphones fitted. Masking noise built from **your own** stimulator/coil click. Volume ready to titrate. |
| Safety | Screening, consent and hearing protection done. Total planned noise exposure checked against hearing-safety limits for long sessions. |

## Stage 1 - Online checks during the setup block

### 1. Raw EEG and channel health
- **Look for:** flat, noisy or drifting channels; line noise; channels that change after the coil is placed (pressure on electrodes).
- **Go:** stable baseline on all channels relevant to the endpoint. A few peripheral bad channels are acceptable if they are documented and not in the region of interest.
- **No-go/fix:** unstable channels near the target or ROI. Re-gel or re-seat them before you continue.

### 2. TMS-pulse artifact and amplifier recovery
- **Look for:** the initial high-amplitude electromagnetic artifact at time zero, its duration, and whether the amplifier saturates or recovers.
- **Go:** the artifact is brief and consistent across trials, and the signal recovers well before the first latency you plan to analyse.
- **No-go/fix:** the apparent pulse artifact runs well beyond the first few milliseconds, or channels stay saturated. Check amplifier recovery, sampling/filter settings, synchronization, electrode contact and lead configuration.

### 3. Decay (back-to-baseline) artifact
- **Look for:** a slow, exponential-like or sloped deflection after the pulse artifact. It is often confined to particular electrodes or follows particular cable paths.
- **Go:** decay is absent, or short and small relative to the planned window.
- **No-go/fix:** a decay that reaches into the analysis window, or that is concentrated on channels near the coil or along one cable path. Fix contact, re-route the wire, and test small coil or intensity changes. Decay can persist even with low impedance, and it grows with intensity. A higher sampling rate shortens the visible pulse artifact but **does not remove decay**.

### 4. Recharge/discharge artifacts
- **Look for:** later time-locked transients from stimulator capacitor recharge.
- **Go:** absent, or falling outside the analysis window (e.g., recharge timing moved, if your device allows it).
- **No-go/fix:** a recharge transient inside the TEP window.

### 5. Early muscle-artifact peak (the most important check at non-motor targets)
- **Look for:** an early, bipolar, MEP-like deflection appearing **after the pulse artifact, at latencies later than about 4 ms**. It is focal over frontal, temporal, jaw or facial channels and has a very localised dipolar topography.
- **Why it matters:** in a 10-30 trial online average it is often the **only** time-locked activity you can see in the early window. The real early TEP is smaller and can be hidden beneath it. Averaging makes time-locked muscle look *cleaner*, not smaller.
- **This is not the participant's twitch.** A felt or visible twitch is a comfort signal and is often absent at non-motor sites. The EEG muscle peak can be large when the participant feels nothing. "Comfortable, no twitch" is **not** evidence that the early window is clean.
- **Go:** the early muscle peak is absent, or small enough that the early window you plan to analyse can still be interpreted.
- **No-go/fix:** the muscle peak dominates the endpoint window. Try small coil rotations or position shifts, then lower intensity (treat this peak as your **intensity ceiling**). If those fail, consider the backup target. Never report this peak as an early cortical response.

### 6. Early local TEP near the target
- **Look for:** a local response in channels under or near the coil in roughly the **8-50 ms** range.
- **Practical target:** about **8-10 µV peak-to-peak** in the 8-50 ms window near the coil, in the 10-30 trial average. This is a working acquisition heuristic, not a validated threshold, so adapt it to your target and system.
- **Go:** a reproducible early local response (it holds up across two consecutive setup averages) with a plausible topography that is not just the pulse, decay or muscle pattern.
- **Adjust:** a weak or absent early response. Optimise orientation, position and intensity, staying within the artifact ceiling and safety limits.
- **Caveat:** a visible early TEP is not automatically cortical. It can still be residual decay (follows an exponential course, tracks intensity, sits on specific channels) or muscle (focal dipole near muscle-rich scalp).

### 7. Late components (N100/P200) and masking check
- **Look for:** a broad, vertex-centred N100 (~100 ms) / P200 (~180-200 ms) complex.
- **Key red flag:** if the early local response is weak but N100/P200 is large, or if **raising intensity grows N100/P200 but not the early response**, suspect auditory or somatosensory contamination, a missed target, or too little effective stimulation.
- **Masking go criterion:** titrate under **real TMS, not sham**, at the final intensity and coil position. Real stimulation needs about **6 dB more** masking than sham because of bone conduction. Raise the volume until the participant detects no click on **10 consecutive pulses** at jittered intervals. Then confirm that the **N100/P200 complex is absent or clearly reduced** in the online average. The EEG check is the reliable one; self-report alone is weak.
- **No-go/fix:** a clear N100/P200 that remains with masking on. Re-titrate, check earphone fit, and add foam/spacer for bone-conducted and scalp sensation.

### 8. Topography of the average
- **Go:** an early topography consistent with stimulation near the target, rather than (a) a focal dipole over temporal or facial muscle, (b) a pattern that follows one cable or electrode cluster, or (c) a waveform that reverses polarity exactly with the coil current direction (suggesting residual pulse artifact).

### 9. Participant tolerability and sensory report
- **Go:** the participant tolerates the stimulation and masking volume for the planned session length. Click loudness, scalp sensation, twitch and discomfort ratings are recorded. These ratings are required if you will compare sites, groups or conditions.
- **No-go:** pain, discomfort the participant cannot tolerate, or a masking level above safe exposure for the planned duration.

### 10. Control/sham condition (if part of the design)
- **Go:** the sham/control setup runs and gives a comparable sensory experience. Masking was **not** titrated on sham. The artifact profile has been compared between active and control conditions where possible.

### 11. Expected trial yield
- **Look for:** the fraction of setup trials you would reject (muscle bursts, blinks, saturation, bad channels).
- **Go:** after the expected rejection rate is applied, the planned number of pulses will still give your pre-specified minimum usable trials per condition.
- **No-go/fix:** projected usable trials below that minimum. Fix the artifact source, or extend the blocks if time and safety allow.

## Summary go/no-go rule

**GO to the main recording only if ALL of the following hold:**

1. Contact, triggers, lead routing and navigation are verified and stable.
2. Pulse, recharge and decay artifacts end, or are manageable, before the analysis window starts.
3. The early muscle-artifact peak does not dominate the endpoint window.
4. The early local TEP is visible and reproducible, if it is a QC criterion or the endpoint.
5. Masking was titrated under real TMS and the online average shows no clear N100/P200.
6. The participant tolerates stimulation and masking, and sensory ratings are recorded.
7. Projected usable trials after rejection meet your minimum.

**PAUSE AND ADJUST** if any item fails. Work through fixes in order of least disruption:
cap/wire fixes → small coil rotation/position change → intensity change (then **re-titrate masking** and re-check decay and muscle) → masking re-titration → backup target.

**NO-GO (stop or reschedule)** if, after reasonable adjustment:
- artifacts still dominate the planned window, or
- no interpretable early response can be obtained within safety and tolerability limits, or
- the participant cannot tolerate the required stimulation or masking.

Set a time box for the setup phase in advance (e.g., a fixed number of optimisation rounds or minutes). This stops "tuning until it looks good" from becoming an undocumented degree of freedom.

---

## During the main recording: stop and re-check triggers

Pause and re-inspect if any of these happen:
- Pulse, decay or muscle artifact changes suddenly, or a new decay appears on a channel.
- Navigation shows target drift or a coil angle change beyond your tolerance. Recheck after any coil repositioning.
- Channels drift, impedance worsens, or the rejection rate climbs.
- Trigger timing or synchronization becomes irregular.
- Intensity, coil or target is changed for any reason. Re-titrate masking under real TMS and repeat the N100/P200 check.
- The participant reports discomfort, the click becoming audible, or new sensations.
- Conditions are becoming unbalanced (e.g., one condition losing far more trials).

---

## What to log at each decision point

- Target coordinates, coil orientation, current direction, handle angle, and intensity. Record intensity as a decision chain: starting RMT/E-field estimate → online TEP response → artifact/discomfort ceiling → final locked value.
- Screenshots of the setup averages and topographies, with the go/no-go decision and the reason for it.
- Pulse-artifact duration and decay/back-to-baseline timing.
- Masking noise source, parameters, final volume (SPL if measured; state if it was not), and the condition it was titrated under.
- Sensory ratings, rejected/bad channels, every adjustment made, and the final estimate of usable trials.

## Target- and endpoint-specific adjustments

- **DLPFC / lateral frontal / temporal / language targets:** expect craniofacial muscle and strong auditory burden. Prefrontal sites can give the loudest perceived click even at lower intensity. Check the early muscle peak first, and verify masking at each site rather than once per participant. Have a backup target ready.
- **M1:** use RMT for safety and calibration, but judge EEG quality separately. Watch for muscle and coil-orientation effects in the early window.
- **Parietal (superior):** often a lower-muscle setup, but it still needs online inspection.
- **Occipital:** plan for visual/sensory confounds.
- **i-TEP or very early endpoints (< ~10 ms):** apply stricter criteria. Use high sampling and verified synchronization, explicitly measure the pulse-artifact window and back-to-baseline timing, and document lead/cable configuration. Do not accept an early peak as neural without control evidence (e.g., current-direction reversal, intensity dependence, control conditions). Peaks that reverse with pulse polarity, track intensity like decay, or sit focally near muscle are no-go for interpretation.
- **Comparisons between sites or groups:** collect sensory ratings in every session. N100/P200 differences are expected from differences in click loudness and scalp sensation alone. Distance-corrected %RMT does not equalise cortical dose between sites.

## Limits

Online checks reduce acquisition failures but do not replace sham/sensory controls or offline QC. Passing perceptual masking does not guarantee that auditory and somatosensory inputs are neurally equivalent across conditions.
