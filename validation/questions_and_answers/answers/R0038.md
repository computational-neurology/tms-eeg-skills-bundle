# Conclusion sentence for the N45 finding after 20 Hz rTMS

## Short answer

I would not write that cortical excitability increased. A larger N45 alone does not support that claim, and the most widely used interpretation of the N45 points the other way. Below is why, followed by sentences you can use instead.

## Why "increased excitability" is not supported

1. **The N45 is usually read as a marker of GABA-A-mediated inhibition, not excitation.** Pharmaco-TMS-EEG studies (e.g., Premoli et al., 2014, J Neurosci) found that positive allosteric modulators of GABA-A receptors (alprazolam, diazepam, zolpidem) increase N45 amplitude. A GABA-B agonist (baclofen) instead increases the N100. Drugs that act on voltage-gated sodium channels or NMDA receptors also change early TEP components, so no peak reflects a single mechanism. Still, by the standard reading, a 30% larger N45 suggests stronger GABA-A-related inhibitory activity, or at least a change in the excitation/inhibition balance. It does not suggest greater net excitability.

2. **TEP amplitude does not measure excitability directly.** "Cortical excitability" in TMS work is usually tied to measures such as MEP amplitude, motor threshold, early TEP components (e.g., P30/N15–P30 at M1) or global mean field power in the first ~30 ms. A change in one negative peak can come from a shift in the timing of nearby components (P30/P60), a change in the reference or source orientation, or a different mix of underlying sources. It does not have to come from a uniform rise in responsiveness.

3. **Several confounds have to be ruled out first:**
   - **Sham/control condition.** Without a sham rTMS or control-site arm, you cannot separate an rTMS effect from time, repeated testing, arousal or vigilance changes, or habituation to the TMS click and scalp sensation.
   - **Sensory (peripheral) evoked potentials.** Auditory and somatosensory responses mostly affect later components (N100/P180), but they can overlap with mid-latency peaks. Changes in noise masking, in perceived intensity, or in habituation between pre and post can alter the waveform.
   - **Stimulation parameters.** Was the post-rTMS test pulse delivered at the same intensity, position and coil orientation (neuronavigated)? Was the hotspot/RMT re-checked? If the motor threshold changed after rTMS, a fixed intensity amounts to a different relative dose.
   - **Preprocessing and artifacts.** Pre and post data need identical pipelines, and ICA/SSP-SIR component removal has to be comparable across sessions. Decay/muscle artifacts near 30–50 ms can bias N45 amplitude.
   - **Statistics.** Is the 30% change significant at the group level, for example with cluster-based permutation over space and time or a pre-registered ROI/time window? Is it above test–retest variability? A 30% change in a single subject or a small sample may fall within normal session-to-session variability.

4. **The expected direction of 20 Hz rTMS is not settled in TEPs.** High-frequency rTMS is often called "facilitatory" based on MEP studies, but individual responses vary a lot and TEP findings are mixed. The stimulation protocol cannot be used to infer the physiological meaning of the outcome.

## Suggested conclusion sentences

Assumptions: the increase is statistically significant at the group level, the test-pulse parameters were kept constant, and a sham/control condition exists (if it does not, use the third option).

- **Primary (recommended):**
  > "Following 20 Hz rTMS over M1, the amplitude of the N45 component of the TMS-evoked potential increased by approximately 30% relative to baseline, which, given the established sensitivity of the N45 to GABA-A receptor–mediated neurotransmission, suggests an enhancement of GABA-A–related cortical inhibition rather than a straightforward increase in cortical excitability."

- **More neutral alternative:**
  > "20 Hz rTMS over M1 was followed by a ~30% increase in N45 amplitude compared with baseline, indicating a modulation of local cortical reactivity, most plausibly involving GABA-A–mediated inhibitory processes."

- **If there was no sham/control arm:**
  > "N45 amplitude over M1 was ~30% larger after 20 Hz rTMS than at baseline; however, in the absence of a sham condition, this change cannot be attributed specifically to rTMS, and its physiological interpretation (likely related to GABA-A–mediated inhibition) remains tentative."

## If you want to make an excitability claim

You would need converging evidence, for example:
- larger MEP amplitude or lower RMT after rTMS compared with sham;
- changes in the earliest local TEP components (e.g., 15–30 ms) or in early local/global mean field power;
- paired-pulse measures (SICI/ICF, or paired-pulse TEPs) to separate inhibitory from facilitatory circuits;
- a sham-controlled design with matched sensory stimulation and constant, neuronavigated test-pulse parameters.

Even then, phrase it as "increased corticospinal/cortical excitability as indexed by X" and give the N45 finding its own inhibitory interpretation, not a reading that matches the excitability claim.
