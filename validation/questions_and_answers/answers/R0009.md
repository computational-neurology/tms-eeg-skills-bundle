# Conclusion sentence for the post-20 Hz rTMS N45 change

## Short answer

I don't recommend writing "cortical excitability increased" from this result alone. A 30% larger N45 over M1 after 20 Hz rTMS is a change in an evoked EEG component. It is not a direct, pure measure of cortical excitability. The N45 in particular is a poor proxy for "excitability" in the usual sense. Below is a sentence you can defend, followed by the reasons and the evidence you would need before a stronger claim holds up.

## Recommended conclusion sentence

> "Following 20 Hz rTMS, the amplitude of the N45 component of the TMS-evoked potential over M1 increased by 30% relative to baseline, indicating that the rTMS session modulated the early cortical response to single-pulse TMS at the stimulated site."

If you have a sham/control rTMS condition and the interaction is significant, you can make it stronger:

> "Compared with sham rTMS, 20 Hz rTMS increased the M1 N45 TEP amplitude by 30% relative to baseline (condition × time interaction, [statistic, p]), consistent with an rTMS-induced change in early cortical reactivity at the stimulated site."

If you want to discuss mechanism, keep it in the discussion and hedge it:

> "Because the N45 has been linked pharmacologically to GABA-A receptor–mediated neurotransmission, this increase may reflect altered local inhibitory, rather than purely excitatory, processes; this interpretation requires confirmation with converging measures."

## Why "cortical excitability increased" is not justified as written

1. **TEP amplitude is not a pure readout of excitability.** A TEP is an averaged, time-locked response. It reflects a spatiotemporal sum of excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic activity across local and network sources, plus whatever sensory and artifact signal is left after preprocessing. A larger peak does not by itself mean the tissue became "more excitable".

2. **The N45 points, if anything, toward inhibition.** Pharmacological TMS-EEG work most often links the N45 to GABA-A–mediated activity. So a larger N45 could just as well be read as stronger inhibitory signalling. That is the opposite of the claim in your draft sentence. Peak labels and neurotransmitter mappings also depend on target, paradigm and preprocessing, so neither reading should be stated as fact.

3. **Check the sign convention.** The N45 is a negative peak. Say whether "increased by 30%" means it became more negative (a larger absolute or peak-to-peak value) or whether the signed value went up (less negative). Readers will read the result differently depending on which one you mean.

4. **The N45 is not free of sensory contribution.** Components before about 60 ms overlap less with peripherally evoked potentials than the N100/P200 do, but the N45 can still carry some auditory or somatosensory signal. Even with active masking, N45, N100 and P180 have been larger after real stimulation than after a perceptually matched sham. If the click loudness, scalp sensation, masking level or coil contact changed between baseline and post-rTMS, part of the 30% may come from that.

5. **A pre/post change needs a control.** Without sham rTMS or an active control site, you can't separate an rTMS effect from time-on-task, arousal or vigilance drift, electrode impedance drift, gel drying, coil displacement or ordinary session-to-session variability in TEP amplitude.

6. **Early-window artifacts and preprocessing can change amplitude.** Residual cranial muscle and decay artifact, and how strongly ICA, SOUND or SSP-SIR were applied, all change early TEP amplitudes. If baseline and post blocks differed in artifact load, rejected trials or components, or retained rank, the amplitude difference may partly come from the cleaning.

## What would support a stronger claim

You could defensibly write "increased corticospinal/cortical excitability" only with converging evidence such as:

- **A sham or control rTMS condition**, with a significant condition × time interaction rather than a within-condition pre/post change alone.
- **Matched sensory conditions** before and after: the same masking noise and level, and a thin foam layer or the same coil contact. Collect separate post-rTMS ratings for click loudness, scalp twitch, discomfort and pain, and show they did not change or model them as covariates.
- **Identical acquisition and stimulation parameters**: neuronavigated coil position and orientation with trials marked for target deviation, the same intensity (with the same %RMT baseline), the same number of trials, and comparable impedances.
- **Identical preprocessing** for both time points: the same pipeline, interpolation window, ICA/SOUND/SSP-SIR settings and rejection criteria. Report the number of trials and components removed and the variance removed per session. Run a sensitivity check showing the effect survives a lighter-cleaning pipeline.
- **A pre-specified N45 window and ROI**, a stated peak versus mean-amplitude versus peak-to-peak measure, and a report of whether the polarity and topography of the N45 stayed the same after rTMS.
- **Converging measures**: MEP amplitude and input–output curves (corticospinal excitability), and SICI or other paired-pulse protocols (GABA-A inhibition). These let you say whether the N45 change tracks excitation or inhibition.
- **Other TEP components** (for example N15, P30, P60) and GMFA/LMFP, to show whether the change is specific to the N45 or a general scaling of the response.

## Assumptions

- The 30% change is a within-subject (or group-mean) comparison of post-rTMS against pre-rTMS, with no sham or control condition described.
- "Over M1" means a local ROI at the stimulated M1, and the post-rTMS TEPs were recorded with the same single-pulse protocol as baseline.
- The statistical test and its significance were not stated. The sentences above assume the change is statistically supported. If it is not, describe it as a numerical change only.
