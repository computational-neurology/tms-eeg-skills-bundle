# Online checks during a TMS-EEG session: go/no-go criteria

## Assumptions
- 32–64+ channel TMS-compatible EEG amplifier with a high sampling rate (≥ 5 kHz) and a DC-coupled or high-dynamic-range front end. You can see the raw data live and show a running average of the pulse-locked response (rt-TEP style) over roughly the last 20–40 trials.
- Single-pulse TMS of a cortical target (motor or non-motor) with neuronavigation, noise masking and a foam spacer or thin-layer separation.
- The thresholds below are practical rules of thumb from common TMS-EEG practice. They are not universal standards. Adapt them to your hardware, your target and your preregistered protocol, and write the criteria down **before** the session.

The core logic: **don't run the full block until a short test block (about 20–30 pulses) shows a clean, local, early cortical response that isn't dominated by artifacts or sensory input.** Then keep checking that this stays true during the session.

---

## 1. Before the first pulse (baseline check)

| Check | GO | NO-GO, fix first |
|---|---|---|
| Electrode impedance | All channels < 5 kΩ (with active electrodes, < 10–20 kΩ may be acceptable if your system specifies this); especially low under and around the coil | Channels near the target above threshold, or impedances drifting |
| Resting raw EEG | Stable baseline, visible alpha with eyes closed, no flat or bridged channels | Line noise dominates, channels are railing, or there are slow drifts or pops |
| Lead routing | Leads lie perpendicular to the coil's induced current where possible, are bundled and immobile, with no loops under the coil | Leads run parallel under the coil handle or can move with the coil |
| Trigger/markers | One marker per pulse, correct latency (check against the artifact onset) | Markers are missing, duplicated or jittered |
| Masking | Noise is played at a level the participant tolerates (generally ≤ ~90 dB SPL), and its spectrum is matched to the coil click | Participant can still clearly hear the click at the maximum safe level (see section 3) |
| Neuronavigation | Registration error is acceptable (for example ≤ ~3–5 mm fiducial/surface error) and the target is defined | Registration is poor or the head tracker is loose |

## 2. Test block (about 20–30 pulses at the planned intensity)

Look at **single trials** and the **running average**, both re-referenced to the average and baseline-corrected, with the first few ms after the pulse blanked or interpolated only for display.

### 2a. Pulse and decay artifact
- **GO:** The amplifier recovers quickly (in modern systems the signal usually returns to the physiological range within ~5–10 ms), with no saturation or clipping.
- **NO-GO:** Channels saturate, or a large exponential decay lasts more than about 10–20 ms near the coil. Re-prep those electrodes (lower the impedance), reposition the leads, and check that no electrode is pressed or moved by the coil. Then retest.

### 2b. Muscle artifact (cranial or temporal muscles)
- **GO:** No large biphasic high-frequency deflections in the 0–~20 ms window. Any remaining spikes are small relative to the early TEP.
- **NO-GO:** Muscle responses in single trials are tens to hundreds of µV in the first ~5–20 ms and persist across trials. Rotate the coil (often 15–45° steps), move the target a few mm medially or away from temporalis/frontalis, or reduce the intensity if your protocol allows it. Then retest. Don't count on removing large muscle artifacts offline with ICA or SOUND, because doing so can distort the true early TEP.

### 2c. Early cortical response (the main "is TMS hitting cortex?" check)
- **GO:**
  - A clear early response (roughly 15–50 ms) appears in channels **under or near the coil**, larger than at distant sites and lateralised toward the stimulated hemisphere.
  - Peak-to-peak amplitude in that window is about **≥ 10 µV** in a ~20-trial average, a target that rt-TEP-style approaches commonly use. A conservative floor is ≥ ~6 µV.
  - The waveform is reproducible: two split-half averages look similar.
- **NO-GO / adjust:** The early response is absent or below the floor, or the response is mainly symmetric and vertex-maximal (which suggests a sensory origin). Adjust in this order:
  1. Coil orientation.
  2. Small position shifts within the target region.
  3. Intensity, staying within your safety limits and preregistered range. Estimated E-field targets of roughly 70–120 V/m at the cortex are often used as a reference.

  Retest after each change. If nothing works after a predefined number of attempts (for example 3–4 configurations), log the target as "no reliable TEP" according to your protocol rather than adjusting indefinitely.

### 2d. Sensory contamination (auditory and somatosensory)
- **GO:**
  - The participant reports that the click is inaudible or barely audible under masking.
  - Scalp sensation is low or tolerable (for example ≤ 3–4 on a 0–10 scale for both sensation and discomfort).
  - Little or no vertex N100–P180 complex is visible relative to the early local response.
- **NO-GO:**
  - The click is clearly audible. Raise the masking level within the safety limit, adjust the noise spectrum, or add a thicker foam layer.
  - A large symmetric late vertex component (~100–200 ms) dominates the average.
  - Scalp sensation is strong or painful. Reconsider the coil position or orientation.

  Note that late components are expected in true TEPs too. The warning sign is a *symmetric, vertex-centred* late response that outweighs a weak or absent early local response.

### 2e. Coil placement stability
- **GO:** Online deviation from the target is ≤ ~2–3 mm and ≤ ~2–5° in orientation and tilt, and the coil stays in firm, constant contact.
- **NO-GO:** The coil drifts out of tolerance repeatedly, or the participant moves. Re-fix the head or coil holder and re-register if needed.

## 3. Ongoing monitoring during the recording

Check every block (for example every 50–100 pulses):

| Check | Continue (GO) | Pause/fix (NO-GO) |
|---|---|---|
| Early TEP amplitude and shape | Stable within roughly ±30–50% of the test-block average | Systematic drop or change (coil drift, electrode problem, drowsiness) |
| Trial rejection estimate | < ~20–30% of trials visibly contaminated | Rejection projected to leave fewer trials than your minimum (for example < 80–100 clean trials per condition) |
| Navigation error | Within the tolerance above | Out of tolerance for more than a few consecutive pulses |
| Impedances (between blocks) | Still under threshold | Channels near the coil have degraded |
| Vigilance | Eyes open, stable fixation, no slow rolling eye movements or sustained alpha bursts | Signs of drowsiness. Give a break and talk to the participant |
| Blinks and eye movements | Occasional, not pulse-locked | Pulse-locked blinks (startle response to the click or sensation). Improve masking or add jitter |
| Inter-pulse timing | Jittered ITI (for example 2–3 s or longer) as planned | Fixed or too short ITI (anticipation, carry-over) |
| Participant comfort and safety | No adverse events, sensation tolerable | Headache, nausea, strong discomfort, or any seizure warning sign. **Stop**, following your safety SOP |

## 4. Summary decision rule

**GO (start or continue recording) when all of these hold:**
1. There is no saturation and the artifact recovers within ~10 ms.
2. There is no large, persistent muscle artifact in the first ~20 ms.
3. A local, lateralised early TEP (15–50 ms) appears with an amplitude of about ≥ 10 µV (minimum ~6 µV) that reproduces across split halves.
4. The click is masked or inaudible, scalp sensation is tolerable, and no dominant symmetric vertex response appears.
5. Navigation, impedance and trigger checks are within tolerance.
6. The participant is alert and comfortable.

**NO-GO (fix, then retest with a new short test block) when any one of 1–5 fails.**

**STOP the session when** there is a safety event, the participant withdraws, or a predefined number of optimisation attempts fails to give an acceptable response.

## 5. Practical tips
- Document every change you make (orientation, position, intensity, masking level, foam) together with the time and pulse count, so offline analysis can account for it.
- Pre-specify how many optimisation attempts are allowed and the exact thresholds. This avoids "tuning until it looks good", which can bias results.
- If your design compares conditions or targets, apply the same online criteria to each one. Consider a sham or sensory-control condition so you can separate genuine cortical effects from residual sensory input offline.
