# F3 TMS-EEG: fix the artifacts at acquisition first; ICA settings are secondary

## Short answer

No ICA settings will reliably fix what your pilot shows, so I would not record all 30 participants as they are. Two of the problems can't be recovered offline at all, and the third is only partly recoverable:

1. **Saturated channels can't be recovered.** When an amplifier channel clips, the true signal during that period is simply not recorded. ICA assumes the scalp data are a linear mixture of sources, and clipping is non-linear, so no component can rebuild what is missing. Those channels are lost for the saturated window. At best you can drop them and interpolate, and electrodes near F3 are the ones you most need for local early TEPs.
2. **A decay artifact that dominates the first ~20 ms means that window is mostly hardware, not brain.** ICA, fit-based detrending, SOUND-type methods and interpolation all require explicit modelling choices. Each can shrink or distort real early responses. Varying only the decay-suppression method changes early TEP amplitude *and* topography. Running ICA on a recovery window the artifact dominates is a known failure mode, not a fix.
3. **F3 is a high-risk site for muscle artifact, and ICA often fails there.** At prefrontal sites, TMS-evoked scalp muscle activity was still visible in the 5–15 ms window after a standard two-pass FastICA pipeline in about **62% of participants** (vs. 0% at parietal sites). The "decay" in your first 20 ms may partly be this early muscle peak: a bipolar, MEP-like deflection appearing after ~4 ms over frontal/temporal channels. It is not a cortical response, and ICA removes it poorly.

In practice, saving ~10–15 minutes of setup per session now risks losing the 0–20 ms window, and the local early TEP, in most of your 30 datasets. Even the late window would then depend on heavy cleaning that you can't fully check. The cheaper option is a short artifact-reduction routine for every session, plus a go/no-go check before each main block. If some artifact remains after that, ICA can deal with it (settings below).

## Assumptions

- About 60-channel TMS-compatible EEG, single-pulse TMS over F3 (left DLPFC region), single session per participant.
- Your endpoint includes early TEP components (e.g., <50 ms) and/or local responses near F3, not only N100/P200. If only late components matter, saturation and decay still matter: clipped channels are lost, and heavy early cleaning affects later latencies too.
- I don't know your amplifier model, sampling rate, intensity rule, or coil orientation. Fill these in where they're mentioned below.

## What to do before the main study (one extra pilot session, then ~10–15 min per participant)

### 1. Tell the artifact types apart in the pilot data
Look at single trials (not only averages) from the pulse to 100 ms, channel by channel:
- **Pulse artifact:** very short and very large. Its duration depends on sampling rate.
- **Decay:** a smooth, exponential-like return to baseline with consistent polarity across trials. It is often confined to particular electrodes or follows a cable path, and it grows with intensity.
- **Early muscle peak:** an oscillatory, biphasic deflection after ~4 ms, largest over frontal/temporal/jaw channels. It can be large even when the participant reports no twitch.
- **Saturation:** a flat-topped trace at the amplifier's range limit. Note which channels clip. They are usually the electrodes right under or next to the coil, or electrodes with poor contact.

Each type has a different fix, so do this sorting first.

### 2. Reduce saturation and decay at the source
- **Contact:** re-prepare channels under and around the coil (gel amount, no gel bridges, stable electrodes, impedance/contact within your system's spec). Decay can persist even at low impedance, but poor contact makes it much worse and is a common cause of channel-specific decay.
- **Lead routing:** run electrode wires roughly **perpendicular (≈90°) to the coil handle / induced-current direction**. Avoid wire loops and slack near the coil, keep cables under steady tension, and record the routing (a photo per session). Decay amplitude and even its polarity depend on lead configuration.
- **Amplifier:** check that the amplifier's input range and settings suit TMS-EEG and that its recovery after the pulse is as expected. Use a high sampling rate if early latencies matter. A high rate shortens the pulse artifact but does **not** remove decay, which depends more on intensity and setup.
- **Intensity:** decay duration increases with intensity. If you are dosing at a fixed % RMT, check whether a lower intensity, or one set from the online TEP, still gives a clear early response with less decay and less muscle.
- **Coil position/orientation:** at F3, small rotations or position shifts can greatly reduce the early muscle peak. Pick orientation from individual anatomy where possible rather than defaulting to 45°.

### 3. Online check and go/no-go before every main block
- Record 10–30 setup trials and look at single trials plus the short average.
- **Go** when: no channel saturates near the target, decay recovers within a window you defined in advance (e.g., well before your earliest component of interest), and the early muscle peak does not dominate the analysis window.
- **No-go / adjust** when: any target-region channel clips, decay still dominates beyond your pre-defined window, or the muscle peak is the only time-locked activity visible. Re-prep electrodes, re-route leads, rotate the coil slightly, or reduce intensity, then check again.
- Save a screenshot and notes for each session: target coordinates, coil orientation, intensity, lead routing, masking settings, artifact recovery time, and the go/no-go decision.
- During recording, check again after any change to intensity or orientation. Pause if the pulse, decay, or muscle artifact changes suddenly.

### 4. Also collect for F3 specifically
- **Sensory controls:** auditory masking (adjust it after any intensity/coil change), a foam spacer, and per-participant ratings of click loudness, scalp sensation and discomfort. At prefrontal sites the N100/P200 is largest and follows these ratings, so you need them to interpret late components.
- **A blink/eye-movement recording** at the start of each session, to help ICA identify ocular components.
- **Enough trials** to allow for rejection. At F3 you will lose more trials than at posterior sites.

## ICA settings for the remaining artifact, once acquisition is under control

These settings are for cleaning residual artifact. They won't rescue data where saturation or decay dominates. Treat the numbers as starting points and choose them from your data.

| Step | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Pulse window | Remove and interpolate a window chosen from your data, e.g. starting around **−2 ms to ~10–12 ms**. Set the end where the pulse artifact ends in your single trials, not from a template. |
| Saturated / bad channels | **Mark them bad before ICA** so they are not used in the decomposition. Repair them afterwards (spherical-spline interpolation or SOUND), keeping a full channel-location copy. |
| Bad trials | Reject trials with clipping or very large artifacts before ICA. |
| Data used to fit ICA | A separate copy, high-pass filtered at ~1 Hz *after* pulse removal and interpolation. Don't let this filter carry over into the final TEP data. |
| Algorithm | FastICA (the TESA-style standard). Fix the random seed so results can be reproduced. |
| Dimensionality | PCA compression to about **25–30 components** for a ~60-channel montage, or set from data rank (after average reference, interpolation and bad-channel removal). Report the rank you kept. |
| Pass structure | If needed, use two passes with a clearly separate purpose for each. Pass 1 targets large, stereotyped decay residuals that remain after setup fixes. Pass 2 targets ocular, blink and ongoing (not time-locked) muscle activity. An alternative: use ICA mainly for ocular/background artifact, then SOUND for channel noise and SSP-SIR for the remaining early TMS-evoked muscle artifact. |
| Component rejection rules | Decide from topography, time course, spectrum and the before/after TEP together. Don't reject components with clear TMS-locked activity during routine review. Handle the early muscle response with SSP-SIR, a removal window, or trial rejection instead. Don't remove auditory/somatosensory components by default; handle those with masking, controls and ratings. |
| Second interpolation window | Optional, if residual decay remains in the earliest milliseconds. Report it as a window treated as lost. |
| Filtering / downsampling | After pulse and gross-artifact handling. Low-pass around 80 Hz, notch at line frequency, downsample (e.g., 1 kHz) only after checking for filter ringing around the interpolated window. |

### Checks to plan in advance
- **Sensitivity of component count:** change the number of rejected components by ±2 and check whether the TEP stays stable.
- **Comparison with minimal processing:** in time windows without artifact, the cleaned data should match minimally processed data. ICA-based pipelines tend to reduce TEP amplitudes at all peaks, so record and report the size of this effect.
- **Muscle vs. no-muscle subgroup check:** sort participants by visible muscle activity in the *uncleaned* 5–15 ms epochs, then compare cleaned group TEPs and spatial correlations between the two groups. If they differ, the cleaning has not worked. Pre-register this check for F3.
- **Reporting:** for each participant, report which channels clipped and were interpolated, the pulse and decay windows and how they were handled, which time window was treated as unrecoverable, the components rejected and the rank kept, and the trials retained.

## Bottom line

Spend the pilot time fixing contact, lead routing, amplifier range, intensity and coil angle until no target-region channel clips and decay ends before your earliest component of interest. Then use a short online go/no-go check for each participant. With that in place, a conservative ICA (FastICA, ~25–30 PCA components, bad channels excluded, ocular/background artifact mainly, SSP-SIR for early muscle) plus the checks above will give results you can defend. Without it, no ICA setting can recover the clipped channels or a 20 ms window dominated by decay.
