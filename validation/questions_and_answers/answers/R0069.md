# One or two rounds of ICA in TMS-EEG preprocessing?

## Short answer

**Use two rounds when your data contain a large TMS-evoked muscle and/or decay artifact.** This is usually the case for lateral or frontal targets such as the DLPFC, the frontal or temporal cortex, or the lateral parietal cortex, and often for M1 at higher intensities. Each round is aimed at a different class of artifact:

| Round | When in the pipeline | What it targets |
|---|---|---|
| **ICA 1** | Early. After removing the pulse window, cutting epochs and downsampling, but **before temporal filtering** | Large, short-latency, TMS-locked artifacts: the **TMS-evoked scalp-muscle artifact** (about 0 to 15–20 ms, often in the mV range) and the **electrode/amplifier decay (recharge) artifact** |
| **ICA 2** | Later. After interpolating the removed pulse window, band-pass and notch filtering, and removing bad trials and channels | "Conventional" EEG artifacts: **eye blinks and saccades, persistent (tonic) muscle activity, single-electrode noise, line-noise residue**, and any **residual TMS-evoked muscle or decay activity** missed in round 1 |

**A single round can be enough** when the early artifact is small. Examples: a midline or vertex target, a well-tuned coil orientation and intensity, a clean pulse artifact with fast amplifier recovery, or inspection showing no high-amplitude early deflections. In that case one ICA after filtering, targeting ocular, muscle and noise components, is defensible. It is also simpler and less likely to over-correct.

## Why two rounds? The reasoning

1. **The early artifact dominates the variance.** TMS-evoked muscle activity can be 10–1000× larger than the neural TEP. When it is present, ICA spends most of its components on it, and smaller artifacts such as blinks get poorly separated or mixed into the same components. Removing the large artifact first gives the second decomposition a "fair" problem.
2. **Filtering a huge, sharp artifact causes ringing.** If you band-pass filter before removing the muscle and decay artifacts, the filter spreads them in time, both before and after the pulse. That contaminates the baseline and later TEP components. Round 1 is therefore run on **unfiltered** (or only minimally filtered) data, so you can filter safely afterwards.
3. **Different artifacts need different data conditions.** Ocular and tonic-muscle artifacts are best decomposed on filtered, cleaned data (high-pass ≥ about 1 Hz improves ICA stability). The TMS-evoked muscle artifact has to be dealt with before that filtering.

## Typical two-round sequence (TESA/EEGLAB-style)

Assumptions: about 60+ channels, single-pulse TMS, offline processing in EEGLAB/TESA or an equivalent.

1. Epoch the data, for example −1000 to +1000 ms around the pulse.
2. Remove the TMS pulse window, for example −2 to +10 ms. Adjust this to your pulse/ringing duration. Interpolate the gap only temporarily if you need to downsample.
3. Downsample, for example to 1000 Hz.
4. Remove bad channels and grossly bad trials.
5. Replace the removed pulse window with a constant (zeros) so it does not create spurious components.
6. **ICA round 1** (FastICA, or Infomax/Picard). Remove the components that show the TMS-evoked muscle and decay pattern:
   - very large amplitude in roughly the first 5–20 ms;
   - a focal topography near the coil or under scalp muscles (temporalis, frontalis);
   - high trial-to-trial consistency;
   - broadband or high-frequency spectrum (muscle) or slow monotonic decay (decay artifact).
7. Interpolate the removed pulse window (for example cubic or linear) and apply band-pass (about 1–100 Hz) and notch (50/60 Hz) filters.
8. Remove the interpolated window again and replace it with a constant.
9. **ICA round 2.** Remove components for blinks, horizontal eye movements, persistent muscle, single-channel noise, and any residual TMS-evoked muscle or decay.
10. Interpolate the removed channels and the pulse window, re-reference (for example to the average), and baseline-correct.

## Practical cautions

- **Rank deficiency.** After channel removal, interpolation or average referencing, the data rank drops. Reduce the dimensionality with PCA, or run ICA before interpolation and re-referencing, so you don't get unstable or duplicated components.
- **Over-correction of early TEPs.** Muscle and neural responses in the first about 20 ms overlap in time and space and are both strongly time-locked. Removing "muscle" components can also remove genuine early TEP peaks (for example N15/P20 or P30). Interpret very early components cautiously, and report how much variance was removed.
- **ICA assumptions are imperfect for TEPs.** TMS-locked artifacts and evoked neural activity are not fully independent across trials. ICA works best when artifact and brain signal have clearly different topographies or time courses.
- **Component selection is a major source of variability.** Use explicit, pre-specified criteria, for example TESA's `tesa_compselect` thresholds plus visual verification. Report the number and type of components removed per round and per participant. Different pipelines can produce materially different TEPs from the same data.
- **ICA does not remove auditory or somatosensory evoked potentials.** These are spatially and temporally similar to genuine TEPs. Handle them experimentally with noise masking, foam spacers and a sham/control condition, not with ICA.
- **Alternatives and complements.** These include SOUND (for channel noise) combined with SSP-SIR (for TMS-evoked muscle artifact), or prevention at acquisition: coil angle, lower intensity, careful target choice and a thin coil spacer. Some groups use SSP-SIR or a dedicated muscle-model fitting step in place of ICA round 1.

## Recommendation

- **Default:** use **two rounds** if the raw TMS-locked data show large early (< 20 ms) muscle or decay artifacts. Round 1 removes those before filtering; round 2 removes ocular, tonic-muscle and noise artifacts after filtering.
- **One round only** if the early artifact is small after the pulse window is removed. Run it after filtering and target ocular, muscle and noise components.
- **Either way:** compare TEPs and topographies before and after each round, make sure early peaks aren't simply erased, and report the full procedure transparently.

Key methodological references for this approach include work by Rogasch and colleagues on two-round ICA for TMS-EEG and the TESA toolbox (2014, 2017), and methods reviews of TMS-EEG artifact removal (e.g. Hernandez-Pavon et al., 2023).
