# Stimulation intensity for left-DLPFC TMS-EEG: is 110% RMT appropriate?

## Short answer

**Not as the only criterion. I wouldn't confirm it as written.** 110% of resting motor threshold (RMT) is a common and safe dose for DLPFC rTMS. For a TMS-EEG study, though, a fixed %RMT does not ensure that each participant's DLPFC is stimulated effectively, or that the recorded TMS-evoked potentials (TEPs) are cortical rather than artifact. Current TMS-EEG practice is to use RMT only as a **starting point**. You then adjust intensity for each participant, coil position and coil orientation, using the early TEP read-out and the artifact profile at the DLPFC site.

The one exception is a study that is explicitly about the cortical response *at the dose your rTMS protocol delivers*. In that case keeping 110% RMT can be justified. You would still need to check and report the TEP quality and artifacts at that dose, and say clearly that the dose follows the rTMS convention rather than a TEP criterion.

## Why 110% RMT alone is a weak choice for TMS-EEG over DLPFC

1. **Motor threshold does not transfer to prefrontal cortex.** RMT measures corticospinal excitability at M1. It does not account for differences in scalp-to-cortex distance, gyral folding, or local excitability at the DLPFC target. Scalp-to-cortex distance differs between M1 and DLPFC, and it differs between people. So the same %RMT gives different cortical electric fields across participants. Some may get a weak or absent early TEP; others may get large artifacts.

2. **Evidence on TEP quality.** Work on real-time TEP monitoring (e.g., Casarotto et al., 2022, *J Neurosci Methods*) shows that MT-based intensities often give weak early responses outside M1. Adjusting intensity, position and orientation to get a clear early response (roughly ≥ 6–10 µV peak-to-peak in the first ~50 ms at electrodes near the target, averaged over about 20 trials) gives more reliable and interpretable TEPs.

3. **Artifacts at the DLPFC site.** Lateral prefrontal sites lie over or near the temporalis and frontalis muscles. 110% RMT can cause large TMS-evoked muscle artifacts in the first ~5–30 ms, which can hide early components. It can also cause scalp sensation and blinks. These increase the somatosensory and auditory evoked potentials that contaminate later TEP components (N100/P200). You may need to change the dose, coil angle or exact position to keep those artifacts manageable.

4. **The EEG cap changes the effective dose.** If RMT is measured without the cap (as is common in rTMS sessions), the cap and electrodes increase coil-to-scalp distance during TMS-EEG. That makes "110% RMT" a lower effective intensity at the cortex. If you keep an RMT-based dose, **measure RMT with the EEG cap on**, in the same session.

5. **Reporting guidelines** (e.g., Hernandez-Pavon et al., 2023, *Brain Stimulation*) recommend justifying the intensity, reporting it in several units (%MSO, %RMT, and ideally estimated E-field), and documenting artifact control and TEP quality. A fixed %RMT with no TEP check is increasingly questioned by reviewers.

Safety is not the problem here. Single-pulse or low-frequency TMS at 110% RMT is well within current safety guidelines (Rossi et al., 2021).

## Recommended approach

- **Targeting:** Use neuronavigation (MRI-based if possible; e.g., MNI coordinates or an individual connectivity-based target). Record coil position and orientation and keep them stable within the session.
- **RMT:** Measure RMT at the left M1 hotspot **with the EEG cap on**, using a standard method (e.g., an adaptive/PEST or relative-frequency method, 50 µV MEP criterion in a relaxed FDI).
- **Starting dose:** Begin at 110% RMT so the dose is comparable to your rTMS protocol.
- **Adjustment:** Watch the averaged response online (about 20 pulses). If the early response at the target is weak (< ~6–10 µV peak-to-peak within ~50 ms) or the muscle artifact is severe, adjust coil orientation and position first, then intensity. Stay within safety limits and the participant's comfort.
- **Sensory controls:** Play continuous masking noise built from the TMS click spectrum (with earplugs/earphones). Place a thin foam layer under the coil. Check whether the click is still audible. Consider a sensory/sham control condition.
- **Report:** final intensity in %MSO and %RMT for each participant (mean ± SD, range), number of participants in whom intensity was changed from 110% RMT, and, if possible, the estimated cortical E-field (e.g., from SimNIBS).

## Methods sentences

**Recommended version (TEP-guided intensity, anchored to RMT):**

> Resting motor threshold (RMT) was determined over the left M1 hotspot of the first dorsal interosseous muscle with the EEG cap in place, defined as the lowest intensity eliciting MEPs ≥ 50 µV in at least 5 of 10 consecutive trials. For left DLPFC stimulation, intensity was initially set to 110% RMT and then adjusted individually, together with coil position and orientation under neuronavigation, to obtain a clear early TMS-evoked potential (peak-to-peak amplitude ≥ [6–10] µV within 50 ms post-stimulus at electrodes near the target, averaged over ~20 trials) while minimising muscle and decay artifacts. The resulting intensity was [mean ± SD]% of maximum stimulator output ([mean ± SD]% RMT; range [x–y]%), corresponding to an estimated peak cortical electric field of [mean ± SD] V/m.

**Version if you deliberately keep a fixed 110% RMT (e.g., to probe the rTMS dose):**

> To match the intensity of our rTMS protocol, single TMS pulses were delivered to the left DLPFC at 110% of each participant's resting motor threshold (RMT), determined over the left M1 hotspot of the first dorsal interosseous muscle with the EEG cap in place as the lowest intensity eliciting MEPs ≥ 50 µV in at least 5 of 10 consecutive trials. This corresponded to [mean ± SD]% of maximum stimulator output (range [x–y]%). The presence of an early TMS-evoked response and the magnitude of muscle artifacts were verified online for each participant, and the estimated cortical electric field at the target was [mean ± SD] V/m.

## Assumptions

- Single-pulse TMS-EEG in healthy adults, figure-of-eight coil, biphasic pulses, neuronavigated left DLPFC target.
- Values in square brackets are placeholders to replace with your study's values.
- The TEP amplitude criterion is a commonly used guide, not a fixed standard. Pick it before data collection and report it.
