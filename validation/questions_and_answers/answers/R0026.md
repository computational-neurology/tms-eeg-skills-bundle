# TMS-EEG preprocessing order: 64 ch, 5 kHz, M1, BrainVision

## Assumptions
- Single-pulse TMS over M1, continuous recording (.vhdr/.vmrk/.eeg), with TMS triggers written as markers in the .vmrk file.
- The main goal is TEPs plus GMFA/LMFP, not immediate (<~10 ms) i-TEPs. If you care about the first few milliseconds, see the note at the end.
- Online reference is a single electrode, such as FCz. Any EMG/MEP or EOG channels are typed as non-EEG.
- You have a sham or sensory-control condition, or at least masking and perceptual ratings.
- All windows, cut-offs and thresholds below are **placeholders to set from your own data**, not universal values.

There is no single correct order. TESA, ARTIST, TMSEEG and SOUND/SSP-SIR place baseline, filtering and downsampling differently. What follows is a conservative default built on one rule: **handle the TMS pulse first, filter late, and check the data after every major step.**

---

## Ordered steps

| # | Step | One-line rationale |
|---|------|--------------------|
| 1 | **Load the .vhdr and set metadata**: channel types (EEG/EOG/EMG), 10-20 montage/electrode positions, the online reference, line frequency. Keep an untouched native-rate (5 kHz) copy. | Later steps assume these facts, and the native-rate copy is your reference for all pulse and early-latency checks. |
| 2 | **Extract and check TMS events** from the markers: count pulses per condition, check inter-pulse intervals, and overlay markers on the raw pulse artifact. | Duplicate, missing or shifted triggers blur TEPs and make early windows impossible to interpret. |
| 3 | **Inspect the pulse artifact at 5 kHz**: its duration, channel saturation or clipping, the decay/recharge tail, and early TMS-evoked muscle (mainly temporal channels near the coil). Use this to set the removal window. | Artifact width depends strongly on sampling rate and intensity, so the window must come from your data, not from a convention. |
| 4 | **Epoch around each pulse** with long, generous epochs (e.g., about −1000 to +1000 ms) and no filtering yet. | Long epochs keep edge effects from later filters and resampling away from the TEP window. |
| 5 | **Remove and interpolate the pulse window** (e.g., about −2 ms to the end of the visible artifact, often around +5 to +10 ms at M1), using linear or cubic interpolation. | Any filter or ICA applied across an unhandled pulse spreads ringing into the TEP, and that ringing cannot be removed later. |
| 6 | **Mark bad channels and grossly bad epochs** by visual review plus robust amplitude/variance criteria. Mark the channels but do not interpolate them yet. | Noisy or dead channels and extreme trials distort ICA and topographies. Interpolating before ICA creates rank problems. |
| 7 | **Downsample**, e.g., 5 kHz to about 1 kHz, with the software's anti-alias filter. Compare traces around the pulse before and after. | This cuts the cost of ICA while keeping enough resolution for N15/P30/N45. It is safe only now that the pulse is gone, because anti-alias filtering would otherwise smear it. |
| 8 | **Demean or apply a provisional baseline** (pre-pulse window, e.g., about −500 to −10 ms, free of earlier pulses). | This removes offsets so the ICA input is stable. The final baseline comes later. |
| 9 | **ICA (pass 1)** on good channels only. If needed, fit it on a separate copy that has a gentle high-pass. Remove only ocular, blink, eye-movement and non-stimulus-locked/background muscle components. | ICA handles stereotyped non-TMS artifacts well. Rejecting components that are clearly time-locked to the TMS pulse, or auditory/somatosensory components, risks deleting real TEP signal. |
| 10 | **Handle TMS-evoked muscle and channel noise** if they remain. At M1 this is often modest, but check temporal sites. Use SSP-SIR (inspecting the projected PCs) and/or SOUND, which needs valid electrode positions. Use ordinary ICA only for residual non-stimulus-locked artifacts. | Projection methods target early stimulus-locked muscle more selectively than ICA. Over-projecting flattens the true early response, so compare before and after at 0–60 ms. |
| 11 | **Apply analysis filters late**: a moderate low-pass (e.g., about 45–100 Hz, chosen for your endpoint) plus a 50/60 Hz notch if needed. Prefer gentle slopes, and avoid a 1 Hz high-pass on short epochs. | Filtering only after the sharp TMS transients are gone keeps ringing out of the early TEP. Baseline correction handles slow drift more safely than an early high-pass. |
| 12 | **Interpolate the marked bad channels** (spherical spline) or repair them with SOUND. | You need the full 64-channel montage for an unbiased average reference and topographies, and repairing after ICA avoids rank problems. |
| 13 | **Reject residual bad trials** using the same criteria for all conditions, and report the trials kept per condition. | Remaining artifacts inflate variance, and rejection that differs by condition biases comparisons. |
| 14 | **Re-reference to the common average across all EEG channels** (or a justified Laplacian/CSD). | The reference is part of the feature definition. Applying CAR before choosing ROI channels prevents falsely low LMFP. |
| 15 | **Apply the final baseline correction** (e.g., about −500 to −5 ms or −300 to −5 ms, if that window is quiet). | TEP amplitudes then start from a common pre-pulse level. |
| 16 | **Average the TEPs and compute GMFA and LMFP.** For LMFP, use a predefined ROI over the stimulated M1 (e.g., around C3/C4), selected only after full-channel referencing. | These are the target features. Fix the ROI and windows in advance so they are not chosen after seeing the data. |
| 17 | **QC report and processing log**: traces before and after pulse interpolation, native versus downsampled traces at 0–50 ms, bad-channel map, ICA/SSP-SIR component decisions, trials kept, butterfly plots and topographies, GMFA/LMFP for active versus control. | Most failures in TMS-EEG preprocessing are only visible in these plots, and they make the pipeline reproducible. |

## How this differs from published pipelines
- **TESA-style:** downsamples to about 1 kHz after pulse interpolation and before ICA, then ICA 1 → band-pass/notch → ICA 2, with the average reference and final baseline at the end. This is very close to the order above.
- **ARTIST:** downsamples early and filters before epoching. If you reproduce it, add explicit checks for aliasing and ringing.
- **TMSEEG / some recent studies:** downsample at the very end. This is reasonable if computing time is not an issue.
- **SOUND/SSP-SIR workflows:** some apply a 1 Hz high-pass just before SOUND, after pulse handling and ICA. This is not a "high-pass first" pipeline.

## M1-specific cautions
- **Sensory confounds:** N100/P200 are strongly shaped by click loudness and scalp sensation. Interpret late components only against a sham or sensory control, and collect separate ratings for sound, twitch, discomfort and pain. A well-matched sham can be subtracted, but un-subtracted late peaks are not purely cortical.
- **Muscle-twitch feedback:** at suprathreshold intensities, the hand-muscle twitch (MEP) sends somatosensory feedback that adds to later TEP components. Keep intensity matched across conditions, or model it.
- **Excitability claims:** TEP, GMFA and LMFP amplitudes are not pure measures of cortical excitability. Note also that ICA-based cleaning tends to make all TEP peaks smaller, so compare with care against ICA-free pipelines or normative values.
- **Early latencies:** anything within the first ~10–15 ms (the interpolated window plus the decay tail) needs proof that residual pulse, decay or muscle artifact is not driving it.
- **Immediate responses (i-TEPs):** if these are an endpoint, keep that analysis at the native 5 kHz rate. Justify the interpolation window explicitly, and report lead configuration, synchronization, recharge/decay and the control condition.
