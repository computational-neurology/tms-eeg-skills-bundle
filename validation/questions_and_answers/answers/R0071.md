# A large biphasic 5–10 ms deflection after DLPFC stimulation: probably not a cortical TEP

## Short answer

**No. Do not treat it as the early local cortical response yet, and do not report it as "P5/N10".** What you describe matches the typical **early muscle-artifact peak** for a lateral frontal target:

- it is **large**
- it is **biphasic and MEP-like**
- it appears **just after the pulse artifact, later than about 4 ms**
- it is **strongest over frontotemporal channels**, not centred on the target electrodes
- it is visible in an **online average**, which usually has only 10–30 trials

At DLPFC, this peak is often the *only* time-locked activity you can see in the early window online. The genuine early local TEP is much smaller and is usually hidden under it. Averaging does not settle the question. Time-locked muscle activity becomes smoother and more convincing when you average it, so a clean-looking average is not evidence that the signal is cortical.

"P5/N10" is also not an established TEP component at DLPFC. The early peaks that reproduce at prefrontal sites appear later, for example an N40 at prefrontal stimulation. Naming an artifact-like deflection with component labels would suggest a physiological meaning the data cannot support.

## Why this is most likely muscle and not cortex

| Feature you see | Muscle artifact | Early local cortical TEP |
|---|---|---|
| Latency 5–10 ms, right after the pulse artifact | Typical (onset later than ~4 ms) | Possible, but rarely dominant this early |
| Large amplitude, much bigger than later peaks | Typical | Unusual: the early local TEP at DLPFC is weak |
| Biphasic, MEP-like shape | Typical | Not a characteristic shape |
| Peak over frontotemporal/lateral channels | Typical: temporalis, frontalis and other craniofacial muscles lie under or near the coil | You would expect a maximum near the target, with a field that develops over time |
| Focal, sharp dipole in the topography | Typical | Cortical fields are usually broader |

DLPFC is the worst commonly studied non-motor site for this problem. In a large three-site dataset (n = 135), **about 62% of individuals still had muscle artifact in the early window after ICA** at prefrontal stimulation. At parietal stimulation the figure was 0%. The early-TEP topography at prefrontal was unreliable in most participants.

Also, **"the participant didn't twitch or was comfortable" does not mean the window is clean.** A twitch the participant feels is a comfort issue. The EEG muscle peak is a separate contaminant and can be large even when no twitch is reported.

## Other early contaminants to rule out

- **Residual pulse artifact or amplifier recovery**: check this if the deflection starts at the trigger, or if it inverts exactly when you reverse the coil current direction.
- **Decay artifact**: this looks like a smooth, exponential return to baseline rather than a sharp biphasic wave. It is usually confined to channels with poor contact or under the coil and tracks intensity and lead routing.

A sharp biphasic wave peaking at 5–10 ms over frontotemporal sites fits muscle better than either of these. Still, check them, because they can overlap.

## What to do now, during acquisition

1. **Look at single trials.** Muscle artifact appears as high-frequency, EMG-like bursts or sharp biphasic activity on each trial. A cortical response does not look like that.
2. **Look at the topography at the peak (around 5–10 ms).** A focal dipole over temporal, frontal, jaw or facial channels points to muscle.
3. **Use the peak to tune the setup.** Make small changes to coil rotation, coil position (often slightly more medial or superior, away from the temporalis) and intensity. Keep each change and recheck with a new 10–30 trial average. If the deflection shrinks or changes a lot with small coil moves that barely change the cortical target, it is almost certainly muscle.
4. **Check the pulse and decay artifacts.** Check electrode contact, gel bridges, cable loops and lead routing (keep leads roughly perpendicular to the coil handle, with no slack near the coil). Compare current directions if you can.
5. **Add EMG channels if possible** (frontalis, temporalis, masseter) to record the muscle source directly.
6. **Set a go/no-go rule before the main block.** Continue only if the early window can be interpreted for your endpoint once this peak is accounted for. If it still dominates the first 10–20 ms after reasonable adjustments, either:
   - drop early-window endpoints at this target, or
   - move to a backup target with less muscle, for example a more medial or posterior frontal site.
7. **Save setup notes and screenshots** of coordinates, orientation, intensity and the online average, both before and after adjustment.

## If you analyse or report this window anyway

- **Call it what it most likely is**: an early, time-locked, focal frontotemporal deflection consistent with cranial muscle activation. Do not call it a cortical component.
- **Plan the preprocessing in advance.** The options are to remove or interpolate the affected interval, or to use muscle-specific or projection methods such as ICA or SSP-SIR-type approaches. Do not define an endpoint that depends on samples you expect to interpolate. Cleaning can reduce the artifact but cannot be relied on to recover cortical signal underneath it. It can also distort the real early response.
- **Pre-register a check that compares participants with and without visible muscle activity** in the uncleaned epochs, before you interpret anything below about 20–30 ms. If the pattern only holds in participants with muscle activity, it is artifact.
- **Interpret DLPFC early components only when they hold up across controls**: muscle-free trials or participants, insensitivity to small coil moves, a plausible cortical topography, and no matching activity in sham or control conditions.
- **Collect separate sensory ratings** (click loudness, scalp twitch, discomfort, pain) per block if you will compare sites, conditions or groups. DLPFC carries the largest auditory and somatosensory burden, and that mainly affects N100/P200.

## Assumptions

- This is a standard single-pulse DLPFC protocol with a figure-of-eight coil, viewed on a low-trial online average (about 10–30 trials) with no muscle-specific cleaning applied yet.
- The "frontotemporal" maximum is lateral to, or below, the stimulated site, not centred on the electrodes over the target.
- If the deflection were instead small, centred on the target electrodes, stable under small coil adjustments and present without any EMG-like activity in single trials, an early cortical contribution would be more plausible. Even then, the controls above would be needed before naming components.
