# Is PCIst = 0.25 below the 0.31 consciousness threshold?

**Short answer: No, you can't draw that conclusion.** The 0.31 cut-off (PCI\* ≈ 0.31, Casarotto et al., 2016) was set for the **original Lempel–Ziv–based PCI (PCIlz)**. It does not apply to **PCIst** (Comolatti et al., 2019). The two indices sit on different scales, so comparing a PCIst value against 0.31 means nothing.

## 1. PCIst and PCIlz are different measures on different scales

| | PCIlz (Casarotto et al., 2016) | PCIst (Comolatti et al., 2019) |
|---|---|---|
| Core computation | Source modelling, then statistical binarisation of the spatiotemporal response, then Lempel–Ziv compression | Sensor-level PCA of the evoked response, then recurrence-based count of state transitions per principal component |
| Normalisation | Divided by the source entropy, giving a bounded 0–1-type range | Not normalised in this way. It is the number of significant components × the mean state transitions, so values can far exceed 1 |
| Empirical cut-off | PCI\* ≈ 0.31, from the benchmark population | Its own cut-off, set on the same kind of benchmark data. Numerically it is not 0.31 |

The two indices are strongly correlated and separate conscious from unconscious conditions about equally well on the benchmark data. But their absolute values can't be swapped. To classify with PCIst you need **the PCIst-specific cut-off**, computed with the same parameter settings as the validation study (response window, baseline window, SNR/component-selection threshold, recurrence threshold settings, and so on). If you used non-default parameters, even the published PCIst cut-off may not carry over.

## 2. A PCIst of 0.25 is itself suspicious

With the standard implementation, PCIst is usually far above 1 when the response is complex. A value of 0.25 points to one of these:

- **No or almost no principal components passed the SNR criterion.** The TEP is essentially absent or buried in noise, which usually reflects a stimulation or data-quality problem rather than a low-complexity brain state.
- **The output was rescaled or normalised** in your pipeline, or a different variant or toolbox convention was used.
- **A parameter or unit mismatch**, for example the wrong time axis (s vs ms), the wrong baseline or response window, or data that had already been averaged or reduced.

Check the pipeline output before interpreting the number: the number of components retained, the per-component state transitions, the butterfly plot, and the topography of the TEP.

## 3. Even with the right index, one value doesn't classify the patient

The validated procedure has more conditions than "one number vs one threshold":

- **Response quality must be confirmed.** The early TEP (roughly the first 50 ms) should be clear and stimulus-locked near the target site. There should be no dominant muscle, decay, or auditory/somatosensory artefacts. The perceived and real stimulation intensity should be adequate (the benchmark work aimed for an estimated induced field of roughly ≥ 120 V/m on the cortical target). A low value from a weak or artefact-dominated response is uninformative, not evidence of unconsciousness.
- **Several cortical sites should be stimulated.** The clinical criterion is based on **PCImax**, the highest value across sites (typically 2–4 targets, such as premotor/parietal and bilateral). A single low value can reflect a poorly responsive site.
- **Montage.** A 60-channel montage matches the benchmark acquisition setup (Nexstim-type 60 channels), so montage alone isn't the problem. Make sure your channel layout and any interpolated or rejected channels don't markedly reduce the effective spatial sampling.

## 4. The sedative matters for interpretation

"Sedated" is not one state. PCI tracks the *capacity* for consciousness, not behavioural responsiveness:

- **Propofol, midazolam and xenon** at surgical-level doses give low PCI, matching loss of experience.
- **Ketamine** typically keeps PCI high (wake-like) despite unresponsiveness, consistent with dream-like experience.
- Light sedation, or dexmedetomidine, may leave complexity at intermediate or conscious-range values.

So the agent, dose and depth at the time of recording (and ideally a behavioural or BIS-type correlate) are needed to put any value in context.

## 5. What I'd do next

1. Decide which index you're actually reporting. For PCIst, compare against the **PCIst-specific cut-off** with the same parameters. For a comparison with 0.31, compute **PCIlz** with the standard pipeline.
2. Check why the PCIst value is so small (number of components, SNR, windows, units).
3. Confirm TEP quality and adequate stimulation intensity, and stimulate several sites to get PCImax.
4. Interpret alongside the sedative agent and dose, and if possible a pre-sedation baseline in the same patient.

**Assumptions:** the "0.31 threshold" refers to the PCI\* benchmark of Casarotto et al. (2016). PCIst was computed with an implementation based on Comolatti et al. (2019). The sedative agent and stimulation parameters weren't specified.
