# TMS-EEG Preprocessing Outline: 64 ch, 5 kHz, M1, BrainVision

## Assumptions
- Single-pulse TMS over M1, recorded continuously, with hardware triggers in the BrainVision marker file (`.vmrk`).
- Goal: TEPs, possibly with GMFA/LMFP. Very early responses (i-TEPs, <~10 ms) are **not** the main endpoint. If they are, keep everything at 5 kHz and treat the early window separately.
- You have an active condition, and ideally a sham or sensory-control condition with per-participant sensory ratings.
- The default below follows a conservative MNE-Python style. TESA/EEGLAB uses the same logic with a slightly different order (see the end).
- All windows, cut-offs and thresholds are **placeholders** that you need to confirm on your own data. None of them are universal values.

---

## Ordered steps

| # | Step | One-line rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Load the raw `.vhdr`/`.eeg`/`.vmrk` at the native 5 kHz, set the montage (64-ch positions) and keep an untouched archive copy** | Every later decision about timing, pulse windows and interpolation depends on the native-rate data and correct channel locations, and you need the raw copy for provenance and QC. |
| 2 | **Check metadata: sampling rate, channel types (EEG/EOG/EMG), recording reference, annotations and condition labels** | TMS-EEG preprocessing depends on the order of steps, so a wrong event channel, a missing montage or swapped active/sham labels will quietly break everything downstream. |
| 3 | **Extract and verify the TMS pulse events (hardware triggers first) against the pulse transient** | Small timing errors smear the TEP and make early windows uninterpretable. Count pulses per condition, check inter-pulse intervals, and look for duplicate or missing triggers. |
| 4 | **Inspect the pulse, decay and recharge artifacts at 5 kHz (overlay pulse-locked trials, all channels)** | The pulse artifact's width and amplitude depend mainly on sampling rate and then on intensity. Measure the artifact window on your own data rather than copying a default. |
| 5 | **Mark bad channels (flat, noisy, bridged, saturated or lead-artifact channels), but don't interpolate yet** | Bad channels can dominate ICA and create false topographies. Excluding them now and repairing them after ICA avoids rank and dependency problems. |
| 6 | **Epoch around each pulse (e.g., placeholder −1000 to +1000 ms), with no filtering beforehand** | Epoching aligns trials for pulse handling. Wide epochs keep a clean baseline and protect the analysis window from edge effects in later filtering. |
| 7 | **Remove the pulse window and interpolate across it (placeholder about −2 to +5…10 ms, set from step 4), before any filtering, ICA or resampling** | Filtering or resampling across a pulse 4–5 orders of magnitude larger than the EEG causes ringing that spreads into the whole TEP window and can't be removed later. Use the smallest window you can defend. |
| 8 | **Downsample, if you want to, to a rate that suits your endpoint (e.g., 1–2.5 kHz for standard TEPs), using a resampler with anti-alias filtering** | 5 kHz is costly for ICA. Downsampling is safe only after the pulse is handled, and you must check that the anti-alias filter doesn't ring at the interpolation edges. Keep a 5 kHz copy for early-latency QC. |
| 9 | **First rejection of gross bad trials (amplitude, variance, high-frequency bursts, movement), then remove the trial mean (demean) or apply a provisional baseline** | Large non-stereotyped artifacts make ICA unstable. Removing them first gives a cleaner decomposition. |
| 10 | **ICA on good channels only, for ocular, blink and non-stimulus-locked/background muscle artifacts. If ICA needs it, fit on a separate high-passed or detrended copy and apply the result to the analysis data** | ICA works well for stereotyped physiological artifacts. Don't reject TMS-evoked muscle or clearly stimulus-locked components (true TEPs are stimulus-locked too). Don't reject auditory/somatosensory components by default. |
| 11 | **Deal with any remaining TMS-evoked muscle or decay residue (SSP-SIR, or a second ICA pass only with a stated target and before/after QC, or trial rejection)** | Over M1 the cranial-muscle artifact is usually smaller than over lateral frontal sites, but it depends on coil position and orientation. Projection methods keep more of the early signal than wider interpolation or ICA rejection. |
| 12 | **Repair bad channels now (spherical-spline interpolation, or SOUND if you use it)** | Doing this after ICA keeps rank-deficient, dependent channels out of the decomposition while giving a full 64-channel montage for referencing and topographies. |
| 13 | **Apply analysis filters late and gently: low-pass (placeholder about 45–80 Hz) and line-noise notch (50/60 Hz) if needed. Avoid a 1 Hz high-pass on short epochs** | By this point the sharp TMS transients are gone, so filtering won't smear them into the TEP. Gentle filters with wide transition bands limit ringing that can look like N15/P30. |
| 14 | **Re-reference to the common average across all EEG channels (or a justified Laplacian/CSD)** | The final reference must cover the full repaired channel set. For LMFP, choose the ROI channels only after this full-channel reference. |
| 15 | **Final baseline correction (placeholder e.g., −500…−300 to −10…−5 ms, free of earlier pulses or task events)** | This sets each epoch's pre-pulse level to zero so post-pulse amplitudes can be compared. Choose the window from your design and visual QC, not by convention. |
| 16 | **Final trial QC and per-condition trial counts** | Residual artifacts can show up after filtering or ICA, and losing trials unevenly across active and sham conditions biases the comparison. |
| 17 | **Average TEPs per condition and compute GMFA and LMFP (ROI over/around left/right M1, e.g., C3/C1/CP3-type channels)** | These summarize the global and local response for the stats. Compute them from the same pipeline output for every condition. |
| 18 | **QC report and processing log** | Plot raw vs. interpolated traces around the pulse, the bad-channel map, ICA components kept or removed, spectra before and after filtering, the butterfly plot with topographies, GMFA/LMFP, and active-vs-sham artifact balance. Log every parameter so the pipeline is reproducible. |

## How this differs from published pipelines
- **TESA-style:** channel rejection → epochs → baseline → pulse removal/interpolation → downsample → bad trials → ICA 1 → band-pass/notch → ICA 2 → channel interpolation → average reference → final baseline. Its logic matches the outline above, but filtering is earlier (between the two ICA passes).
- **ARTIST:** downsamples early (straight after pulse removal). That is source-specific and needs anti-alias QC if you copy it.
- **TMSEEG / some recent pipelines:** downsample last.
- **SOUND/SSP-SIR workflows:** ocular/background ICA → SOUND (also repairs noisy channels) → SSP-SIR for TMS-evoked muscle → late filtering, downsampling and baseline. This is not a "high-pass first" approach.
- Where baseline, downsampling, filtering and re-referencing go varies between methods. The default here puts analysis filtering and referencing late, after the TMS-specific cleaning.

## M1-specific caveats
- **MEPs and reafference:** at suprathreshold intensity, twitches in the hand muscle feed somatosensory input back to the cortex, which adds to later TEP components. Record EMG, and consider MEP amplitude as a covariate or stimulate below threshold if your question allows.
- **Auditory and somatosensory confounds:** N100/P200 are strongly shaped by click loudness and scalp sensation. Without a matched sham or sensory control and sensory ratings, don't read late components as purely cortical, and don't compare TEP amplitude across sites or groups without them.
- **ICA shrinks TEPs:** ICA-based cleaning systematically reduces TEP amplitudes across peaks, so compare only with pipelines processed the same way.
- **Early does not mean clean:** components before about 60 ms are more site-specific but can still carry residual pulse, decay or muscle artifact. Check which early windows overlap interpolated or projected data.
- **Interpretation:** TEP, GMFA and LMFP are scalp-level responses shaped by cortical, network, sensory, muscle and preprocessing factors. They are not pure measures of cortical excitability.
- **i-TEPs:** if you care about the first few milliseconds, analyze at 5 kHz. Report the exact pulse window, lead configuration and synchronization, and show recharge/decay and muscle QC against a control condition.
