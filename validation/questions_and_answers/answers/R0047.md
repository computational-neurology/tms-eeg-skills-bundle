# TMS-EEG preprocessing pipeline (64 ch, 5 kHz, M1 stimulation, BrainVision)

**Assumptions:** single-pulse (or paired-pulse) TMS over M1, roughly 100 to 200 trials per condition, a TMS-compatible amplifier (DC or high-sampling-rate recording that does not saturate for long), and a goal of TMS-evoked potentials (TEPs) and possibly TMS-related oscillations. The order follows the widely used two-round ICA approach (TESA/EEGLAB style, Rogasch et al., 2017). The same logic carries over to FieldTrip or MNE-Python. Alternatives are listed after the main pipeline.

## Ordered steps

1. **Import the .vhdr/.vmrk/.eeg set and attach 10–20 channel locations.** You need correct channel positions for interpolation, ICA topographies and re-referencing. Make sure the online reference channel is handled explicitly (for example, add it back as a zero channel if it is FCz).

2. **Check the TMS markers against the artifact itself.** Marker latency can jitter or drift relative to the actual pulse. Align epochs to the pulse peak, or confirm the marker is accurate to within one sample (0.2 ms at 5 kHz), because every later time window depends on t = 0.

3. **Look at the continuous data and flag bad or saturated channels (do not interpolate yet).** Channels with poor contact, bridging, or long amplifier saturation would corrupt ICA and the average reference. Log them now and remove them before ICA.

4. **Epoch around the pulse with wide windows (for example −1000 to +1000 ms).** Long epochs keep filter edge effects and ICA training data away from the analysis window (roughly −200 to +500 ms).

5. **Demean or baseline-correct each epoch (for example −500 to −10 ms).** Removing DC offsets improves ICA decomposition and visual inspection. Keep the baseline window clear of the pulse.

6. **Remove the TMS pulse artifact window (for example −2 to +10/15 ms; adjust after looking at the data).** The pulse and the amplifier recovery are non-neural, huge, and cannot be corrected. At 5 kHz you can set the window precisely from the grand-average artifact shape. Also remove a capacitor-recharge artifact if your stimulator produces one at a fixed delay.

7. **Interpolate across the removed window (cubic or linear) temporarily.** Filtering or resampling over a step discontinuity causes ringing that spreads the artifact into neighbouring data.

8. **Downsample from 5 kHz to 1 kHz (or 1–2 kHz) with an anti-aliasing filter.** This cuts computation time for ICA and later steps while keeping all TEP-relevant frequencies. Do it only after the pulse window has been bridged.

9. **Reject bad trials (amplitude or joint-probability thresholds plus visual check).** Trials with large movement, coil contact or ocular bursts would dominate the ICA solution and the average. Record how many trials you reject per condition and participant.

10. **Replace the interpolated window with zeros or constants again before ICA.** Interpolated segments are artificial, and including them biases the ICA unmixing.

11. **ICA round 1 (FastICA or Infomax), focused on the large TMS-evoked muscle and decay artifacts.** Scalp and temporalis muscle activation (about 5–20 ms) and electrode-movement decay are orders of magnitude larger than TEPs. Isolating them first gives a cleaner second decomposition. M1 targets usually evoke less muscle artifact than lateral or frontal sites, so this round can be conservative. Reject only components with clear early-latency, high-amplitude, lateralized topographies.

12. **Re-interpolate the pulse window, then band-pass filter (for example 1–100 Hz, zero-phase) and notch or line-noise filter (50/60 Hz).** This removes slow drifts and line noise before the second decomposition. A 1 Hz high-pass improves ICA, but if slow late components such as the N100 or P180 matter, consider a lower cutoff or apply the ICA weights to less-filtered data.

13. **Remove the interpolated window again and run ICA round 2.** This round targets the remaining stereotyped artifacts: blinks, eye movements, persistent muscle, electrode noise, ECG, and residual decay. Classify components by topography, time course and spectrum (ICLabel or TESA's component classifier can help, followed by manual confirmation).

14. **Interpolate the pulse window a final time.** This restores continuity for plotting and frequency analysis. Report the interpolated window and do not interpret TEP peaks inside it.

15. **Spherically interpolate the bad channels removed in step 3.** This restores the full montage, which a symmetric average reference and group-level topographic statistics require.

16. **Re-reference to the common average.** The average reference reduces the dependence of TEP topographies on reference location and is the most common convention for comparability. Account for rank deficiency (interpolated channels plus average reference) in any later ICA or source analysis.

17. **Final baseline correction (for example −500 to −10 ms) and trial averaging per condition.** This gives clean TEPs (for example N15, P30, N45, P60, N100, P180 over M1). Optionally also run time–frequency analysis on single trials.

18. **Quality control: butterfly plots, global mean field power (GMFP), topographies, and a comparison with a sham or control condition.** These confirm that artifact removal did not distort the signal. M1 TEPs also contain sensory confounds that preprocessing cannot remove, so judge components against control data.

## M1-specific notes

- **Peripheral sensory input:** suprathreshold M1 stimulation produces MEPs and a muscle twitch, so re-afferent somatosensory input contributes to later TEPs (about 60 ms onward). Record EMG to quantify MEPs, and consider subthreshold intensities or matched control stimulation.
- **Auditory and somatosensory artifacts:** the coil click and scalp sensation evoke N100/P200-like responses. Preprocessing cannot remove these. Rely on noise masking, a foam spacer, and sham or realistic-sham conditions.
- **Order sensitivity:** filter, interpolation and ICA choices change early TEP peaks. Pre-register the pipeline, keep it the same across conditions and participants, and report every parameter (windows, filters, components removed per participant).

## Alternatives and variants

- **SOUND + SSP-SIR (Mutanen et al.):** SOUND suppresses channel-specific noise. SSP-SIR suppresses TMS-evoked muscle artifacts by spatial projection, which can be gentler than ICA for heavy early muscle artifacts.
- **ARTIST (Wu et al., 2018):** a fully automated pipeline built around the same artifact categories, useful for large datasets.
- **Exponential-decay fitting (TESA) or detrending:** an option for slow electrode or amplifier decay artifacts instead of ICA round 1.
- **Saturation-free systems with short recovery:** if the amplifier recovers within about 5 ms, a shorter removal window (for example −1 to +6 ms) keeps more early-latency data.
