# TMS-EEG preprocessing pipeline (64 ch, 5 kHz, M1, BrainVision)

**Assumptions:** single-pulse TMS over M1. Event markers for the pulse are in the `.vmrk` file. The amplifier is DC-coupled or has a wide bandwidth and does not saturate for long, so pulse-artifact recovery takes only a few ms. EMG (e.g. FDI/APB) is recorded either on auxiliary channels or as a separate recording. There are at least about 100 trials per condition. The order below follows the widely used two-round ICA approach (TESA-style). Where steps can be swapped for alternatives, this is noted.

## Ordered steps

| # | Step | Typical settings | One-line rationale |
|---|------|------------------|--------------------|
| 1 | **Import the `.vhdr`/`.vmrk`/`.eeg` files, assign channel locations, check the event markers** | Confirm one marker per pulse; check inter-pulse intervals; look for marker jitter against the artifact peak | Every later step is time-locked to the pulse, so marker errors propagate everywhere. |
| 2 | **Separate the EMG/auxiliary channels and extract MEPs** | Peak-to-peak MEP at 15–50 ms; flag trials with pre-activation (EMG RMS in the 100 ms before the pulse) | MEPs document stimulation consistency and pre-activation. They also let you check or covary for reafferent somatosensory input, which contaminates M1 TEPs. |
| 3 | **Inspect the continuous data and note bad channels** (don't remove them yet) | Look for flat, bridged or very noisy channels and channels under the coil that saturate | Knowing which channels are bad early keeps them from dominating epoch rejection and ICA. |
| 4 | **Epoch around the pulse** | e.g. −1000 to +1000 ms | Epoching *before* any filtering stops the very large pulse artifact from smearing through the continuous data via filter ringing. |
| 5 | **Demean or baseline-correct** | Whole-epoch mean or pre-pulse window (e.g. −500 to −10 ms) | Removes DC offsets so that later interpolation and ICA are not biased by channel offsets. |
| 6 | **Remove the TMS pulse artifact window** | Cut about −2 to +10 ms (check the end point in a full-rate butterfly plot; with a fast-recovering amp it can be shorter, 5–8 ms). Also cut the coil recharge artifact if it falls inside the epoch, or move the recharge delay beyond your analysis window | The pulse artifact is orders of magnitude larger than neural signal and has no physiological content. |
| 7 | **Temporarily interpolate the removed window** | Cubic (or linear) interpolation from ±a few ms of surrounding data | Bridges the gap so that the next step's anti-alias filter does not ring on a step discontinuity. |
| 8 | **Downsample** | 5000 → 1000 Hz (or 2000 Hz if you need >200 Hz content) | Cuts compute and ICA memory load. It is safe only now that the pulse artifact is gone, because the anti-alias low-pass would otherwise spread the artifact. |
| 9 | **Reject bad trials and remove bad channels** | Visual review plus automated z-score/kurtosis/amplitude criteria; exclude MEP-outlier or pre-activation trials as you pre-specified | Large non-stereotyped artifacts (movement, electrode pops) violate ICA assumptions and degrade component separation. |
| 10 | **Replace the interpolated window with constant values (e.g. zeros), then run ICA round 1** | FastICA/Infomax; remove only components with the large, early (<~30–50 ms) TMS-evoked muscle artifact and the electrode-decay artifact | These artifacts are far larger than everything else and need removing *before* filtering, because high-pass filtering a decay artifact distorts it and spreads it in time. Interpolated data would add rank-deficient, non-physiological content to the ICA. |
| 11 | **Re-interpolate the pulse window** | Same as step 7 | Filters need continuous data, so the gap has to be bridged again before filtering. |
| 12 | **Band-pass and line-noise filter** | Zero-phase Butterworth, e.g. 1–100 Hz (0.1–1 Hz high-pass if you analyse late components); notch or band-stop at 48–52 Hz (or 58–62 Hz) | Removes slow drift and high-frequency noise. Doing it after the big artifacts are removed avoids filter-induced distortion. |
| 13 | **ICA round 2** (after removing the interpolated window again) | Remove blinks, saccades, ECG, persistent scalp muscle, single-channel noise and any residual decay | Removes the remaining physiological and non-physiological artifacts, which are now separable because the dominant TMS artifacts are gone. |
| 14 | **Optional: SOUND and/or SSP-SIR** (alternatives to or supplements for steps 10 and 13) | SOUND for channel-specific noise; SSP-SIR for residual TMS-evoked muscle activity | These are well-validated alternatives when ICA cannot cleanly separate muscle activity (less common for M1 than for lateral targets, but it happens). |
| 15 | **Interpolate removed channels** | Spherical spline | Restores the full montage so that the average reference and topographies are not biased by missing sites. |
| 16 | **Re-reference to the common average** | Average of all 64 channels | Gives a reference-independent spatial representation that suits 64-channel coverage and later source analysis. |
| 17 | **Final baseline correction** | e.g. −500 to −10 ms (exclude the pre-pulse edge of the cut window) | Aligns the trials so that TEP amplitudes (N15, P30, N45, P60, N100, P180) are measured relative to pre-stimulus activity. |
| 18 | **Final handling of the pulse window** | Leave it interpolated but mark it, or set it to NaN; don't interpret data inside it | Peaks inside the reconstructed window are artificial and must not be read as TEPs. |
| 19 | **Average and run quality checks** | Butterfly plots, GMFP, topographies; compare with sham or sensory-control conditions | Confirms that the artifacts are gone and separates true cortical TEPs from auditory/somatosensory evoked potentials (the N100–P200 complex is especially at risk). |

## Practical notes

- **Keep a full-rate copy** of the epoched raw data so you can check the artifact duration and the TMS-evoked muscle amplitude at 5 kHz.
- **Never filter across the pulse discontinuity.** Every filtering step must come after the window has been removed and bridged.
- **Be conservative with ICA.** Remove only components with a clear artifact signature (time course, topography, spectrum), and report how many you removed per participant. Over-removal can strip out early TEPs near the coil.
- **M1-specific confound.** The MEP-related reafferent input arrives about 40–60 ms after the pulse and can inflate the P60/N100. Consider subthreshold intensity, sorting trials by MEP amplitude, or including MEP size as a covariate.
- **Report everything** in line with current TMS-EEG reporting checklists: cut windows, interpolation method, filter type/order/cut-offs, ICA algorithm and rejection criteria, trials and channels removed, reference, and baseline window.
