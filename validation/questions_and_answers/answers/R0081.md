# ICA for early TMS-EEG artifacts over F3: fix the recording first

## Short answer

No ICA settings will repair the problem you describe. Recording all 30 participants as they are is the riskiest choice in this plan. Spend one or two more pilot sessions fixing the acquisition, then use ICA to clean up what is left. Some lab time now is much cheaper than a dataset whose first 20–30 ms cannot be interpreted.

Why ICA cannot fix it:

1. **Saturated channels have lost their data.** When an amplifier clips, the true signal is gone, and no unmixing method can bring it back. Clipping is also non-linear, so it breaks ICA's core assumption: a fixed, linear mixture of independent sources. Saturated channels often take extra time to recover after the clipping ends, which spreads the damage further.
2. **Large decay artifacts break ICA's assumptions.** These usually come from electrode polarization, electrode or lead movement, or a poor electrode–skin interface. Their amplitude, time course and topography change from trial to trial and from channel to channel. ICA works best when an artifact has a stable spatial map. A variable decay gets spread over several components, and each of those also carries brain signal. Removing them distorts or removes the early TEP (around N15, P30 and N45 over F3), which is the part you care about.
3. **Very large artifacts take over the decomposition.** If the artifact is orders of magnitude bigger than the EEG, ICA uses most of its degrees of freedom on the artifact. The neural components come out poorly separated, and that affects later artifact removal too, including blinks and muscle.
4. **The result depends on the pipeline.** Different cleaning pipelines produce visibly different early TEPs from the same data. If the raw data are dominated by artifact, your conclusions about early responses will depend on analysis choices, and reviewers will notice.

## What to do in the lab first (usually 1–2 pilot sessions)

Aim for this: no saturated channels, and the signal back near baseline within about 5–10 ms of the pulse on the average.

- **Impedance:** keep all electrodes below 5 kΩ, especially under and next to the coil. Abrade the skin thoroughly. Uneven or high impedance is the main cause of long decay artifacts.
- **Electrodes:** use TMS-compatible, low-profile Ag/AgCl electrodes (C-ring or slit), so eddy-current heating and polarization stay low.
- **Lead wires:** run them at right angles to the coil handle, away from the coil and not looped. Twist or bundle them and fix them in place. Wire movement and loops pick up the pulse and cause decays.
- **Coil contact:** don't press the coil hard on the electrodes. A thin spacer helps, and so does keeping the coil from rocking on the cap. Pressure on electrodes causes movement-related decays.
- **Amplifier:** check that you use a TMS-compatible amplifier in an input range that does not saturate, with DC coupling or a very low high-pass filter. Sample at 5 kHz or more (preferably about 20 kHz) so you can define the pulse and its recovery accurately. If your system has sample-and-hold or a fast-recovery mode, use it.
- **Coil orientation and intensity:** over F3, a small change in coil angle or intensity can noticeably reduce cranial muscle activation and electrode artifacts. If possible, check the TEP online with real-time averaging, adjust, and record the final parameters.
- **Masking:** keep noise masking and a foam spacer in place. Sensory confounds are a separate issue, but they matter for interpreting the 30–200 ms range.

Set a per-session quality check before collecting real data, for example: "no channel saturated for more than X ms after the pulse; average signal at 10–15 ms below Y µV". Fix or re-prepare the setup until a session passes.

## Recommended ICA approach (after acquisition is fixed)

Assumptions: about 64 channels, an EEGLAB/TESA-style pipeline, single-pulse TMS over F3, about 100–150 trials per condition, and 1-s epochs on each side of the pulse.

### Preprocessing before ICA

| Step | Setting |
|---|---|
| Epoch | −1000 to +1000 ms around the pulse |
| Remove the pulse | Replace data from about −2 to +6–12 ms (set the end from your own data, at the point where all channels are back in range) with constant values. Interpolate it only after ICA. |
| Channels | Remove channels that saturate or are noisy in many trials before ICA. Interpolate them after ICA. Never pass clipped data to ICA. |
| Downsampling | To 1000 Hz, after the pulse has been removed, with proper anti-alias filtering |
| Baseline | Demean over the whole epoch, not a pre-stimulus baseline. Baseline-corrected epochs give ICA a worse fit. |
| Filtering | Don't filter across the pulse interval before ICA round 1, because it causes ringing. Filter only after the pulse has been removed and interpolated. |
| Rank | Reduce dimensions (PCA) to the true data rank: channels minus removed channels, minus 1 if average-referenced. Rank deficiency produces unstable or "ghost" components. |

### Two-round ICA (the TESA approach)

**Round 1: large TMS-evoked artifacts (decay and early muscle)**
- Algorithm: FastICA, symmetric approach, tanh nonlinearity. Fix the random seed so results can be reproduced.
- Input: unfiltered epochs with the pulse removed, before the band-pass filter.
- Component selection: only components with a very large early amplitude relative to the rest of the epoch. TESA-style automatic rule: mean absolute amplitude in about 11–30 ms that is 8 or more times the rest of the epoch. Check every rejection by hand. Remove as few components as possible.

**Between rounds:** interpolate the pulse window (cubic), band-pass about 1–100 Hz, apply a line-noise notch, and reject bad trials.

**Round 2: everything else**
- FastICA again (Infomax/extended Infomax or AMICA are reasonable alternatives), on filtered data.
- Remove blinks, eye movements, ECG, persistent muscle and electrode noise.
- Leave TMS-locked components with a plausible cortical topography and time course.

### Data amount

ICA needs roughly k·n² samples, with k ≥ 20 and n the number of channels. For 63 channels that is about 80,000 samples, or about 80 one-second epochs at 1 kHz. Your trial counts should be enough, but rejected trials and a reduced rank lower the effective amount.

### Alternatives and checks for the early window

- **Exponential decay fitting** (single or double exponential per channel and trial, subtracted before ICA) often deals with residual decays better than ICA does.
- **SOUND** (channel noise) and **SSP-SIR** (TMS-evoked muscle) are well-established options to compare with ICA round 1.
- **Sensitivity analysis:** process a subset with two pipelines (for example ICA-only vs. decay fitting + ICA). Report early TEP components only if they hold up across both.
- **Transparency:** preregister the component-rejection rules, do selection blind to condition, and report the number of components removed and the variance they explained per participant.
- **Stability:** check that components are reliable across ICA runs (ICASSO/RELICA-style repetitions) for at least a subset.

## Bottom line

The ICA settings above are a sound standard. But if you record the current setup, you will probably lose 0–20 ms entirely, and 20–40 ms will depend on the pipeline. Plan to fix the acquisition first. If you truly cannot, limit your interpretation to later TEP components (N100 onward), state that in your preregistration, and still run the sensitivity analyses above.
