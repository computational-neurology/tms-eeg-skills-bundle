# TMS-EEG preprocessing pipeline: 64 ch, 5 kHz, M1 stimulation (BrainVision)

## Assumptions
- Single-pulse TMS over M1, with a stimulus marker for each pulse in the `.vmrk` file.
- The amplifier was not saturated for long (a DC-coupled or high-range amplifier with a 5 kHz sampling rate). The pulse artifact lasts roughly 2–10 ms.
- Mains frequency is 50 Hz. Use 60 Hz instead if you recorded in the Americas.
- Any EMG/MEP channels are stored in the same file.
- The steps follow a TESA/FieldTrip/MNE-style "two-round ICA" pipeline (Rogasch et al., 2017). Other options are listed at the end.

## Ordered steps

| # | Step | One-line rationale |
|---|------|--------------------|
| 1 | **Import the `.vhdr/.vmrk/.eeg` files. Add the 10-10 channel locations. Split off EMG/EOG channels** (and store MEP amplitudes per trial). | You need correct geometry for interpolation and source/topography work, and non-EEG channels must not enter the EEG reference or ICA. |
| 2 | **Check the TMS markers against the actual pulse artifact.** Re-align events to the artifact onset if they jitter, and drop duplicate or missing markers. | Even sub-millisecond misalignment smears the early TEP (N15/P30) and spoils artifact cutting. |
| 3 | **Do a first visual pass of the continuous data.** Flag flat, bridged or very noisy channels and gross non-stimulation artifacts (but do not interpolate yet). | Bad channels distort the average reference, ICA and later interpolation. Interpolation is postponed until the artifacts have been cleaned. |
| 4 | **Epoch around each pulse** (e.g., −1000 to +1000 ms) **on the unfiltered data**, then demean. | Long epochs leave room for filter edges and baseline. Filtering before the pulse is removed causes ringing that spreads the huge pulse artifact into the TEP. |
| 5 | **Remove the TMS pulse artifact** (typically −2 to +10 ms; adjust by inspecting the average over all channels). Also remove any **recharge artifact** if the capacitor recharge falls inside the epoch. | The pulse is non-physiological and several orders of magnitude larger than the EEG. Every later step (filtering, ICA, re-referencing) would spread it. |
| 6 | **Temporarily interpolate the removed window** (cubic or linear) and **downsample to 1000 Hz** (or 1–2 kHz), using the anti-aliasing filter. | Bridging the gap stops the anti-alias filter from ringing at the cut edges. Downsampling cuts ICA computation and memory costs by a factor of 5 without losing TEP-relevant bandwidth. |
| 7 | **Put the removed window back to zero/NaN** (i.e., undo the interpolation before ICA). | Interpolated samples are artificial and rank-deficient, so they would bias ICA decompositions. |
| 8 | **Reject bad trials** (visual and/or z-score/kurtosis thresholds) and confirm which channels are bad. | Single extreme trials (movement, large blinks during the pulse, coil contact) dominate ICA and the average. |
| 9 | **ICA round 1 (e.g., FastICA) on the unfiltered epochs** to remove large **TMS-evoked muscle** and **electrode-decay** components (very high amplitude in the first ~50 ms, with a focal lateral topography near the coil). | These artifacts are tens to hundreds of µV. They must be removed before filtering, or a high-pass filter will spread them into later latencies. |
| 10 | **Re-interpolate the removed window, band-pass filter (e.g., 1–100 Hz, zero-phase, 4th-order Butterworth) and notch at 48–52 Hz**, then remove the window again. | Filtering takes out slow drift and line/high-frequency noise for the second ICA. It is done only after the big artifacts are gone, to limit ringing and smearing. |
| 11 | **ICA round 2** to remove **blinks, eye movements, residual muscle, ECG, persistent line noise and remaining decay**. Use a component classifier (TESA `compselect`, ICLabel) plus manual review. | It cleans the remaining physiological and non-physiological sources without touching the early TEP. Keep removals conservative, because over-cleaning removes real neural signal. |
| 12 | **Interpolate the bad channels** (spherical spline) that were removed earlier. | It restores a complete, uniform montage, which the average reference and group-level topographies need. |
| 13 | **Re-reference to the common average.** | This is the standard TMS-EEG reference. It removes the dependency on the online reference, which is often near the stimulated area. |
| 14 | **Interpolate the removed pulse window for display only** (or leave it blank). Report the removed interval explicitly. | It avoids implying that data exist in that window, while still allowing continuous plots and time-frequency analysis. |
| 15 | **Baseline-correct** (e.g., −500 to −10 ms or −200 to −5 ms). | It removes residual offsets so that post-stimulus deflections are measured relative to the pre-pulse state. |
| 16 | **Average and run QC.** Check the butterfly plot, GMFP and topographies of N15, P30, N45, P60, N100 and P180. Compare against a **sham/realistic-sham or sensory-control condition**, and report the trials and components retained. | It confirms that a plausible M1 TEP is present and that late components (N100/P180) are not dominated by auditory or somatosensory evoked potentials. |

## Important notes
- **Sensory (auditory/somatosensory) evoked potentials are not removed by ICA.** Manage them at acquisition (noise masking, foam spacer) and analysis (sham or control subtraction, or comparison) stages (Hernandez-Pavon et al., 2023).
- **Alternatives to steps 9–11:** SSP-SIR for TMS-evoked muscle artifacts (Mutanen et al., 2016), SOUND for channel noise (Mutanen et al., 2018), or a fully automated pipeline such as ARTIST (Wu et al., 2018). Pick one approach in advance and report it, because the choice of pipeline measurably changes early TEPs.
- **The window lengths above (artifact cut, epoch, baseline, filter cutoffs) are defaults.** Adjust them after inspecting your grand-average data, and keep them identical across conditions and participants.
- **For reproducibility, save intermediate files and record removed components and trials** (ICA is not deterministic unless you fix the random seed).
