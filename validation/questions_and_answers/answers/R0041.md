# Online checks during a TMS-EEG session: go/no-go criteria

## Assumptions
- Single-pulse TMS-EEG over cortex (e.g., M1, DLPFC, parietal), with a TMS-compatible amplifier (sample-and-hold or wide dynamic range, DC or very low high-pass, sampling rate ≥ 5 kHz).
- Neuronavigation is available, plus noise masking and a foam spacer or thin layer under the coil.
- You can view a real-time average of a small batch of trials, as in the rt-TEP approach of Casarotto et al. (2022). If you can't, approximate it with a quick online average in the acquisition software.
- The numbers below are practical lab thresholds, not universal standards. Change them for your montage, reference, target and population, and write them into your protocol before data collection.

---

## 1. Before the first stimulation block (gate 0)

| Check | GO | NO-GO / fix first |
|---|---|---|
| Electrode impedances | All channels < 5 kΩ (or within the manufacturer's spec for active systems), especially under and around the coil | Channels near the coil > 5–10 kΩ. Re-prep them, because high impedance makes decay artifacts and recharge artifacts much worse |
| Raw resting EEG | Clean alpha with eyes closed, no flat or railing channels, line noise not dominant | Flat or saturated channels, large drifts, a bridged-looking montage |
| Amplifier settings | Correct sampling rate, range and filter settings. DC or ≤ 0.1 Hz high-pass. No online notch that would hide problems | Wrong range (saturates on the pulse) or aggressive online filters |
| Triggers | One marker per pulse, correct codes, stable latency (a jitter check on a test pulse, ideally < 1 sample at the acquisition rate) | Missing, duplicated or jittering markers |
| Lead routing | Electrode cables run away from the coil and orthogonal to the coil handle, fixed in place, not looped | Cables under the coil or moving with it |
| Masking | Noise level set so the participant reports they can't hear the click (or barely can) at the planned intensity. Stays under the safe-volume limit (roughly ≤ 90 dB SPL) | Participant clearly hears the click at a tolerable noise volume. Fix the noise spectrum, add earmuffs or foam, or add a spacer layer |
| Somatosensory | Participant rates scalp sensation and discomfort as low (e.g., ≤ 3/10 on a 0–10 scale), with no twitching of facial or scalp muscles | Strong tapping or muscle twitch. Rotate or move the coil, or lower intensity |

---

## 2. Online TEP quality check: the main go/no-go (gate 1)

Deliver a short test block of about 20–30 pulses at the planned intensity and target. Check the online average: baseline −100 to −5 ms, pulse window interpolated or blanked to about 5–8 ms, average reference or reference as planned, and a light low-pass filter for display only.

### 2a. Artifact checks (first ~15 ms)

| Check | GO | NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| Amplifier saturation / recovery | Signal returns to baseline within ~5–8 ms after the pulse on all channels | Railing or slow recovery past ~10 ms on channels near the coil |
| Muscle artifact | Little or no biphasic high-amplitude deflection at 5–15 ms. As a rough rule, early peak-to-peak < ~50 µV near the coil and not far bigger than later components | Large (often > 50–100 µV) sharp biphasic waves at 5–15 ms over temporal or frontal muscles. Rotate the coil, shift it medially or away from the muscle, or lower intensity. If cleanup ICA would have to remove most of the early response, it's a no-go |
| Decay artifact | No slow exponential drift lasting tens of ms in individual channels | Large slow decays on specific channels. Fix their impedance, contact and cable position |
| Recharge artifact | Recharge timed away from the analysis window (e.g., delayed recharge set past ~500 ms or at the end of the epoch) | Recharge spike inside the TEP window |

### 2b. Is there a genuine early cortical response? (~15–50 ms)

| Check | GO | NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| Early TEP amplitude | A clear, reproducible early response in the channels under or near the coil. A common working criterion (rt-TEP practice): peak-to-peak ≥ ~6–10 µV in the first ~50 ms, distinguishable from baseline noise (e.g., > 3× pre-stimulus SD) | Flat or noise-level early response. Change angle and orientation (usually in 15–45° steps), move the target by 0.5–1 cm, or raise intensity, then test again |
| Topography | Maximum near the stimulated site at early latencies, then spreads over time | Early maximum far from the target, or a symmetric, broad, vertex-centred pattern. That suggests a sensory or artifactual origin, not a local cortical one |
| Reproducibility | Split-half averages (odd vs even trials) look alike in latency, polarity and topography | Split halves disagree, meaning the response is mostly noise |

### 2c. Is the response contaminated by sensory input? (~80–250 ms)

| Check | GO | NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| Auditory / multisensory evoked potential | No dominant vertex N100–P180 (Cz/FCz) that's larger than the early local response. Participant reports no audible click | A large vertex N100/P200 that dominates the waveform, especially with a weak early local response. Improve masking and the spacer, check the participant's report, and recheck. If it can't be removed, a sham or sensory control condition becomes essential |
| Late response vs early response | Late components are consistent with the early local response | The waveform looks the same whatever the target. That pattern suggests a generic sensory evoked potential |

### 2d. Stimulation parameters

| Check | GO | NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| Intensity | At the planned level, e.g., a %RMT, or an estimated induced E-field at the target of roughly 70–120 V/m as used in rt-TEP-guided protocols. It also produces a valid early TEP (2b) | An early TEP appears only at intensities that cause intolerable discomfort or strong muscle artifact. Reconsider the target or orientation first, intensity last |
| Neuronavigation | Coil-to-target error < ~2–3 mm, angle or tilt error < ~3–5°. Coil flush with the scalp | Consistent drift past those limits. Re-register if the tracker or head reference has moved |

**Decision at gate 1:** go only if 2a, 2b and 2c all pass. If not, change one parameter at a time (orientation, then location, then intensity), run another 20–30 pulse test, and log every change. Set a limit on optimisation attempts in advance (e.g., 3–5 iterations, or 20–30 min) to protect the participant's comfort and alertness. If no configuration passes, record the session as failed for that target and don't collect the full dataset.

## 3. During recording: continuous monitoring (gate 2, each block)

Check these after every block (e.g., every 60–100 pulses) and in real time where possible:

| Check | Continue | Pause / fix | Stop the block or session |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coil position | Within the error limits above | Brief excursions. Reposition, mark and discard the affected trials | Can't keep the position (participant moving, coil holder failing) |
| Rolling TEP average | Early response stable vs the gate 1 template (similar amplitude, latency, topography) | Amplitude falls by more than ~30–50% or the topography changes. Check the coil, then the participant's state | Response lost and can't be restored |
| Bad trials | Fewer than ~10–20% of trials show obvious artifacts (blinks at pulse time, movement, saturation) | 20–30% bad. Remind the participant, take a short break | > 30% bad and not improving. You won't reach the target clean-trial count |
| Channels | Few or no channels failing; ≤ ~5–10% interpolable overall, none in the critical cluster under the coil | Single channels drifting or with rising impedance. Fix them between blocks | Several channels near the target fail |
| Vigilance | Eyes open and fixating, alpha not rising steadily, no slow-wave or theta bursts, responsive between blocks | Signs of drowsiness. Take a break, talk with the participant, open the room or turn on lights | Persistent drowsiness that changes TEP amplitude or shape. State is a confound, so stop the block |
| Masking / perception | Participant still reports no click and acceptable sensation | Click becomes audible (earplugs shifted, noise volume). Adjust | Can't mask it again and the N100/P200 grows |
| Comfort | Tolerable; discomfort rating stable | Rising discomfort, headache, neck strain. Take a break, adjust the headrest | Participant asks to stop |
| Trial count | On track for the planned number of usable trials (often ≥ 100–150 clean trials per condition for TEPs) | Behind schedule. Plan extra blocks | Can't reach the minimum within safety and time limits |
| Hardware | Coil temperature within limits, stimulator output stable | Coil heating close to cutoff. Pause or swap coils | Stimulator faults, or misfires that change the pulse delivered |

---

## 4. Absolute stop criteria (safety: always no-go)

- Any sign of seizure or seizure-like activity: unusual clonic movements, loss of awareness, or spreading rhythmic EEG discharges after TMS. Stop immediately and follow the lab's emergency protocol.
- Syncope or pre-syncope (pallor, sweating, dizziness), or severe headache, nausea or pain.
- The participant withdraws consent at any time.
- Pulses delivered outside the approved parameters, e.g., a wrong intensity or frequency because of a setup error.

---

## 5. Minimal checklist to log for each session

- Impedances at start and end
- Final coil location (MNI or native coordinates), orientation and intensity (%MSO, %RMT, estimated E-field)
- Masking noise settings and the participant's click-perception ratings
- Sensation and discomfort ratings for each block
- Snapshot of the gate 1 online TEP (butterfly plot and topography at the early peak)
- Number of optimisation iterations, with what changed at each
- Bad channels and trials noted online for each block, plus breaks and the reasons for them
- Navigation error statistics
- Any adverse events

**Bottom line:** continue recording only when (1) the pulse artifact recovers fast and muscle and decay artifacts are small, (2) there's a reproducible early (< 50 ms) response near the target, roughly ≥ 6–10 µV peak-to-peak, (3) sensory potentials don't dominate, (4) coil position and participant state stay stable, and (5) the participant is safe and comfortable. If any of the first four fails, optimise and retest. If safety fails, stop.
