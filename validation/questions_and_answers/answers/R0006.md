# Conclusion wording for a post-20 Hz rTMS increase in M1 N45

## Short answer

I'd advise against writing that cortical excitability increased. A 30% larger N45 after 20 Hz rTMS does not support that claim on its own, for three reasons:

1. **TEP amplitude does not measure excitability directly.** A TEP peak is the summed, averaged EEG response to many excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic potentials, both local and network-level. Brain state, artifacts, sensory input, acquisition settings and preprocessing all change it. No single TEP component, the N45 included, measures "excitability" by itself.
2. **The N45 is mostly linked to inhibition.** In pharmacological TMS-EEG work, drugs that boost GABA-A receptor activity (such as benzodiazepines) tend to make the N45 larger. So if the bigger N45 is cortical, one reasonable reading is *stronger GABA-A-mediated inhibition*. That is roughly the opposite of the sentence you were asked to write. This mapping comes from pharmacology studies, though, so it is a hypothesis and not proof. Your design does not show which mechanism caused the change.
3. **A pre/post change has other possible explanations.** These include coil repositioning, a different hotspot or orientation in the post block, changes in arousal or attention, electrode impedance drift, different numbers of trials kept, ordinary test-retest variability, and changes in sensory input. Responses before about 60 ms overlap less with sensory-evoked potentials than later peaks do, but the N45 can still carry some sensory contribution. It can also be distorted by leftover scalp-muscle activity and by ICA cleaning.

## Recommended conclusion sentences

Choose the one that matches your design and statistics.

**If you had a sham or control rTMS condition and a significant session × time interaction:**

> "Compared with sham, 20 Hz rTMS over M1 was followed by a significant increase in N45 amplitude (+30% relative to baseline), indicating a modulation of the early local TMS-evoked response; given the pharmacological association of the N45 with GABA-A–mediated inhibition, this change is compatible with altered cortical inhibitory processing rather than a straightforward increase in excitability."

**If you only compared pre with post, with no control condition:**

> "N45 amplitude over M1 was 30% larger after 20 Hz rTMS than at baseline. In the absence of a sham/control condition, this change cannot be attributed specifically to rTMS, and it should not be interpreted as a direct index of increased cortical excitability."

**Neutral, purely descriptive version (safe in any case):**

> "20 Hz rTMS was associated with a 30% increase in N45 amplitude over M1, indicating a change in the early TMS-evoked cortical response."

If you need a sentence about excitability, it would only hold with converging evidence (see below). Even then, it should read something like "...consistent with a change in cortical excitability/inhibition balance". It should not read "cortical excitability increased".

## What would strengthen the inference

- **Control condition.** Sham rTMS with matched sound and scalp sensation, or active stimulation at a control site, using the same pre/post timing. Test it with a condition × time interaction, not just the change within the active session.
- **Statistics.** Report the test, the effect size with its confidence interval, the sample size, and the exact window and channels used. Also report how individual participants varied. A 30% change in the group mean says little without these.
- **Converging measures.** Add MEP amplitude or a recruitment curve from the same session. Add paired-pulse measures (SICI, LICI) or the N100 to look at inhibition. GMFA/LMFP give a global view alongside the single-channel peak.
- **Stable stimulation.** Show that coil position, orientation and intensity were the same in both blocks, for example with neuronavigation logs and fixed % stimulator output. Report trial counts kept after rejection in each block.
- **Sensory and muscle checks.** Collect separate ratings for click loudness, scalp twitch, discomfort and pain before and after rTMS. Show that masking was the same in both blocks. Check that the N45 window has no leftover cranial-muscle activity and was not changed differently by ICA in the two blocks. Keep ICA settings and rejection criteria the same across sessions.
- **How the peak was measured.** Use the same latency window or peak-picking rule, the same ROI channels and the same reference before and after. State whether "increase" means a more negative peak or a larger absolute amplitude.

## Assumptions

- The N45 was measured as a negative deflection over electrodes near the stimulated M1. "Increased by 30%" means a larger absolute (more negative) amplitude.
- Pre and post TEPs were recorded at the same intensity and target and processed with the same pipeline.
- The question does not say whether there was a sham/control condition or what statistical test was used. The wording above therefore covers both the controlled and the uncontrolled case.
