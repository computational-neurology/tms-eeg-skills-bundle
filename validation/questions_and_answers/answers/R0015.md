# How to choose stimulation intensity for DLPFC TMS-EEG: a brief guide

**Assumptions:** single-pulse (or paired-pulse) TMS-EEG in healthy adults, a figure-of-eight coil with biphasic pulses, and MRI-guided neuronavigation. If your setup is different (e.g., rTMS, a patient group, no MRI), the logic below still applies, but the numbers may need adjusting.

---

## 1. Why this is harder for DLPFC than for M1

- **There is no direct readout.** You cannot see a MEP over DLPFC, so you cannot titrate intensity to a physiological threshold there.
- **Motor threshold does not transfer well.** Resting motor threshold (RMT) depends on M1's scalp-to-cortex distance, gyral geometry and corticospinal excitability. DLPFC differs on all three, so "110% RMT" gives a different cortical dose from person to person.
- **Higher intensity brings more confounds.** Over lateral prefrontal sites, more intensity means larger scalp-muscle artifacts (frontalis, temporalis), a louder click and stronger somatosensory input. All of these can contaminate or mimic TEPs.

So you are trying to deliver enough cortical E-field for a clear, reliable TEP without swamping the data with artifacts and sensory potentials.

---

## 2. Options for setting intensity

| Approach | How | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **% MSO (fixed)** | Same output for everyone | Simple | Ignores individual anatomy and excitability. Weakest choice. |
| **% RMT** (e.g., 90–120% RMT) | Find RMT over M1 (FDI/APB), then scale it | Widely used, easy to compare with the literature, gives a safety anchor | Assumes M1 and DLPFC are comparable, which is often untrue |
| **RMT + distance adjustment** | Correct the RMT-based intensity for the difference in scalp-to-cortex distance (roughly 2.5–3% MSO per mm, Stokes et al.) | Cheap, partly corrects for anatomy | Linear approximation that ignores gyral orientation |
| **E-field-based dosing** | Use individual head models (e.g., SimNIBS) or a navigated E-field system to match a target E-field at DLPFC (e.g., the E-field reached at M1 at RMT, or a fixed V/m) | Most principled control of cortical dose | Needs an MRI and modelling, and model uncertainty remains |
| **TEP-guided titration** (rt-TEP) | Look at the averaged early response online and adjust intensity, site and orientation until a clear early TEP appears with minimal artifact | Directly makes sure the target actually responds | Needs online visualisation software and a pre-specified criterion to avoid bias |

**Recommendation:** combine them. Use RMT as a safety and reporting anchor, use an E-field or distance correction to set the starting dose, and use online TEP and artifact checks to make the final adjustment.

---

## 3. Practical workflow

1. **Measure RMT** over M1 with neuronavigation, using the same coil, waveform and current direction you will use for DLPFC. Use a standard method such as an adaptive/ML-PEST procedure or 5/10 MEPs ≥50 µV. Record it in %MSO.
2. **Define the DLPFC target.** It can be MRI- or fMRI-based, connectivity-based, or Beam F3 if you have no MRI. Note that more lateral and inferior sites produce much larger muscle artifacts.
3. **Set a starting intensity.** Typical DLPFC TMS-EEG starting points are about 100–120% RMT, or the E-field-matched equivalent. Some studies use 90% RMT, but subthreshold-for-M1 intensities often give weak or unreliable prefrontal TEPs. If you have E-field estimates, aim for a cortical field at the target in the range shown to produce reliable TEPs (on the order of ~70–120 V/m in navigated-E-field studies).
4. **Optimise coil position and orientation first, before raising intensity.** Make small shifts and rotations to minimise early muscle and decay artifacts. Orientation often matters more than a few % MSO.
5. **Check the TEP online** with a short block of about 20–30 pulses:
   - Look for a clear early response (<50 ms) near the stimulated site, with a peak-to-peak amplitude well above the baseline noise (≥ ~10 µV is a commonly used rt-TEP criterion).
   - Check that the response is not dominated by muscle artifact (large biphasic deflections within the first ~15 ms) or by a pure N100/P200 sensory vertex response.
   - If the response is weak, increase intensity in small steps (e.g., 5% RMT), up to a pre-set ceiling.
   - If the artifacts are large, adjust site and orientation, or lower intensity.
6. **Control sensory confounds** at the final intensity. Use noise masking adjusted to the click at that intensity, a thin foam spacer to reduce bone conduction and scalp sensation, and ideally a realistic sham or sensory control condition. Ask participants about click audibility and scalp discomfort.
7. **Fix the intensity for the session.** If the study is longitudinal or pre/post, keep the same %MSO or E-field across sessions unless your protocol says to re-titrate.

---

## 4. Optional but useful: a short intensity–response pilot

In a few pilot participants, record 3–4 intensities (e.g., 80, 100, 120% RMT, or matched E-fields). Choose the lowest intensity that gives a reliable, artifact-tolerable early TEP. This avoids ceiling effects and keeps sensory input lower. Prefrontal TEPs generally grow with intensity, and are smaller than M1 TEPs at matched %MT.

---

## 5. Safety

- Screen participants and follow current safety guidelines (Rossi et al., 2021). Single-pulse at these intensities carries very low risk.
- For rTMS or paired-pulse protocols, check the intensity, frequency and train-duration limits.
- Pre-specify a maximum intensity (%MSO and %RMT) and stopping rules for discomfort.

---

## 6. What to pre-register and report

- Intensity as %MSO **and** %RMT, plus the RMT method and muscle
- Stimulator, coil, waveform, current direction, and coil orientation relative to the gyrus or midline
- How the target was defined, plus neuronavigation details
- The E-field estimate at the target (if modelled) and the scalp-to-cortex distance
- The titration criteria and whether intensity was adjusted online, including the rule used, decided **before** data collection
- Masking procedure and sensory control condition
- The number of participants for whom intensity or position had to change because of artifacts or intolerance
