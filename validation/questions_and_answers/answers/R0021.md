# How long a window to remove and interpolate around the TMS pulse

## Short answer

- **Typical default:** remove about **−2 ms to +10 ms** around the pulse, then interpolate. Many TESA-style pipelines use this window.
- **Good hardware and data:** with clean high-sampling-rate data, low impedances and good lead routing, the real pulse artifact often settles within **~4–6 ms**. A window of about −1/−2 to +5/+6 ms may then be enough.
- **Longer windows (+15 to +25 ms):** only use these if inspection shows the signal is still non-physiological that long. Even then, consider whether a longer window is really the right tool (see below).
- **No fixed number exists.** The right length depends on your amplifier, sampling rate, stimulator and pulse shape, electrode impedance, lead routing and target. Set it from your own data using a rule you decide in advance, and report it.

Assumptions: a modern TMS-compatible DC or high-range amplifier, sampling at ≥5 kHz, biphasic pulses, and an interest in TEP components from roughly 15–20 ms onward.

---

## 1. Know what the window is for

Removal plus interpolation is meant **only for the short pulse artifact**. This includes:
- the huge spike while the field is on (~0.1–0.3 ms for biphasic pulses, longer for monophasic);
- amplifier ringing, saturation or recovery afterwards;
- any gating period, if you use a sample-and-hold amplifier.

It is **not** the right tool for these longer artifacts:
| Artifact | Typical time course | Better tool |
|---|---|---|
| Electrode/skin decay (polarization) | tens to hundreds of ms | ICA, curve/exponential fitting, detrending |
| Cranial muscle artifact | ~5–40 ms, can be very large | ICA (TESA's two-round approach), SOUND + SSP-SIR, or design changes such as target/coil angle |
| Stimulator recharge artifact | appears at a fixed delay | Move the recharge delay past your epoch, or remove that short segment separately |
| Auditory/somatosensory evoked potentials | 50–200 ms | Masking, sham/control conditions; not preprocessing |

If you stretch the window to cover decay or muscle artifacts, you throw away the early TEPs (P5/N8/N15/P30). You also hide the problem instead of fixing it.

## 2. How to choose the length from your data

1. **Look at the raw, unfiltered, full-rate data.** Use single trials and the trial average from about −10 to +50 ms. Check every channel with butterfly plots, and give extra attention to electrodes under and near the coil, which are usually worst.
2. **Find the onset.** Check how much trigger-to-artifact latency jitters (usually under a sample, but not always) and whether any artifact appears before the pulse. Start the window 1–2 ms before the earliest artifact onset. −1 to −2 ms is usually enough. −5 ms is a conservative option if timing is uncertain.
3. **Find the offset with a pre-defined criterion.** For example, per channel, the first time point after which the signal stays:
   - within ±k·SD of the pre-stimulus baseline (e.g., k = 3–5) on the trial average; and/or
   - below an absolute amplitude limit (e.g., ±50–100 µV) and a slope limit (e.g., < a few µV/sample), with no steps or flat-lines from saturation.
4. **Combine across channels and trials.** Use the latest offset across channels, or a high percentile (e.g., 95th) if a few bad channels will be interpolated or rejected anyway. Add a **1–2 ms safety margin**.
5. **Use one window for all subjects in a study** where possible. Base it on pilot data or a fixed rule, to avoid analysis flexibility. If you must adjust per subject, decide the rule in advance and report each subject's window.
6. **Check against a phantom recording if you can.** Recording on a phantom (e.g., saline or a melon) with your exact setup shows how long the pure hardware artifact lasts, with no neural or muscle signal mixed in.

## 3. Weigh the trade-off against your components of interest

- If you need **early components** (N15, P30, or anything under ~20 ms), keep the window as short as your data allow. Show that results don't depend on the cutoff.
- If you only analyze **later components** (N45, P60, N100, P180) or later oscillations, a slightly longer, conservative window (e.g., to +10–15 ms) costs little.
- **Never interpret signal inside the interpolated period.** Be careful with anything within a few ms of the window edges.

## 4. Practical processing points that affect the window

- **Remove the artifact before any filtering, downsampling or re-referencing.** Filters spread the huge pulse over time: ringing, the Gibbs effect, and anti-aliasing filters smearing it backwards before the pulse. Once that happens, no window is long enough.
- **Interpolation method:**
  - Cubic interpolation fitted to short segments (~1–2 ms) on each side is common (e.g., TESA). Linear interpolation is simpler and avoids overshoot.
  - Before ICA, many pipelines **replace the window with zeros or a constant** rather than interpolating, because interpolated segments are not independent sources. They interpolate after ICA and before filtering.
  - Autoregressive or other model-based fills exist but add assumptions.
- **Run the removal step again after downsampling** if your pipeline downsamples. Check that the window still covers the artifact at the new rate.
- **Reduce the artifact at recording time** so the window can be short:
  - high sampling rate (≥5 kHz; 20 kHz or more is common);
  - amplifier input range that doesn't saturate;
  - low impedances (< 5 kΩ);
  - leads routed perpendicular to the coil handle and kept still;
  - TMS-compatible small electrodes;
  - stimulator recharge delay set beyond the epoch.

## 5. Check that the choice is robust

- **Sensitivity analysis:** rerun the pipeline with several end points, e.g., +6, +8, +10, +12 and +15 ms. Check that your outcome measures don't change in a meaningful way: peak amplitudes and latencies, global mean field power (GMFP), local mean field power (LMFP), topographies and source estimates. If they do change, the early part of your result depends on preprocessing, so be cautious about interpreting it.
- **After processing:** check that there are no edge transients, steps or ringing at the window borders in the cleaned data.

## 6. What to report

- The exact window (e.g., −2 to +10 ms relative to the trigger) and whether it varied across subjects.
- The criterion used to choose it, and the sampling rate at which it was applied.
- The interpolation method (type, and the data segments used to fit it), and where it sits in the pipeline: before or after ICA, filtering and downsampling.
- Any second removal step (e.g., for the recharge artifact).
- Any sensitivity analysis on the window length.

**Bottom line:** start from about −2 to +10 ms. Shorten the window if your data show the artifact ends earlier. Set the end point with an objective "return to baseline" criterion plus a small margin. Handle longer artifacts (decay, muscle) with dedicated methods rather than longer windows. Show that your conclusions hold across reasonable window lengths.
