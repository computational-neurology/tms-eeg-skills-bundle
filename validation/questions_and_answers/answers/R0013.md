# Online QC During a TMS-EEG Session: What to Check and Go/No-Go Criteria

## Assumptions

You didn't give the target, protocol or endpoint, so I assumed the following:

- Single-pulse TMS-EEG with a TEP endpoint. The endpoint may cover early local components (roughly 8–50 ms), late components (N100/P200), or both.
- A TMS-compatible amplifier with triggers synchronised to the stimulator, and some form of online average view. This can be a dedicated real-time TEP display or the acquisition software's online averaging.
- Auditory masking through in-ear earphones, with or without foam under the coil.
- The target could be motor or non-motor. Where the criteria change for frontal or lateral targets, or for immediate (i-TEP, <~8 ms) endpoints, this is flagged.

Treat the numbers below as practical starting values to write into your protocol before the session. They are not universal thresholds. The rule that matters most is to **define the go/no-go criteria before the main block**, not while looking at the data.

---

## 1. Principle: the two decision points

1. **Pre-acquisition gate.** Deliver setup pulses and inspect single trials. Then build a short online average of **10–30 trials**. Collect the main blocks only after every criterion below is met.
2. **In-session monitoring.** Re-apply the same checks during and between blocks. Re-run the full gate whenever intensity, coil position or orientation, target, or masking changes.

Online TEPs are an **optimisation and QC signal, not a result**. A 10–30-trial preview is noisy. Use it to judge whether the setup is good enough, and don't tune it to perfection.

---

## 2. Pre-acquisition gate: checks and criteria

### A. Hardware, synchronisation and channels

| Check | GO | ADJUST / NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger timing and event labels | Every pulse produces a correctly labelled marker with a stable latency relative to the artifact onset | Missing, duplicated or jittering markers → **no-go** until fixed. Nothing downstream can be trusted. |
| Impedance / contact, especially under and around the coil | Stable contact under the coil, no gel bridges, no loose electrodes | Unstable or bridged channels near the target → fix before proceeding |
| Lead and cable routing | Wires near the coil run roughly perpendicular to the coil handle or main axis. No loops, slack or tension near the coil. | Loops or parallel routing near the coil → re-route, then re-inspect the artifacts |
| Raw continuous EEG | Stable baseline, no saturating or drifting channels | Channels saturating between pulses or drifting → fix the contact |

### B. TMS-locked artifacts (inspect single trials first, then the average)

| Artifact | What to look for | GO | ADJUST / NO-GO |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TMS-pulse artifact** | Brief, high-amplitude deflection at the pulse | Short and stable across trials. Ends well before your analysis window. | Duration well beyond the first few ms → check amplifier recovery, recharge timing, sampling and filter settings, synchronisation |
| **Decay / back-to-baseline** | Exponential-like slope after the pulse artifact, often in specific channels or along specific cable paths | Returns to baseline before the earliest latency you intend to analyse | Decay reaching into the endpoint window, or tracking specific electrodes or wires → fix contact or routing, reduce intensity, or move the coil slightly. Re-test. **Don't count on offline cleaning to rescue it.** |
| **Recharge / discharge artifact** | Later stimulator-related transient | Absent, or outside the analysis window (for example, recharge delay set appropriately) | Inside the analysis window → change the stimulator recharge timing before collecting |
| **Early muscle-artifact peak** | Bipolar, MEP-like deflection just after the pulse artifact (later than ~4 ms). Focal and dipolar over frontal, temporal, jaw or facial channels. | Small enough that the early window stays interpretable for your endpoint | It dominates the early window → small coil rotations or shifts, then lower intensity. If still dominant and early components are the endpoint, consider the backup target. |
| **Craniofacial / scalp EMG bursts** | High-frequency bursts in single trials | Occasional, rejectable | Present on most trials → adjust the coil, as above |
| **Ocular / blink artifacts locked to the pulse** | Startle blinks after pulses | Infrequent | Frequent → check masking and comfort, which often signal an audible click or a startling sensation |

**Critical distinction:** the early EEG muscle peak and the participant's felt twitch are separate signals. At non-motor sites the EEG peak can be large while the participant reports no twitch. "The participant is comfortable" is **not** evidence that the early window is clean. Judge the window from the EEG itself.

### C. Evidence of cortical engagement (the online TEP)

| Check | GO | ADJUST / NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| **Early local TEP near the target channels** (about 8–50 ms) | A clear, reproducible early response near the coil. A practical acquisition target is around **8–10 µV peak-to-peak** in the 8–50 ms window, adapted to your montage and reference. | Early response weak or absent → adjust orientation or position first, then intensity (within the artifact and comfort ceiling) |
| **Topography of the early response** | Plausibly centred near the target, with a field broader than a single focal dipole at the scalp edge | Focal dipole over temporal, frontal or facial channels → treat it as muscle, not cortex |
| **Early vs late balance** | Early local response visible, with late components not overwhelming it | **Red flag:** weak early local response plus large N100/P200. This points to sensory contamination, a target mismatch, or insufficient effective stimulation. Raising intensity in this situation often grows only the late sensory part. |
| **Polarity check** (only if early or i-TEP components are the endpoint) | Early peaks don't simply invert with current direction the way the pulse artifact does | An "early component" that reverses exactly with pulse polarity → treat it as residual pulse artifact |

Use RMT, or a fixed %RMT, only as a safety and starting reference. For non-motor targets, set the final intensity from the online TEP, the artifact ceiling and tolerability, and log that decision chain.

### D. Sensory controls

| Check | GO | ADJUST / NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| **Masking titration** | Titrated under **real TMS** at the final intensity and coil position, never under sham or a tilted coil. Real TMS needs about 6 dB more masking than sham. Volume is raised until the participant detects no click on **10 consecutive pulses** at jittered intervals. | Titrated on sham, or before the intensity was fixed → re-titrate |
| **Masking verification in the EEG** | No clear N100/P200 auditory complex in the online average (with masking off it typically sits around 100 ms and 200 ms, frontocentral) | Clear AEP-like N100/P200 → raise the masking, use click-based rather than plain white noise, or add or thicken foam. Self-report alone is not sufficient. |
| **Hearing safety** | Masking level × planned session duration within your exposure limits | Level needed to mask exceeds a safe exposure for the planned duration → switch to a customised (click-derived) masker, shorten the session, or accept and document partial masking |
| **Somatosensory / scalp sensation** | Foam or spacer in place if planned. Sham or control condition set up and checked. | Strong scalp sensation that the control doesn't match → revisit the control |
| **Sensory ratings** | Separate 0–10 ratings of **click loudness, scalp twitch, discomfort and pain**, collected after setup and then per block | Not collected → collect them. This is mandatory if you will compare sites, conditions or groups. |

### E. Participant

| Check | GO | NO-GO |
|---|---|---|
| Tolerability of stimulation and masking | Participant is willing and comfortable at the final settings | Intolerable discomfort or pain → lower the intensity or move the coil. If no tolerable setting gives a usable response, stop or switch to the backup target. |
| Safety | No adverse signs | Any safety concern → **stop** according to your TMS safety procedure, whatever the data quality |
| State | Awake, eyes as specified, relaxed jaw, neck and face | Drowsiness or clenching → re-instruct, take a break |

### F. Trial budget

| Check | GO | ADJUST |
|---|---|---|
| Online rejection rate in the setup block | Expected usable trials after rejection meet your pre-registered minimum per condition | Rejection rate too high to reach the minimum in the available time → fix the cause (usually muscle, contact or blinks) before starting. Don't just add more trials to make up the loss. |

*Example rule:* if you need ≥100 clean trials per condition and plan 150, the setup-block rejection rate must stay below about 30%.

## 3. Consolidated go/no-go rule (pre-register this)

**GO to the main block only if all of the following hold:**

1. Triggers are correct and synchronised, and channels under and around the coil are stable.
2. The pulse artifact is short and stable, and **decay and recharge end before the earliest analysed latency**.
3. **The early muscle-artifact peak and EMG don't dominate the endpoint window.**
4. **A reproducible early local TEP is visible near the target** (practical target around 8–10 µV peak-to-peak, 8–50 ms) wherever early components are a primary endpoint or QC variable. Its topography is not a focal muscle-like dipole.
5. **Masking has been titrated under real TMS at the final settings and verified by the absence of a clear N100/P200 complex in the EEG.** Somatosensory and sham controls are in place.
6. The participant tolerates stimulation and masking, there is no safety concern, and sensory ratings have been collected.
7. The projected number of usable trials meets the pre-specified minimum.

**PAUSE AND ADJUST** if any of criteria 2–5 fails. Work through the fixes in this order:

1. Fix wires or contact.
2. Make small coil rotations or position shifts.
3. Adjust intensity within the safety and artifact ceiling.
4. Re-tune masking or foam.
5. Move to the backup target.

Re-run the gate after every change.

**NO-GO (stop or abandon this target or session)** in any of these cases:

- There is any safety or tolerability problem.
- Synchronisation can't be made reliable.
- Pulse, decay or muscle artifact still dominates the endpoint window after the adjustments above.
- No tolerable setting gives an interpretable early response when early components are the endpoint.
- Masking can't suppress the AEP within safe exposure limits while your interpretation depends on late components. In that case, either stop or explicitly re-scope the endpoint and document it.

## 4. During the main recording

Recheck continuously or between blocks:

- **Coil drift:** target position and angle in neuronavigation. Re-centre if the coil moves beyond your pre-set tolerance.
- **Artifacts:** a sudden change in pulse, decay or muscle artifact → **pause**. Usual causes are a shifted coil, a loosened electrode, or a moved cable.
- **Channels:** impedance or contact drift, and newly bad channels near the coil.
- **Triggers:** timing and labels stay correct.
- **Running rejection rate** against the trial budget.
- **Running online average:** the early local response remains present, and N100/P200 doesn't grow as the session goes on. Growth suggests masking is failing or the participant is habituating to the noise.
- **Masking and comfort:** re-titrate and re-verify the AEP check after **any** change in intensity, coil or target.
- **Sensory ratings** after every block.
- **Condition balance:** active, sham and control blocks keep a comparable artifact profile. If one condition has systematically more muscle or decay, the comparison is confounded.
- **Participant state:** alertness, fatigue, discomfort.

Log every adjustment with a timestamp, and save QC screenshots: setup average, topography, artifact profile, coil position, intensity and masking level.

## 5. Stricter criteria for specific situations

- **Frontal, lateral frontal or temporal targets (for example, DLPFC):** expect muscle artifact, loud perceived clicks and the poorest masking efficacy. Inspect the 5–15 ms window online in particular. Prefrontal sites often produce the loudest perceived click even at lower intensities, so tune masking per site, not once per participant. If the endpoint depends on early topography or source estimates, an unresolved early muscle peak is a no-go for that target.
- **Immediate TEPs (i-TEPs, first few ms):**
  - Document pulse-artifact duration and back-to-baseline timing separately.
  - Use high sampling rates and record the lead and cable configuration.
  - Verify that early peaks don't simply flip with current-direction polarity.
  - Don't accept an early peak as neural without control evidence.
  - Decay recovery in high-quality setups can be around 2–3 ms, but this varies strongly with the setup, and decay lengthens with intensity.
- **Between-site or between-group comparisons:** the sensory ratings and a matched masking procedure are a go criterion, not optional extras. N100/P200 differences follow perceived click loudness and scalp twitch.

---

## 6. Things not to conclude online

- A clean-looking average is not proof of cortical origin. Time-locked muscle and decay become *more* convincing after averaging.
- A comfortable participant with no twitch does not mean the early window is free of muscle artifact.
- "Participant can't hear the click" does not mean there are no auditory or sensory contributions. Verify in the EEG, and even then, perceptual masking doesn't guarantee that neural confounds are absent.
- Good neuronavigation does not remove the need for online artifact inspection.
- Offline preprocessing (ICA, decay fitting, interpolation) cannot reliably recover data dominated by pulse, decay, muscle or sensory artifacts. If it looks bad online, fix it online.

## 7. Compact checklist (printable)

- [ ] Triggers labelled and synchronised
- [ ] Contact stable under the coil, no bridges, wires perpendicular, no loops
- [ ] Pulse artifact short; decay and recharge end before the analysis window
- [ ] Early muscle peak (later than ~4 ms, focal frontal/temporal/jaw) not dominating
- [ ] Early local TEP present near the target (about 8–10 µV peak-to-peak, 8–50 ms) with a plausible topography
- [ ] No "weak early + large late" pattern
- [ ] Masking titrated on real TMS at final settings (no click on 10 consecutive pulses)
- [ ] No clear N100/P200 in the online average; hearing-exposure budget OK
- [ ] Foam and sham/control in place; ratings collected (sound, twitch, discomfort, pain)
- [ ] Participant tolerates the settings; no safety issues
- [ ] Projected usable trials ≥ pre-registered minimum
- [ ] Setup screenshots and parameters saved → **GO**
- [ ] During blocks: drift, artifacts, impedance, rejection rate, AEP growth, ratings, and condition balance all monitored and logged
