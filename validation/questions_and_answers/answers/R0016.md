# Stimulation intensity for single-pulse TMS-EEG over left DLPFC

## Short answer

You can use 110% RMT as a starting point, but you shouldn't use it as the only rule for setting intensity. In your rTMS protocol, %RMT is the accepted dosing convention and mainly a safety calibration. In TMS-EEG over a non-motor target like the DLPFC, the outcome is the EEG response. The motor threshold tells you little about that:

- **RMT measures corticospinal excitability at M1, not prefrontal engagement.** Scalp-to-cortex distance, gyral folding and local excitability all differ between M1 and DLPFC, and they differ between people. So 110% RMT gives different cortical doses at the DLPFC from one participant to the next. Even the usual coil-to-cortex distance correction doesn't make the modelled cortical E-field equal across sites. "110% RMT" therefore doesn't mean equal stimulation across participants.
- **The DLPFC is the most artifact-prone of the commonly used non-motor sites.** In a large three-site comparison, prefrontal stimulation produced:
  - residual muscle artifact that survived ICA in about 62% of individuals
  - the loudest perceived click, even though it used the lowest intensity
  - the highest twitch and discomfort ratings
  - the worst white-noise masking
  - the largest N100/P200, which partly tracks sensation rather than cortical activity

  A fixed 110% RMT may push some participants into an early window dominated by cranial-muscle artifact. In others it may produce only a weak early local TEP.
- **The felt twitch and the EEG muscle artifact are different things.** At the DLPFC the participant often feels no twitch. The EEG can still show a large early bipolar, MEP-like deflection (after about 4 ms, over frontal, temporal and jaw channels). In a short online average this deflection is often the only time-locked activity you can see. "Comfortable, no twitch" does not mean the early window is clean.

**Recommendation:** use 110% RMT as the starting and safety reference. Then adjust intensity for each participant from a short online TEP check, within a limit you set in advance. The upper limit is the early muscle-artifact peak (and discomfort). The lower requirement is a visible early local response. Lock the final value before the main recording.

## Suggested procedure

1. **RMT.** Measure RMT over left M1 with the same coil and pulse waveform you will use for the DLPFC. Use a standard method (for example, the lowest intensity that gives MEPs of at least 50 µV in 5 of 10 trials in relaxed FDI). Record it as %MSO.
2. **Starting intensity.** Start at 110% RMT over the neuronavigated DLPFC target. Set coil orientation from the individual anatomy or an E-field model, not the 45° M1 convention.
3. **Online check.** Deliver about 20 to 30 setup pulses and inspect single trials and the online average. Look at:
   - pulse, recharge and decay artifacts
   - the early muscle-artifact peak
   - the early local TEP (roughly 8 to 10 µV peak-to-peak between 8 and 50 ms near the coil is a practical working target, not a universal threshold)
   - the late N100/P200
4. **Adjust in this order:**
   - first, small changes to coil rotation and position, to shrink the muscle peak without changing intensity
   - then intensity, within a range you pre-specify (for example, 90 to 120% RMT, and never above your safety maximum)
   - If raising intensity enlarges N100/P200 but not the early local response, suspect sensory contamination and stop increasing.
5. **Masking.** Retune auditory masking (noise level and click perception) after every intensity change. Keep the foam spacer in place.
6. **Lock and log.** Lock the final intensity before the main block. Record %MSO, %RMT and the reason for any change from 110% RMT. Collect sensory ratings (loudness, scalp sensation, discomfort) from every participant.

If you need one fixed intensity for all participants (for example, to match the rTMS arm), keep 110% RMT. In that case, make the online check a pre-registered go/no-go rule and treat unequal cortical dose as a stated limitation. Don't present the intensity as equal stimulation across participants.

## Methods sentence (recommended version)

> Single-pulse TMS was delivered to the left DLPFC at an initial intensity of 110% of each participant's resting motor threshold (RMT), determined over the left primary motor cortex using the same coil and pulse waveform as the lowest intensity eliciting motor evoked potentials of ≥50 µV in at least 5 of 10 consecutive trials in the relaxed first dorsal interosseous muscle. Because RMT indexes corticospinal rather than prefrontal excitability, it served as a starting and safety reference only: before the main recording, intensity and coil orientation/position were adjusted within a pre-specified range ([e.g., 90–120% RMT], not exceeding [safety maximum] %MSO) on the basis of online averages of [20–30] trials, with the aim of obtaining an early local TEP near the stimulation site while keeping the early cranial-muscle artifact small enough for the [e.g., 15–50 ms] analysis window to remain interpretable; auditory masking was re-adjusted after any intensity change, and the final intensity was then fixed for the session. Final intensities are reported as both %RMT and %MSO (mean ± SD and range), together with RMT (%MSO, mean ± SD).

## Methods sentence (if you keep a fixed 110% RMT)

> Single-pulse TMS was delivered to the left DLPFC at 110% of each participant's resting motor threshold (RMT; determined over left M1 with the same coil and waveform as the lowest intensity eliciting MEPs ≥50 µV in at least 5 of 10 trials in the relaxed first dorsal interosseous), corresponding to [mean ± SD, range] %MSO. Because RMT reflects corticospinal excitability and does not equate cortical dose at prefrontal sites, a pre-registered online check of [20–30] trials was used to confirm, before the main recording, that the early TEP window was not dominated by pulse, decay or cranial-muscle artifact; coil orientation and position (but not intensity) were adjusted if it was.

## Reporting notes

- **Report intensity as numbers, with spread.** Give RMT (%MSO), stimulator output (%MSO) and intensity relative to threshold (%RMT), each as mean or median plus SD or range. Report the inter-trial interval and the total number of pulses delivered in the same way. Published TMS papers often report these items inconsistently or only by implication.
- **Reporting checklist.** If you use TMS-RAT, write against v1.0 (the prospective version), not v1.1. TMS-RAT has not been validated for TMS-EEG. Report the EEG acquisition and TEP-measure details it doesn't cover:
  - amplifier and sampling rate
  - trigger synchronisation
  - impedance
  - lead routing
  - pulse interpolation window
  - preprocessing pipeline
  - TEP analysis windows
  - trials delivered versus trials retained
- **Sensory controls.** Plan a sensory control, such as realistic sham or a sensory-matched control, and collect sensory ratings. Without them, the DLPFC N100/P200 can't be read as a direct cortical response.

**Assumptions:** single-pulse TMS-EEG; a figure-of-eight coil with neuronavigation; healthy adult participants; and the 110% RMT rule carried over from your rTMS protocol without a specific reason to match that dose. If the TMS-EEG is a pre/post read-out of the rTMS session, keep the TEP-probe intensity fixed across both time points within each participant, whichever rule you use.
