# Choosing Stimulation Intensity for DLPFC TMS-EEG: A Brief Guide

## Assumptions
- Single-pulse (or paired-pulse) TMS-EEG over DLPFC in healthy adults, with a figure-of-eight coil, neuronavigation, and an EEG system that lets you view averaged TEPs online.
- Your primary endpoint involves the TEP (early local components and/or GMFA/LMFP). If you only care about late components, the logic still applies, but sensory contamination becomes the main concern.

## The core idea
At DLPFC, intensity is **not a single number you take from the motor cortex**. Treat it as a decision chain:

**starting reference (RMT and/or E-field) → online TEP response → artifact and discomfort ceiling → final locked value**

RMT measures corticospinal excitability. It tells you little about how strongly a prefrontal patch is driven or what the EEG will show. A fixed "% RMT" therefore does **not** give equivalent cortical activation at DLPFC. Use it as a safety and starting anchor, not as the dosing rule.

## Step-by-step

### 1. Get a starting reference
- Measure RMT at M1 (with the same coil and current direction you will use) as a safety and calibration anchor. Common practice is to start somewhere around 90–120% RMT. Treat that range as a starting point, not a target.
- If you have individual MRI, model the E-field (for example SimNIBS, or the on-board estimate on an E-field-navigated system). Use it to set an initial intensity in terms of estimated cortical field at the target, and to choose coil orientation.
- Note that coil-to-cortex distance correction of %RMT is standard practice but **does not equalise cortical dose**. If dose matters (for example comparisons with other sites or groups), model the E-field per participant.

### 2. Fix orientation and position before raising intensity
- Do not default to the M1 45° convention. At DLPFC the best orientation depends on the individual's gyral folding. Aim for induced current roughly perpendicular to the target gyral crown or sulcal wall.
- Small rotations or position shifts of a few mm often improve the early response and reduce muscle artifact **more than turning intensity up does**. Optimise these first.

### 3. Titrate with online TEPs
- Deliver short blocks (about 10–30 pulses) and look at single trials and the online average near the coil.
- A practical acquisition target is an early local response of roughly **8–10 µV peak-to-peak in the ~8–50 ms window** at channels near the target. This is a rule of thumb, not a universal threshold.
- Increase intensity stepwise (for example a few % MSO at a time) only while the early local response improves **and** artifacts stay acceptable.
- Do not over-fit to a noisy 10–30 trial preview. Look for a consistent improvement, not one lucky average.

### 4. Respect the artifact ceiling (critical at DLPFC)
DLPFC is the hardest common non-motor site. In a large three-site comparison, about two in three participants still had residual muscle artifact after ICA at prefrontal sites. The prefrontal site also gave the loudest perceived click, the highest twitch and discomfort ratings, and the poorest white-noise masking.
- **The early muscle-artifact peak sets your upper limit.** This is a focal, bipolar, MEP-like deflection that appears after the pulse artifact at latencies later than ~4 ms, over frontal, temporal or facial channels. It grows with intensity and is often the only thing visible in the online average. Do not mistake it for an early TEP.
- **"No felt twitch" does not mean "clean".** The participant's comfort and this EEG peak are separate signals. Inspect the 5–15 ms window directly.
- If raising intensity enlarges N100/P200 but not the early local response, suspect auditory or somatosensory input rather than better cortical engagement. Stop increasing.
- If muscle artifact dominates the early window at any useful intensity, rotate or reposition the coil, or move to a nearby lower-muscle subregion (more medial or posterior), before accepting a contaminated setup. Do not plan to "clean it out later."

### 5. Re-tune masking at the final intensity
- Click loudness and bone conduction change with intensity and coil contact. Titrate masking noise (ideally noise built from your own coil's click) **under real stimulation at the final intensity and position**, not during sham.
- Check that N100/P200 shrinks in the online average. Self-report alone is a weak check.
- Use a thin foam spacer if needed to reduce bone-conducted sound and scalp sensation.

### 6. Lock it and document it
- Lock intensity before the main blocks and keep it fixed across conditions and sessions unless your design calls for re-titration. If it does, pre-specify the rule.
- Stay within established safety guidelines. Screen participants and keep the final value below any pre-set maximum (for example a cap in %RMT or %MSO stated in your protocol).
- Collect sensory ratings (click loudness, scalp sensation, discomfort) at the final intensity, especially if you will compare against another site, group or sham.

## Go / no-go before the main recording
Proceed if all of the following hold:
- There is a reproducible early local response near the target.
- The early muscle-artifact peak does not dominate your analysis window.
- There are no pulse or decay artifacts that would compromise the endpoint.
- Masking is verified at the final intensity.

Otherwise, adjust orientation, position, intensity, masking or electrodes, in that order of preference.

## What to report
- RMT (with coil, current direction and method) and/or modelled E-field at the target.
- Final intensity in %MSO, %RMT and, if available, V/m, plus the **rule** used to reach it (RMT-based, E-field-based, online TEP-based, or capped by artifact or safety).
- Online QC criteria, coil orientation and position, masking procedure, and sensory ratings.
- If sites are compared: whether cortical dose was modelled or assumed, stated plainly as a limitation if assumed.

## One-line summary
Start from RMT and/or E-field for safety, optimise coil angle and position first, and raise intensity only until a clear early local TEP appears (~8–10 µV). Stop when the early muscle peak or sensory-driven N100/P200 starts to dominate, then re-tune masking and lock the value.
