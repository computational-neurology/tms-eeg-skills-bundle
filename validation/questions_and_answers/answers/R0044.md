# Choosing Stimulation Intensity for DLPFC TMS-EEG: A Brief Guide

**Assumptions:** single-pulse TEP study in healthy adults, figure-of-eight coil, neuronavigation available, and an endpoint that includes early (<50 ms) local TEPs and possibly later components. If you only care about late components or connectivity, the logic still applies but the early-window criteria matter less.

---

## Core principle

For DLPFC, **no single rule is enough on its own**, and that includes a fixed percentage of resting motor threshold (RMT). RMT measures corticospinal excitability in M1. It is only loosely related to how strongly DLPFC is activated or to how clean the EEG will be. Think of intensity as a **decision chain**:

> starting estimate (RMT and/or E-field) → online TEP response → artifact/discomfort ceiling → final locked value

---

## Step-by-step

### 1. Get a starting estimate
- **Measure RMT** in M1 with the same coil and current direction you will use at DLPFC. Use it as a **safety and starting reference**, not as the final dose.
- If you have individual MRI, **model the E-field** (e.g., SimNIBS or on-board navigated E-field). Use it to set coil orientation (aim for current roughly perpendicular to the target sulcal wall or gyral crown, not an automatic 45°) and to estimate the cortical dose at the target. Correcting %RMT for coil-to-cortex distance does **not** equalise cortical dose between sites or people.
- Set orientation and position **before** fine-tuning intensity. A small rotation or a shift of a few millimetres often improves the early TEP more than adding intensity does.

### 2. Tune intensity online using TEPs
- Use real-time TEP monitoring with **10–30 trial averages**, and check single trials before trusting the average.
- Practical target: a visible early local response near the coil. A working benchmark is about **8–10 µV peak-to-peak in the 8–50 ms window** at channels near the coil. This is a guide for acquisition, not a universal threshold.
- Raise intensity in small steps only while the early local response keeps improving.

### 3. Respect the artifact ceiling (critical at DLPFC)
- DLPFC carries the heaviest muscle and sensory burden of the common non-motor sites. Roughly two-thirds of participants still have residual muscle artifact in the early window after ICA.
- Your **ceiling is the early muscle-artifact peak**. This is a bipolar, MEP-like deflection that starts more than about 4 ms after the pulse and is focal over frontal, temporal, jaw or facial channels. It grows with intensity, and in the online average it is often the only time-locked activity you can see.
- **Do not use "no felt twitch" as permission to go higher.** The participant's twitch is a comfort signal. The EEG muscle peak is a separate contaminant and can be large when nothing is felt.
- If muscle dominates the first 10–20 ms, first try changing coil angle or position, then lowering intensity. If that fails, consider a slightly more medial or posterior DLPFC spot with less muscle.

### 4. Watch for sensory-driven "gains"
- If raising intensity mainly increases **N100/P200** while the early local response stays flat, suspect auditory or somatosensory contamination rather than stronger cortical activation.
- **Retune auditory masking (noise) every time you change intensity**, and also after changes to coil, foam or position. Collect sensory ratings (click loudness, scalp sensation, discomfort). At prefrontal sites, click loudness does not scale simply with intensity.

### 5. Check safety and tolerability
- Stay within current safety guidelines for single-pulse TMS and your ethics-approved maximum. Record discomfort or pain ratings and stop raising intensity if tolerability becomes limiting.

### 6. Lock and keep it
- Once the early response can be interpreted and artifacts are manageable, **lock the intensity** for the session. Do not keep adjusting it during data collection.
- Don't over-fit to a noisy 10–30 trial preview. Take the lowest intensity that reliably gives an interpretable response.

---

## Go / no-go before the main blocks
- **Go:** stable early local response, muscle peak small enough that your planned early window can be interpreted, no major pulse or decay artifacts, masking adequate, participant comfortable.
- **No-go / adjust:** muscle peak dominates the early window, response only appears in N100/P200, or discomfort limits intensity. Change orientation, position or intensity first. Use a backup target if needed.

---

## Special cases
- **Comparing DLPFC with other sites or groups:** %RMT (even distance-corrected) does not give matched cortical dose. Either model the E-field per person and normalise by it, or state that dose is unmatched as a limitation. Always collect sensory ratings.
- **Longitudinal or intervention designs:** keep the intensity rule fixed across sessions (e.g., same E-field target or same %RMT plus the same online criteria), and use neuronavigation to reproduce coil pose.

---

## What to report
- The intensity basis (RMT, E-field, online TEP threshold, or a combination) and the final value as %MSO, %RMT and, if modelled, the estimated E-field at the target.
- RMT method, coil model, orientation and current direction.
- Online QC criteria used to accept the intensity, masking settings, and sensory/discomfort ratings.
- Any per-participant deviations, for example intensity lowered because of muscle artifact.
