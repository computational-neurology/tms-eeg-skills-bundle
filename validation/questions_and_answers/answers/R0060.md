# Fixing coil pose over an MRI-defined DLPFC target: is it the right approach?

## Short answer

**Only partly.** Once you have chosen a coil pose, keep it stable with neuronavigation for the whole main acquisition. That part is good practice. The problem is treating the anatomical pose as final before you have seen any EEG. Two parts of the plan need to change.

1. **The 45° handle angle is not justified for DLPFC.** 45° (posterolateral-to-anteromedial) is a convention for primary motor cortex. Simulation work (Gomez-Tames et al. 2018: 18 head models, 2401 scalp positions, 12 coil angles) shows that one optimal angle that works across subjects exists only for M1 and S1. In frontal cortex, gyral folding varies so much between people that the best orientation is subject-specific. Your coordinate fixes 3 of the 6 coil degrees of freedom (x, y, z). It does not tell you the yaw.
2. **An anatomically optimal target does not guarantee a usable TMS-EEG signal.** For TMS-EEG, the pose has to work on three counts at once:
   - it engages the target;
   - it produces an early response you can interpret;
   - it keeps artifacts manageable.

   DLPFC is the worst commonly studied site on the artifact count. In a study of 135 participants across three sites (Biabani et al. 2024), about **62% of individuals still had muscle artifact at prefrontal sites after ICA**, compared with 34.5% at premotor and 0% at parietal. Prefrontal stimulation also gave the loudest perceived click, the highest twitch and discomfort ratings, the weakest masking and the largest sensory-driven N100/P200. If you skip a setup check, there is a real risk of collecting a full session whose early window is dominated by craniofacial muscle activity. Preprocessing cannot reliably recover that.

Good neuronavigation improves precision and reporting. It does not remove the need for online artifact and TEP checks.

## Two further points about the target itself

- **(-38, 44, 26) is a group-level MNI coordinate.** It is widely used as a group-average DLPFC site defined by connectivity. Mapped into one person's MRI, it is "optimal" only in the atlas sense. It is not individually optimized. If your hypothesis is about connectivity, consider whether an individualized target is needed. Any individual target should be constrained to an a priori region mask, because single-subject frontal maps are noisy.
- **The best scalp position is usually not the scalp point closest to the target.** Modelled shifts average about 5.5 mm and are larger for deeper targets. Small position changes can therefore stay faithful to the cortical target. Model the E-field at the cortical target, not at the scalp projection.

## Recommended procedure

### Before the session: set the starting orientation from the E-field, not from 45°

1. Build an individual head model (T1, plus T2 if available) and place the coil over the subject-space target.
2. Sweep coil yaw in the E-field model, for example 24 orientations in 15° steps (Balderston et al. 2020 workflow). Choose the yaw that maximizes induced current in a target region of interest, such as a sphere of about 5–10 mm around the coordinate.
   - Sampling the whole region is more stable than sampling a single voxel.
   - In general, aim for induced current roughly perpendicular to the local sulcal wall or gyral crown.
3. Set roll and pitch so the coil sits tangential to the scalp. Load the target, the chosen yaw vector and the orientation map into the navigation system.
4. Write down the allowed search window **in advance**. As an assumption, if you need no explicit target-dose constraint:
   - position changes of no more than about 5 mm from the modelled optimum;
   - yaw changes of no more than about ±30°;
   - modelled E-field in the target region kept at or above about 80% of its maximum.

   This keeps any later adjustment tied to the target rather than drifting toward whatever "looks best".
5. Work out the intensity from the E-field model and RMT, but treat it only as a starting point. RMT and %RMT are not enough to set dose at a non-motor site.

### At session start: a short online optimization phase (about 10–20 minutes)

1. Check impedances, cap fit and cable routing under the coil. Route leads roughly perpendicular to the coil handle with no slack near the coil. Set auditory masking (noise and foam) at the planned intensity.
2. Stimulate at the modelled pose. Look at single trials and a **10–30 trial online average**.
3. Look for these things:
   - **The early muscle-artifact peak.** This is a bipolar, MEP-like deflection after the pulse artifact, from about 4 ms onward. It is focal over frontal, temporal, jaw or facial channels. At DLPFC it is often the only time-locked signal visible online, and it can be large even when the participant feels no twitch. "Comfortable, no twitch" does **not** mean the early window is clean.
   - **The early local TEP near the target, about 8–50 ms.** As a practical acquisition target, aim for roughly 8–10 µV peak-to-peak. This is not a universal threshold.
   - **Pulse, recharge and decay artifacts**, and noisy channels.
   - **Late N100/P200.** If it is large while the early local response is weak, suspect sensory contamination or ineffective stimulation.
4. If the early muscle peak dominates, try small yaw changes (steps of about 10–15°) and small position shifts within the pre-registered window. Re-check with a short average after each change. Lower the intensity only if this does not help. The muscle peak sets the upper limit on intensity.
5. **Stopping rule.** Lock the pose and intensity at the first setting within the window where both of these hold:
   - the early local response is visible;
   - the muscle peak no longer dominates the planned early analysis window.

   Do not keep chasing the largest-looking average: 10–30 trial previews are noisy.
6. Re-tune masking after any change in orientation, position or intensity.
7. **Go/no-go.** If no pose within the window gives an interpretable early window, decide in advance between two options:
   - accept the session for late or global endpoints only;
   - move to a pre-specified backup DLPFC site that is more medial or posterior and less affected by muscle artifact.

   Do not run the full session hoping ICA will fix it.

### During the main acquisition: now keep the pose fixed

- Hold the **locked** pose with continuous navigation tracking. Set tolerances in advance, for example no more than about 2–3 mm and about 5° deviation (an assumption; choose your own). Reposition whenever the coil drifts outside them. Log trial-wise deviations, or blocks with excessive deviation, for exclusion.
- Do not re-optimize between blocks. A pose that changes partway through confounds your outcome. Re-check the online average and artifacts once per block. Pause only if something degrades, such as a channel failing, cap slippage, or the muscle peak growing.
- Collect four separate 0–10 ratings after every block: click loudness, scalp twitch, discomfort and pain. At DLPFC, N100/P200 follows these ratings, and you need them to interpret late components or compare conditions or groups. Plan a sensory control, such as realistic sham or site-matched scalp electrical stimulation with the click preserved.

## What to record and report

- How the target was defined: group MNI coordinate, transformation to subject space, and resulting subject-space coordinates.
- Coil model, final position, handle angle, current direction, and roll and pitch relative to the scalp.
- The E-field-derived yaw and the final yaw, with the reason for any difference.
- The pre-registered search window, the stopping rule, and how far the final pose was from the modelled optimum.
- The intensity decision chain: model or RMT starting value, online response, artifact or discomfort ceiling, final locked value. Include modelled E-field at the target.
- Navigation registration error, trial-wise coil deviations and the tolerance rule.
- Masking settings, foam, sensory ratings, and the online QC and go/no-go criteria, including setup screenshots or notes.

## Summary

| Element of your plan | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Neuronavigated, MRI-mapped DLPFC target | Good. Remember it is a group coordinate mapped to the individual, not individually optimized. |
| Handle at 45° | Not justified for DLPFC. Choose yaw per subject from an E-field sweep. |
| No adjustment before acquisition | Not recommended. Run a short online TEP and artifact check, adjust within a pre-specified window, then lock. |
| Pose held fixed during acquisition | Correct, **after** locking. Enforce it with navigation tolerances and logging. |

**Assumptions:** single-pulse TMS-EEG with figure-of-eight coil, an endpoint that includes early TEP components, and the lab able to build individual head models. If your endpoint is only late or global, the early-window optimization matters less. Sensory controls and ratings then matter even more.
