# Interpreting a 30% N45 increase after 20 Hz rTMS over M1

## Short answer

I don't recommend writing that cortical excitability increased. The data don't support that sentence, and it probably points the wrong way. Below is wording you can defend, followed by the reasons.

## Suggested conclusion sentence

**Option A (recommended, cautious):**
> "Following 20 Hz rTMS over left M1, the amplitude of the TMS-evoked N45 increased by approximately 30% relative to baseline, indicating that high-frequency rTMS modulated early local cortical reactivity to single-pulse TMS."

**Option B (mechanistic, if you want to name a mechanism):**
> "Following 20 Hz rTMS, N45 amplitude increased by ~30% relative to baseline. Given pharmacological evidence linking the N45 to GABA-A receptor-mediated neurotransmission, this change may reflect enhanced GABA-A-mediated inhibition rather than a net increase in corticospinal excitability. This interpretation requires confirmation with sham-controlled and multimodal (e.g., MEP, paired-pulse) measures."

If you also have MEP data showing a facilitation, you could add:
> "...alongside a concurrent increase in MEP amplitude, suggesting that the after-effects of 20 Hz rTMS involve both facilitatory corticospinal and inhibitory intracortical changes."

## Why "cortical excitability increased" is not justified

1. **The N45 is mainly read as an inhibitory marker.** Pharmaco-TMS-EEG studies show that GABA-A-positive allosteric modulators such as alprazolam, diazepam and zolpidem *increase* N45 amplitude (Premoli et al., 2014, J Neurosci). A larger N45 is therefore more naturally read as stronger GABA-A-mediated inhibition, the opposite of "increased excitability." Other work has linked the N45 to NMDA and glutamatergic modulation and to α5-GABA-A subunits. So the mechanism is not settled, but it gives no clear support for "more excitable."

2. **"Cortical excitability" is a vague construct.** In TMS research it usually means MEP amplitude, motor threshold or input-output curves. A single TEP peak cannot stand in for these. TEP amplitude reflects the size and synchrony of the evoked response plus a mix of excitatory and inhibitory processes. It is not a scalar "excitability" index.

3. **20 Hz rTMS is not reliably facilitatory.** The rule "high frequency means excitatory" comes from group-level MEP findings. Individual responses vary a lot, and many participants show no change or the opposite effect. You cannot assume the direction of the effect from the protocol.

4. **A pre-post change alone does not establish a causal rTMS effect.** Without a sham or control condition, a 30% change could reflect:
   - time-on-task, fatigue or changes in arousal and vigilance (eyes open/closed, drowsiness), which strongly affect TEPs;
   - coil repositioning or orientation drift, or changes in scalp-to-coil distance (neuronavigation logs should be checked);
   - test-retest variability, since early TEP peaks often show notable session-to-session and within-session variability;
   - regression to the mean if baseline values were unusually low.

5. **Possible confounds in the early window.** The N45 latency range can be contaminated by residual TMS-evoked muscle or decay artifacts. It can also be affected by any change in somatosensory or auditory co-stimulation, for example if the masking noise, foam padding or perceived intensity changed after rTMS. Confirm the following:
   - preprocessing was identical for pre and post (same ICA/SSP-SIR criteria, same number of removed components);
   - trial counts were comparable;
   - the effect has a plausible scalp topography and is not driven by a few electrodes near the coil.

6. **Statistics and reporting.** "Increased by 30%" needs inferential support:
   - a paired test or mixed model on pre/post, preferably as a Condition (active vs sham) × Time interaction;
   - spatiotemporal correction, e.g., cluster-based permutation, instead of a single electrode or peak chosen after seeing the data;
   - effect size with a confidence interval;
   - clarity on whether the 30% is a group mean, a median, or one participant's value.

## Assumptions made

- "Increased" means a larger absolute (more negative) N45 deflection, measured at electrodes near the stimulated M1 (e.g., C3/FC3/CP3 for left M1).
- Single-pulse TEPs were recorded at the same intensity before and after rTMS, with intensity not re-adjusted after rTMS.
- No sham condition and no concurrent MEP or paired-pulse data were mentioned, so the wording above assumes they are not available.

## What would strengthen the claim

- A sham-rTMS (or control-site) session, with the effect tested as a Condition × Time interaction.
- MEPs or input-output curves alongside the TEPs, which test corticospinal excitability directly.
- Paired-pulse TMS-EEG (SICI/LICI-TEP) to test the inhibitory interpretation directly.
- Sensory control: realistic sham, masking-noise checks and perception ratings before and after rTMS.
- Reporting of neuronavigation stability, trial counts, and the rejected components or trials per session.
